GLOBAL DESIGN DIRECTION  
\==================================================

Overall feel  
Airy, premium, editorial, analytical, modern legal-business publication.

Visual rhythm  
Alternate between:  
• hero / image-led section  
• trust or proof strip  
• short editorial text block  
• card grid  
• featured research block  
• split text \+ image section  
• latest-content grid  
• restrained CTA

Do not stack long paragraphs one after another.

Content width  
Use a readable text measure of roughly 620–760px for body copy.  
Wider sections can use 2-column grids, cards, or data panels.

Spacing  
Large vertical whitespace between sections.  
Avoid dense blocks. Let sections breathe.

Brand colors  
Deep Navy  
Rust Red  
Warm Gold  
Ivory  
Charcoal

Use navy as the anchor color, ivory/white as the dominant background, rust and gold as restrained accents.

Typography  
Serif or editorial display face for major headings.  
Clean sans serif for body copy, metadata, labels, buttons, and navigation.  
Do not use bold everywhere. Let size, spacing, and contrast create hierarchy.

Image direction  
Airy, architectural, premium, editorial.  
No cliché gavels, oversized scales, staged handshakes, fake court drama, or generic corporate team imagery.  
Images should feel like modern legal-business journalism rather than a law-firm brochure.

SECTION HEADING  
A number without a definition is not a benchmark.

BODY COPY  
Our research is designed to show the work behind the conclusion, because the same figure can tell a very different story depending on how it was collected and what it includes.

When a statistic matters, we look for the original source. When two sources disagree, we look at definitions, dates, samples, and methodology before treating either figure as definitive. When a market claim is based on a narrow survey, a vendor dataset, or a modeled forecast, we make that context visible.

That does not make every answer simple, and sometimes the evidence will remain incomplete. What it does is make the conclusion easier to understand, challenge, and use.

HEADING  
Why Verdict Point Exists

BODY  
Legal-industry information often arrives with a point of view attached to it, and that is not necessarily a problem. Software companies publish technology research because they understand their users. Marketing agencies publish search advice because they see campaign performance. Industry organizations publish member research because they have access to long-running communities and datasets. Newsrooms report the stories moving fastest because that is where their reporting strength lies.

Each perspective can be useful. At the same time, each sees the market from a different position.

Verdict Point exists to connect those views without pretending they are interchangeable. We look across datasets, company disclosures, surveys, public records, industry reports, and original research so that we can understand what a number actually measures, where its limitations sit, and how much weight it deserves.

HEADING  
How We Use AI and Automation

BODY  
Technology may assist with research organization, drafting, data cleanup, or editing, but it does not replace source verification.

Factual claims, statistics, and attributed findings should be checked against the underlying source before publication. When automation materially affects how a research asset was produced, we can explain the process if that context helps readers evaluate the work.

The goal is to use technology to improve the research process without asking readers to trust a claim simply because it was presented confidently.

SOURCE OF TRUTH  
Use the approved Verdict Point brand identity board for visual direction.

COLOR PALETTE  
Deep Navy — \#0F2A44  
Primary brand anchor. Use for navigation, headings, footer, dark sections, icons, and high-contrast UI.

Rust Red — \#8B2E2E  
Editorial accent. Use sparingly for rules, active states, small labels, highlighted links, data emphasis, and selected CTAs.

Warm Gold — \#C9A96A  
Premium secondary accent. Use for dividers, subtle highlights, key numbers, methodology details, and restrained decorative elements.

Ivory — \#F7F4ED  
Primary warm background. Use for large page sections, cards, soft contrast areas, and editorial whitespace.

Charcoal — \#2B2F36  
Primary body-copy neutral. Use for paragraphs, captions, metadata, and secondary headings on light backgrounds.

RECOMMENDED COLOR BALANCE  
55–65% white / ivory  
20–25% deep navy  
5–10% charcoal  
3–5% rust red  
2–4% warm gold

Do not make every section dark navy. The brand should feel airy and editorial, not heavy.

WEBSITE TYPOGRAPHY  
The logo wordmark is treated as a custom brand asset. Do not attempt to recreate the logo with live web text.

Recommended web font system:  
Display / H1 / H2: Playfair Display, weight 500  
H3 / Pull Quotes: Playfair Display, weight 500  
Body Copy: Inter, weight 400  
UI / Navigation / Buttons: Inter, weight 500  
Eyebrows / Labels: Inter, weight 500, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.10–0.14em  
Data / Table Labels: Inter, weight 500

Avoid heavy 700–900 weights unless needed for accessibility or a small UI emphasis. Verdict Point should feel authoritative through proportion, spacing, and contrast rather than excessive bolding.

VISUAL DETAILS  
Use thin rust-red or warm-gold rules instead of thick borders.
