# Law Firm SEO: A Practical 90-Day Plan for Small Firms

Law firm SEO is the work of making a legal website understandable, useful, and eligible to appear for relevant searches. A small firm needs a focused system: accurate business information, crawlable pages, strong practice-area coverage, attorney evidence, and measurement tied to qualified inquiries.

This 90-day plan puts those tasks in order. It uses Google's current documentation, legal-marketing evidence, and attorney-advertising guidance. It does not promise a ranking position or a fixed timeline.

## Key Takeaways

- **Baseline First:** Confirm Search Console, indexing, and the Google Business Profile before writing new pages.
- **Page Structure:** Give each practice area a useful page with accurate attorney and firm details.
- **Evidence-Based Content:** Publish answer-first content drawn from real questions and cited sources.
- **Measure Inquiries:** Track qualified inquiries alongside clicks, impressions, and average position.

## Why Small Firms Need a Narrow Plan

Search competition is expensive and uneven. Ubersuggest estimated 4,400 U.S. monthly searches for "law firm SEO" on October 25, 2026. It reported SEO difficulty of 40 and an average paid-search cost of $103.24 per click.

Those figures measure a marketing query, not demand for every legal service. They still show why agencies compete heavily for law-firm attention. A small practice should avoid copying enterprise plans built around dozens of offices and broad national topics.

Client acquisition also spans more than search. A [Clio article updated in November 2025](https://www.clio.com/blog/how-do-clients-find-their-lawyers/) reports that 59% of surveyed clients sought referrals and 17% used an online search engine. The figures come from Clio's referenced client research and should not be treated as a current universal split. They support a practical point: search should reinforce reputation and intake, not sit apart from them.

## What Google Says About Search Visibility

Google describes search as crawling, indexing, and serving results. A page cannot compete if Google cannot discover or index it.

Google's [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) recommends useful, reliable, people-first content. It also advises publishers to use the words readers use in titles, headings, alt text, and link text.

For local results, Google names three main factors:

- **Relevance:** how well the business matches the search.
- **Distance:** how far the business is from the searcher or stated location.
- **Prominence:** how well known the business appears across links, reviews, and other signals.

Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local position. The firm can improve its information and reputation signals, but it cannot control distance or guarantee placement.

## Days 1–15: Build a Reliable Baseline

The first phase checks whether the site and local profile can be found. It also records performance before changes begin.

### Verify Search Console and Indexing

Connect the correct website property to Google Search Console. Submit the XML sitemap, then inspect the homepage and every main practice page.

Record these baseline values for the prior 90 days:

total clicks and impressions;

branded and nonbranded queries;

pages receiving search traffic;

device and country distribution;

indexing exclusions and crawl errors;

inquiries attributed to organic search.

The Search Console Performance report shows clicks, impressions, click-through rate, and average position. Average position reflects the topmost result from your site, so read it alongside clicks before drawing conclusions.

### Check the Technical Foundation

Crawl the site and fix faults that block discovery or frustrate users. Start with issues that affect important pages.

The first technical review should cover:

accidental `noindex` tags and blocked resources;

broken internal links and redirect chains;

duplicate or conflicting canonical URLs;

missing sitemap entries;

secure HTTPS delivery;

mobile content parity;

slow templates and intrusive popups;

incorrect titles, headings, and status codes.

Google says page experience can affect search performance. Its documentation also warns that good page experience alone does not guarantee top rankings.

### Audit the Google Business Profile

Confirm the legal business name, address, phone number, hours, website, and primary category. Remove old phone numbers and closed-office references from major directories.

Google permits individual practitioner profiles under specific conditions. A lawyer must be public-facing and directly contactable at the stated location. Support staff should not create practitioner profiles.

Do not create profiles for virtual offices that the firm does not staff during stated hours. Follow the profile rules before changing names, locations, or practitioner listings.

## Days 16–30: Fix the Site Architecture

The website should map the firm's real services to the problems prospective clients search for. One page should own each primary intent.

### Create a Page Map

List every existing page beside its intended query, audience, and action. Merge pages that compete for the same purpose.

A small-firm structure often needs:

one homepage for the firm and core location;

one page for each main practice area;

one page for each staffed office;

attorney biography pages;

an About page with verifiable firm details;

a contact page with clear intake expectations;

supporting articles that answer narrower questions.

Avoid a separate page for every keyword variation. A useful page can address related language when the underlying need is the same.

### Improve Practice-Area Pages

A practice page should explain what the firm handles, who it serves, and what a prospective client can expect next. It should also show why the named attorneys can speak on the topic. A detailed method for these pages appears in [law firm SEO for practice-area pages](/legal-marketing/law-firm-seo-practice-area-pages/).

Include these elements where accurate:

a plain-language description of the legal issue;

matters the practice covers and excludes;

the general process and possible decision points;

attorney names, licenses, and relevant experience;

jurisdiction and office information;

links to related questions and official sources;

a clear, restrained contact action.

Do not invent outcomes, client numbers, awards, or experience. Claims about specialization, past results, and testimonials require review under the firm's jurisdictional rules.

### Strengthen Attorney and Firm Entities

Use the same firm and attorney names across the site, profiles, bar records, and major directories. Link each byline to a complete biography.

Google's Organization structured-data guidance says markup can help it understand administrative details and distinguish an organization. Structured data should describe visible facts. It does not replace a clear page or create a ranking entitlement.

## Days 31–60: Publish Evidence-Based Content

Content should answer questions that connect to the firm's actual work. Begin with a small cluster around one priority practice area.

### Choose Topics from Real Demand

Use four evidence sources:

Search Console queries where the site already receives impressions.

Intake questions asked by qualified prospects.

Current result pages for relevant local and legal queries.

Keyword data showing demand, intent, and competitive difficulty.

Group questions by decision stage. A reader may need a definition, a process explanation, a cost framework, a deadline overview, or a comparison. Do not force all questions into a listicle.

### Use an Answer-First Article Format

Each article should state the answer near the top, then explain conditions and exceptions. Legal subjects need careful jurisdiction and date labels.

A useful article includes:

one precise title and search purpose;

a direct opening answer;

descriptive headings based on real questions;

primary sources for statutes, rules, or statistics;

a named author and reviewer where appropriate;

a reviewed or updated date;

two or three relevant internal links;

a short FAQ based on distinct follow-up questions.

Google's current guidance for AI search repeats the same foundation. It recommends clear technical structure and original, expert-led content that adds value beyond common summaries.

### Build Internal Links Deliberately

Every supporting article should link to the relevant practice page. Practice pages should link back to selected guides when those guides answer a next question.

Use descriptive anchor text. Google's link guidance says concise, relevant anchors help readers and Google understand the destination.

Do not add dozens of repetitive keyword links. One helpful connection is stronger than a footer filled with near-duplicate anchors.

## Days 61–75: Improve Local Trust Signals

Local work should make the firm's real-world presence easier to verify. It should not manufacture extra locations.

### Maintain Accurate Listings

Check the firm's name, address, phone number, website, and hours across priority directories. Fix inconsistencies that could confuse a prospective client.

For every staffed office, confirm:

the office page matches the Business Profile;

calls reach the correct intake team;

listed hours reflect actual availability;

attorneys shown at the office work there;

directions, parking, and accessibility details are accurate.

Location pages should contain office-specific information. Swapping a city name into identical copy adds little value.

### Create an Ethical Review Process

The [American Bar Association's September 2026 website review](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/law-practice-today/2026/september-2026/what-does-your-law-firms-websites-say-to-prospective-clients/) identifies Model Rules 7.1, 7.2, 1.18, and 1.6 as relevant starting points. State rules and ethics opinions control in each jurisdiction.

Review these items before publication:

descriptions of experience or expertise;

testimonials and past results;

awards and third-party badges;

fee statements and consultation offers;

contact forms and chatbot prompts;

privacy notices and tracking scripts;

claims about office locations or availability.

Marketing staff can manage the workflow. A qualified lawyer should review claims governed by professional rules.

### Earn Relevant Mentions

Useful links and mentions often follow real participation. Start with relationships the firm already has.

Potential sources include:

local and state bar associations;

legitimate legal and business directories;

community organizations the firm supports;

authored articles and professional presentations;

original research that publishers can verify;

expert comments tied to the attorney's practice.

Avoid buying bulk links or publishing thin guest posts across unrelated sites. Google's spam policies can treat manipulative link practices as violations.

## Days 76–90: Measure Inquiries and Improve the Plan

Rankings are diagnostic signals. The business outcome is qualified work that the firm can serve. Choose measures from [law firm SEO metrics](/legal-marketing/law-firm-seo-metrics/) that connect to signed work. Market context appears in [law firm marketing statistics](/legal-marketing/law-firm-marketing-statistics/).

Create a simple monthly report with agreed definitions.

| Measure | What It Shows | Important Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nonbranded impressions | Visibility beyond firm-name searches | An impression does not equal attention |
| Organic clicks | Visits from unpaid search | A click may be irrelevant |
| Qualified inquiries | Potential matters meeting intake criteria | Requires consistent intake coding |
| Consultations booked | Progress through intake | Scheduling quality affects the number |
| Signed matters | Retained work attributed to search | Attribution can be incomplete |
| Inquiry-to-client rate | Intake conversion quality | Compare similar practice areas |
| Priority pages indexed | Search eligibility | Indexing does not guarantee ranking |
| Business Profile actions | Calls, directions, and website visits | Platform reporting has limits |

Search Console explains that average position uses the topmost result from the property. It should not be treated as a precise rank for every user and location.

Compare 28-day and 90-day periods, while noting seasonality and material site changes. Review query groups and landing pages rather than celebrating one isolated keyword.

## A Practical Weekly Operating Rhythm

Small firms can maintain the program without publishing every day. Assign one owner and a short weekly block.

Use this cadence:

- **Monday:** review indexing, major errors, and profile changes;
- **Tuesday:** update one priority page with new evidence;
- **Wednesday:** answer one recurring intake question;
- **Thursday:** verify listings, reviews, and local information;
- **Friday:** connect Search Console data with inquiry records.

Monthly, review the pages that gained or lost impressions. Quarterly, revisit content accuracy, attorney biographies, service scope, and ethical approval.

## What to Avoid

Most small-firm SEO waste comes from expanding before the foundation works. Common failures include: Some law firm SEO tactics create risk without producing inquiries.

publishing many generic AI-written articles;

creating duplicate city pages for unstaffed locations;

opening practitioner profiles that break Google's rules;

reporting traffic without qualified inquiries;

hiding key information behind scripts or forms;

letting old attorney or office details remain live;

claiming guaranteed rankings or case outcomes;

using statistics without the study date or source.

Google advises that a small local business can complete much SEO work internally. Hire outside help when the site, competition, or available time requires it. Ask any provider how work will be measured and whether it follows Google Search Essentials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Long Does Law Firm SEO Take?

Google says some changes can appear quickly, while others take weeks or months. Competition, site history, technical condition, and content quality affect the timeline.

### How Much Content Should a Small Law Firm Publish?

Publish only as much as the firm can research, review, and maintain. A focused set of strong practice pages and supporting answers is better than a large archive of repetitive posts.

### Does a Law Firm Need a Google Business Profile?

A public-facing local practice can use a verified profile to manage information in Search and Maps. The firm and its practitioners must follow Google's eligibility and representation rules.

### Are Backlinks Required for Law Firm SEO?

Links can support discovery and prominence, but relevance and trust come first. Earn links through useful work, professional relationships, and verifiable expertise.

### Should SEO Reports Include Signed Cases?

Yes, when the firm can track them consistently and lawfully. Reports should connect search activity to qualified inquiries, consultations, and retained matters while acknowledging attribution gaps.
