# Legal Industry Statistics: How Firm Size Shapes the U.S. Market

The U.S. legal market is often described through one large revenue number or a headline about the biggest firms. Neither view shows how the industry is built. The latest verified firm-size data points in the opposite direction: most offices of lawyers belong to enterprises with fewer than 20 employees, and nearly three-quarters are in enterprises with fewer than five.

That structure matters when people interpret legal industry statistics. A national employment figure cannot describe the economics of a solo practice. Results from a group of large and midsize firms cannot be applied to every local office. Even the words “firm” and “establishment” refer to different units in Census data.

This article separates those measures before comparing them. It uses the official 2022 Statistics of U.S. Businesses firm-size workbook, the latest verified federal pay figure, and current market reporting without presenting older structural data as if it were collected in 2026.

## Key Statistics and Data

The figures below describe different parts of the market. The reporting year and population matter as much as the number itself.

- \*\*Firms in Offices of Lawyers:\*\* The 2022 U.S. Census SUSB workbook reports 156,583 firms classified under NAICS 541110, Offices of Lawyers.  
- \*\*Small-Enterprise Concentration:\*\* Of those firms, 149,187, or 95.28%, were in enterprises with fewer than 20 employees.  
- \*\*Fewer Than Five Employees:\*\* The largest size band contained 116,668 firms, equal to 74.51% of the total.  
- \*\*Establishments and Firms Differ:\*\* The same table reports 166,972 establishments. One enterprise can operate more than one establishment.  
- \*\*Industry Employment:\*\* Offices of Lawyers accounted for 1,078,148 employees in the SUSB table. This is employment, not the number of licensed attorneys.  
- \*\*Lawyer Pay:\*\* The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a $159,670 median annual wage for lawyers in May 2025. That measure excludes self-employed workers and is not partner profit.  
- \*\*Large-Firm Market Pulse:\*\* Thomson Reuters Institute reported 13% profit growth and 7.3% worked-rate growth for 2025 in the market covered by its 2026 report. The results should not be generalized to all U.S. firms.

## Start With Who and What the Data Counts

Legal-market sources count people, businesses, locations, and money. Those categories overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A useful comparison begins by defining the unit behind each figure.

| Measure | What It Counts | What It Should Not Be Called |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Licensed Lawyers | People admitted to practice under the source’s rules | Employees or law firms |  
| Employed Lawyers | Wage-and-salary workers in the lawyer occupation | All licensed lawyers or partners |  
| Firm | A business enterprise under the Census definition | An office location |  
| Establishment | One operating location | A unique law firm |  
| Enterprise Employment Size | Employees across the entire enterprise | Number of attorneys in one office |  
| Revenue or Receipts | Business income under a named dataset and period | Profit or lawyer compensation |

The difference between a firm and an establishment is especially important. A firm is the enterprise. An establishment is a location where business is conducted. A single firm with several offices can therefore contribute one firm but several establishments to the same dataset.

The size bands also use employees across the enterprise. They do not report the number of lawyers, partners, or billers. A 15-person enterprise could include attorneys, paralegals, administrators, intake employees, and other staff. Calling that a “15-lawyer firm” would change the meaning of the source.

\> Definition Box  
\> In this article, “firm size” follows the Census SUSB definition: employment across the entire enterprise. It does not mean attorney headcount, partner count, or employees at one office.

## Most U.S. Law Firms Are Small Enterprises

The \[official 2022 SUSB detailed-size workbook\](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us\_state\_naics\_detailedsizes\_2022.xlsx) provides a direct firm-size view for NAICS 541110, Offices of Lawyers. It reports 156,583 firms after applying the Census definition and disclosure methods.

The distribution is heavily concentrated at the small end. Firms in enterprises with fewer than five employees account for 74.51% of the total. Adding the 5–9, 10–14, and 15–19 bands raises the under-20 share to 95.28%.

| Enterprise Employment Size | Firms | Share of 156,583 Firms |  
| --- | ---: | ---: |  
| Under 5 employees | 116,668 | 74.51% |  
| 5–9 employees | 22,518 | 14.38% |  
| 10–14 employees | 6,946 | 4.44% |  
| 15–19 employees | 3,055 | 1.95% |  
| 20–49 employees | 4,912 | 3.14% |  
| 50–99 employees | 1,333 | 0.85% |  
| 100–499 employees | 948 | 0.61% |  
| 500 or more employees | 203 | 0.13% |

The broader bands in the lower half of the table are calculated only from the verified, non-overlapping Census rows. For example, the 20–49 band combines the 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35–39, and 40–49 rows. It does not combine a subtotal with its underlying bands.

These legal industry statistics do not say that small firms serve small matters or produce modest revenue. They show only how many firms fall into each enterprise employment band. Practice area, client type, geography, pricing model, ownership, and demand still shape the economics within each group.

## Firm Counts and Office Counts Answer Different Questions

The same SUSB table reports 166,972 establishments, compared with 156,583 firms. The gap is not an error. It reflects enterprises that operate more than one location.

A firm count is useful when the question concerns business ownership or enterprise size. An establishment count is more useful when the question concerns operating locations, local employment, or geographic presence. Neither number should silently replace the other.

This distinction becomes more important as firm size rises. A local practice may have one enterprise and one office. A national firm may remain one enterprise while operating dozens of establishments. Counting locations as separate firms would overstate the number of independent businesses.

The practical rule is straightforward: retain the source’s noun. If a table reports establishments, call them establishments. If it reports firms, call them firms. That small wording choice prevents a large analytical error.

## Employment Does Not Equal the Lawyer Population

The SUSB table reports 1,078,148 employees in Offices of Lawyers. That total includes the employees covered by the business dataset, not only people working as lawyers. It can include legal support, finance, operations, technology, marketing, intake, and administrative roles.

Occupational data answers a different question. The \[Bureau of Labor Statistics lawyer profile\](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/lawyers.htm) reports a median annual wage of $159,670 for lawyers in May 2025. Median means half of the wage-and-salary workers in the occupation earned more and half earned less.

That wage should not be treated as the income of a typical law-firm owner. BLS notes that its wage figures do not include self-employed workers, and partner economics can involve draws, distributions, capital arrangements, and business profit rather than wages alone.

The distinction also affects technology planning. A firm’s headcount may include many roles that touch case management, billing, intake, or document systems. For that reason, adoption figures in \[legal technology statistics\](https://verdictpoint.org/legal-tech/legal-technology-statistics/) should be compared with workflow roles rather than attorney counts alone.

## Large-Firm Results Are a Market Pulse, Not a Census

Current reports from large and midsize firms can show where one commercially important segment is moving. They cannot describe every business in a market where 95.28% of firms have fewer than 20 enterprise employees.

The \[2026 State of the U.S. Legal Market\](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/reports/state-of-the-us-legal-market-2026) reported 13% profit growth and 7.3% growth in worked rates during 2025 for the market it monitors. Those are strong results, but the proper conclusion is limited: the tracked segment had a profitable year supported in part by rate growth.

It would be inaccurate to apply that 13% figure to all 156,583 firms in the Census table. The sources use different populations, methods, and periods. Smaller firms may have different client mixes, pricing power, staffing models, and exposure to demand shifts.

The same caution applies when reading \[law firm profitability statistics\](https://verdictpoint.org/law-firms/law-firm-profitability-statistics/). Revenue growth, profit growth, partner profit, utilization, realization, and collected revenue are separate measures. Firm size may change how each one behaves.

## Firm Size Changes the Operating Model

The Census distribution helps explain why one industry-wide operating benchmark is rarely enough. A small practice and a multi-office enterprise do not carry the same staffing, technology, marketing, or governance structure.

### Smaller Enterprises Concentrate Responsibilities

In a smaller firm, lawyers may also oversee intake, client communication, billing, hiring, and marketing. Cash expenses can look lean while owner time carries work that a larger business assigns to employees or vendors.

That makes cost comparisons difficult. A lower payroll line does not necessarily mean the work disappears. It may be absorbed by partners or staff whose time is recorded elsewhere, if it is recorded at all.

### Larger Enterprises Add Coordination Costs

As firms grow, they can support specialized teams in finance, pricing, business development, information security, knowledge management, and operations. Specialization may improve control and capacity, but it also creates coordination needs across people, systems, offices, and client requirements.

Billing illustrates the difference. A small firm may rely on a compact process managed by a few people. A larger enterprise may need approval rules, rate arrangements, invoice review, e-billing formats, and reporting across many matters. The most relevant \[law firm billing statistics\](https://verdictpoint.org/law-firms/law-firm-billing-statistics/) therefore depend on what the firm is trying to diagnose.

## How to Compare Legal-Market Numbers Without Mixing Them

A disciplined comparison does not require every source to use the same method. It requires the reader to keep unlike measures separate and explain what each one contributes.

1. \*\*Name the Population:\*\* State whether the source covers firms, establishments, employees, licensed lawyers, or a surveyed segment.  
2. \*\*Retain the Reporting Year:\*\* A 2022 structural count can remain useful, but it must not be described as a 2026 count.  
3. \*\*Define the Size Measure:\*\* Specify whether size is based on enterprise employment, office employment, attorney headcount, revenue, or another measure.  
4. \*\*Separate Structure From Momentum:\*\* Census data can describe the market’s shape, while a current report can show movement in a narrower segment.  
5. \*\*Keep Wages, Revenue, and Profit Apart:\*\* Employee pay does not equal owner income, and revenue does not equal profit.  
6. \*\*Preserve Source Limits:\*\* Disclosure flags, exclusions, survey samples, and coverage rules belong with the finding.

Following these steps makes the numbers less dramatic but more useful. It also reduces the risk that a benchmark built for one segment becomes an unrealistic target for another.

## What the Firm-Size Data Means for 2026–2027 Planning

The strongest conclusion is structural rather than predictive. The U.S. market for offices of lawyers contains a small number of very large enterprises and a much larger population of small ones. In the 2022 SUSB data, nearly three out of four firms had fewer than five enterprise employees, and more than 95% had fewer than 20.

That distribution should shape how legal industry statistics are used. A technology budget, compensation comparison, marketing benchmark, or profitability target should be matched to a relevant operating model. A figure from a large-firm report may still be valuable, but it should be labeled as evidence about that segment rather than the whole market.

For planning into 2027, the safest approach is to use the latest verified structural dataset for market composition, then add newer sources for pay, demand, pricing, and profitability. That preserves freshness without pretending every measure was collected in the same year.
