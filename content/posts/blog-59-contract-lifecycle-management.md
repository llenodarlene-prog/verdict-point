# Contract Lifecycle Management Stages and Ownership

Contract lifecycle management covers an agreement from its initial request through performance, renewal, or closeout. Drafting and signatures form part of that process. Someone must also handle the commitments that follow, including changes, review points, and decisions about continuing the relationship.

For a legal team, the practical question concerns ownership. Who supplies the facts, approves exceptions, monitors performance, and acts before a notice deadline? A contract repository can hold the document without answering those questions.

Start with a clear process and the records each handoff requires. The seven-stage map below offers a planning framework, rather than a mandatory industry standard. Its suggested owners and examples require adaptation to the actual agreement and organization.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lifecycle Scope:** Include intake, negotiation, approvals, performance, changes, and the final renewal or closeout decision.
- **Named Ownership:** Identify an accountable person for each stage, with backup coverage and escalation arrangements.
- **Handoff Evidence:** Pass the approved agreement and relevant instructions to the people responsible for delivery.
- **Contract Terms:** Verify deadlines, notice methods, and obligations against the actual documents before recording instructions.

## Separate the Process from the Software

CLM describes a way of managing agreements, while a software product supports selected activities within that process. [IBM's explanation](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/contract-lifecycle-management) spans drafting through renewal or termination. It also notes that organizations use different numbers of stages. Broader adoption figures for legal software appear in the [legal technology statistics](/legal-tech/legal-technology-statistics/).

That variation matters when comparing process diagrams. A three-phase overview can group activities that another framework separates. Counting boxes tells you little about whether the organization has assigned the work within them.

Likewise, SAP's lifecycle overview includes ongoing management and renewal alongside creation and execution. This helps distinguish managing an active agreement from completing a signature request. The product decision should follow the work the organization needs to support.

Before selecting a platform, ask colleagues to trace one agreement through their existing process. Identify where the file moves, who makes decisions, and which facts need checking. Record gaps before proposing an automated replacement.

## A Seven Stage Ownership Map

Use the following map to connect each stage with a responsible role and completion evidence. The assignments are recommendations for discussion. They do not establish signing authority, professional duties, or another organization's actual reporting structure.

The receiving owner should understand what the previous stage has completed and what remains unresolved.

| Stage | Accountable Role to Identify | Evidence Needed Before Handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request and intake | Business requester or designated intake owner | Purpose, parties, scope, required timing, responsible business owner |
| Draft preparation | Assigned legal or contracts professional | Appropriate starting document and confirmed commercial inputs |
| Negotiation and review | Designated negotiation lead | Current draft, tracked changes, open issues, specialist input |
| Internal approval | Approvers under company policy | Recorded approvals and resolution of required exceptions |
| Signature and handover | Execution coordinator and receiving owner | Final signed agreement, schedules, instructions, accepted ownership |
| Performance and changes | Named operational contract owner | Obligation register, performance records, approved change documents |
| Renewal or closeout | Business decision owner | Review decision, verified notice instructions, final records |

One person may fill several roles, but record that deliberately. If a team shares responsibility, name the person coordinating its response. A department label alone leaves the next person guessing whom to contact.

For significant decisions, connect this map with the [legal leader's responsibilities and authority](/legal-business/general-counsel/). Legal review and business approval require separate clarification. The workflow should identify each decision rather than treating every legal reviewer as an authorized signer.

## Start with a Request Someone Can Act On

Intake should give the receiving team enough information to identify the work. Ask for the business purpose, proposed parties, expected services, and desired timing. Distinguish an external deadline from an internal preference so reviewers understand the request.

Use a short intake record with practical fields.

- **Parties and Entities:** Identify which legal entities would enter the agreement and who supplied that information.
- **Commercial Scope:** Describe the goods, services, payment structure, and expected period without substituting assumptions for negotiations.
- **Business Ownership:** Name the person responsible for explaining the need and coordinating operational input.
- **Documents and Timing:** Attach the proposed paper and explain any requested signature date or unusual dependency.

If an item remains unknown, mark it unresolved and assign follow-up. Do not let a required field encourage someone to invent a price or entity name. A complete-looking form can still contain unverified information.

For draft preparation, identify the authorized starting document and who maintains it. Confirm whether the transaction fits that document's intended use. Have the responsible professionals assess unusual terms rather than treating a template as automatic approval.

## Keep Negotiation and Approval Distinct

Negotiation produces proposed terms, while internal approval records the organization's decision under its own arrangements. Track both activities without allowing a revised attachment to obscure which version received approval. Keep unresolved commercial and legal questions visible.

An issue log can help participants prepare a decision. As a recommended format, record the clause or business issue, proposed change, responsible reviewer, and current position. Link each entry to the draft it concerns.

Before requesting approval, check the following points.

- **Current Version:** Confirm which document and attachments form the proposed agreement.
- **Relevant Reviewers:** Identify which people need to assess financial, operational, privacy, or other applicable issues.
- **Exceptions:** Show deviations requiring a decision and record the authorized response.
- **Changed Terms:** Revisit approvals when later revisions affect the terms those approvals covered.

Also check how the organization distinguishes approval from authority to execute. A workflow button can record a step without establishing the person's authority for the transaction. Resolve that question through the applicable corporate arrangements and qualified advice.

Keep the final decision with the approved version. If the negotiation restarts, preserve the earlier record and explain what changed. Avoid overwriting it with a document that suggests the earlier approval covered new terms.

## Make Signature a Handoff to Delivery

A completed signature process should lead into assigned operational work. [Thomson Reuters' CLM overview](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/what-is-contract-lifecycle-management/) expressly includes monitoring and eventual renewal or termination. That scope extends beyond producing an executed document.

As an organizational reference, the [UK government's contract management principles](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/commercial-capability-contract-management-standards/contract-management-principles) emphasize clear responsibilities and effective handover. They also call for documented management plans. These principles concern UK government practice, rather than binding rules for US private businesses.

For an internal handover, ask the receiving owner to confirm the relevant records and instructions. The following checklist is a proposed workflow, not a legal requirement.

- **Collect the Agreement:** Locate the final signed document, schedules, and incorporated materials needed for the assignment.
- **Confirm Ownership:** Record the operational owner, backup contact, and route for unresolved questions.
- **Extract Commitments:** Identify relevant actions and dates, with references to the supporting provisions.
- **Check Interpretation:** Have the responsible reviewer resolve unclear requirements before turning them into instructions.
- **Confirm Receipt:** Record that the receiving owner accepted the handoff and knows the next action.

Keep document control within that process. A team setting [requirements for legal document management](/legal-tech/document-management-software-law-firms/) should consider versions, access, and retrieval. Those controls support the handoff, while the operational owner remains responsible for the assigned action.

## Build an Obligation Register from the Agreement

An obligation register connects a required action with its owner, timing, and evidence. It should point back to the relevant provision. Treat extracted information as something to verify, particularly when the wording depends on another clause or document.

Use a compact record rather than relying on a calendar title alone. Capture the action, contract reference, trigger, owner, reviewer, and completion evidence. If an amendment changes the obligation, preserve the relationship between the original and revised records.

The following example is entirely hypothetical. Assume an agreement requires a continuation decision and a notice at least 60 days before expiry. Assume the team starts its review 120 days before expiry, using the same day-counting basis.

| Illustrative Review Point | Time Before Assumed Expiry | Assigned Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal review starts | 120 days | Owner gathers performance and business requirements |
| Assumed notice deadline | 60 days | Authorized team completes the verified notice process |
| Agreement expires | 0 days | Owner follows the agreed continuation or closeout plan |

The interval between the first two points equals 60 days: 120 minus 60. That is an internal planning window in this example. It is not a standard notice period or legal interpretation of a real agreement.

For an actual contract, verify the trigger, counting rules, delivery method, recipient, and any relevant amendment. Have the responsible lawyer resolve ambiguity. A reminder labeled renewal cannot establish that the notice instructions are correct.

## Manage Changes and the Final Decision

When a business proposes a change, identify the affected agreement and required review. Describe how the change would affect scope, price, timing, or responsibilities. Keep the proposal distinct from an approved amendment or other operative document.

Ask the process owner to maintain a clear sequence: proposed change, review, approval, documented outcome, and updated operating instructions. This is a recommended control sequence. The actual agreement and applicable requirements determine what formal steps the parties need.

For renewal, gather performance evidence and the current business need before seeking a decision. Identify unresolved issues, alternatives, and any instruction that needs legal review. Record the decision and the person authorized to carry it out.

For closeout, ask what remains outstanding. Check final deliverables, payment questions, records, and any continuing commitments requiring attention. Do not assume that an expiry date makes every remaining issue disappear.

Assign each outstanding action to someone who can confirm its completion. Preserve the final record and the basis for any unresolved item. This creates a usable handoff if a dispute or later question arises.

## Measure the Work with Defined Start and End Points

Useful contract lifecycle management reporting distinguishes different activities and populations. Define what enters each measure before comparing results. Otherwise, a shorter reported turnaround could reflect a changed definition rather than a changed process.

For a proposed cycle-time measure, choose one start event and one end event. For example, measure calendar days from accepted intake to final internal approval. Record pauses separately if you want to examine them rather than silently subtracting them.

Then document these boundaries alongside the report.

- **Included Agreements:** State which contract types, entities, or teams form the measured group.
- **Observation Period:** Identify when the agreements entered or completed the defined process.
- **Open Work:** Explain whether incomplete matters appear separately or fall outside the completed group.
- **Changed Scope:** Record major changes that make an agreement unlike the original request.

Keep signature speed separate from ongoing performance. An agreement can move quickly through approval without demonstrating successful delivery. Use the records relevant to each question rather than treating one timing measure as overall success.

## Pilot One Agreement Type Before Expanding

Start with a defined agreement type and a manageable set of participants. Walk through the proposed ownership map using approved sample documents. Check what each person receives and whether the next action is clear.

Include a scenario involving an unresolved term, a changed approver, and a post-signature amendment. Ask participants to locate the correct record and explain who decides. Record gaps and revise the process before expanding it.

If evaluating software, ask vendors to demonstrate those same tasks. Verify the relevant configuration, available support, and written commercial terms. Do not infer that a product can supply an owner or approval policy the organization has not defined.

Before expanding the workflow, confirm who maintains it and handles exceptions. Keep the contract lifecycle management map beside its current operating instructions. Revisit ownership when people, responsibilities, or business requirements change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Does Contract Lifecycle Management Include After Signing?

Contract lifecycle management after signing includes managing performance, obligations, changes, and eventual renewal or closeout. The agreement and assigned responsibilities determine the specific actions.

### Are Seven Stages Mandatory?

No, organizations group contract lifecycle management activities differently. The seven-stage map here is a recommended planning framework.

### Does Legal Automatically Own Every Commitment?

Do not infer ownership from legal review alone. Assign the operational action and clarify which decisions require legal input.

### Is a Renewal Reminder Enough?

A reminder does not verify the underlying notice requirement. Check the agreement, amendments, responsible person, and required delivery process.

### Can Software Replace an Approval Policy?

Contract lifecycle management software does not set policy. The organization still needs to define its decision authority and review requirements. Evaluate software against those documented instructions.
