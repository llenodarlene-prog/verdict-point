# Car Accident Statistics: Fatal Crashes, Deaths, and Risk Measures

US traffic deaths fell to an estimated 36,640 in 2025, a 6.7% drop from 2024, according to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA). The estimated fatality rate was 1.10 deaths per 100 million vehicle miles traveled, which NHTSA calls the second-lowest rate in recorded history.

The 2025 figures are early estimates. The latest final data cover 2024: 36,297 fatal crashes that killed 39,254 people. Good car accident statistics keep those two kinds of numbers apart, because estimates change once every record is in.

This page sets out the latest estimate, the final 2024 counts, who died, and how to read the different risk rates. We checked each figure against NHTSA sources on November 13, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **2025 Estimate:** NHTSA estimates 36,640 traffic deaths in 2025, down 6.7% from 2024.
- **Record-Low Rate Range:** The 2025 estimated rate of 1.10 deaths per 100 million miles is the second-lowest on record.
- **Final 2024 Count:** There were 36,297 fatal crashes and 39,254 deaths in 2024.
- **People Outside Cars:** Pedestrians made up 7,080 deaths and motorcyclists 6,228 in 2024.
- **Alcohol Share Fell:** Alcohol-impaired-driving deaths fell from 48% of fatalities in 1982 to 30% in 2024.

## The 2025 Early Estimate

NHTSA announced its 2025 estimate in an [April 1, 2026 press release](https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/traffic-deaths-2025-early-estimates-2024-annual). The agency reported an estimated 36,640 traffic fatalities, a 6.7% decrease from 2024, and a fatality rate of 1.10 per 100 million vehicle miles traveled.

| Measure | 2025 Estimate | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Traffic deaths | 36,640 | Early estimate |
| Change from 2024 | -6.7% | Early estimate |
| Deaths per 100 million miles | 1.10 | Early estimate |

An early estimate is a statistical projection. NHTSA revises it once states finish sending complete crash records. Treat 2025 figures as preliminary until the final file is released.

## Final 2024 Car Accident Statistics

NHTSA's [Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS) encyclopedia](https://www-fars.nhtsa.dot.gov/Main/index.aspx) holds the final 2024 national figures.

| Measure, 2024 | Figure |
|---|---:|
| Fatal crashes | 36,297 |
| Deaths | 39,254 |
| Deaths per 100 million vehicle miles | 1.19 |
| Deaths per 100,000 people | 11.54 |
| Deaths per 100,000 registered vehicles | 12.69 |
| Deaths per 100,000 licensed drivers | 16.37 |
| Vehicle miles traveled | 3,294 billion |

Fatal crashes fell from 37,769 in 2023 to 36,297 in 2024. At the same time, Americans drove more, with vehicle miles rising from 3,247 billion to 3,294 billion.

## Crashes Versus Deaths

A fatal crash is one event. One crash can kill more than one person. That is why 2024 had 2,957 more deaths than fatal crashes, by our calculation.

Dividing 39,254 deaths by 36,297 crashes gives about 1.08 deaths per fatal crash. That average describes the whole year. It does not predict what happens in any single crash.

Keep the unit clear when you quote car accident statistics. "36,297" counts crashes. "39,254" counts people.

## Who Died in 2024

FARS breaks deaths down by the person's role.

| Person Type, 2024 | Deaths |
|---|---:|
| Drivers | 18,686 |
| Passengers | 5,746 |
| Pedestrians | 7,080 |
| Motorcyclists | 6,228 |
| Pedalcyclists | 1,103 |

Drivers and passengers together account for 24,432 deaths, by our calculation. Nearly one in five deaths was a pedestrian.

FARS notes one classification change. Starting in 2022, people on motorized bicycles count as pedalcyclists, not motorcyclists. Comparisons with earlier years should account for that.

## People Outside the Vehicle

A large share of traffic deaths happen to people who are not inside a car or truck. By our calculation from the FARS counts, pedestrians and pedalcyclists together accounted for 8,183 deaths in 2024, about 20.8% of the total. Motorcyclists added another 15.9%.

Drivers and passengers made up about 62.2% of deaths. So more than a third of the people who die in traffic crashes are on foot, on a bicycle, or on a motorcycle. That is one reason a national total cannot be read as a count of car occupants alone.

## Alcohol and Large Trucks

The FARS encyclopedia highlights two long-term facts. The share of deaths in alcohol-impaired-driving crashes fell from 48% in 1982 to 30% in 2024. That is a large drop, though alcohol still plays a part in nearly a third of deaths.

Large trucks are also tracked. Of the 5,218 large trucks involved in fatal crashes in 2024, 63.0% were combination trucks, such as tractor-trailers.

## How Estimates Get Revised

Early estimates are usually close to the final count, but not identical. NHTSA's [early estimate for 2024](https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813710), published in April 2025, projected 39,345 deaths and a rate of 1.20. The final 2024 figures came in at 39,254 deaths and a rate of 1.19.

| 2024 Figure | Early Estimate (April 2025) | Final FARS |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deaths | 39,345 | 39,254 |
| Deaths per 100 million miles | 1.20 | 1.19 |

The final count was 91 deaths lower than the estimate, by our calculation. A similar revision for 2025 would not change the overall picture of decline.

Revisions happen because states send crash records on different schedules. Some deaths occur weeks after a crash, and some reports need follow-up before they are complete. NHTSA's early estimate fills those gaps with a statistical projection, then replaces it with counted records once the annual file is done. That is why careful writers quote the estimate with its label and update the page when the final number arrives later in the year.

## Where Deaths Fell

The national total hides differences between places. NHTSA's 2024 early estimate projected that 8 of its 10 regions would see fewer deaths and a lower fatality rate than in 2023. It also projected decreases in 35 states and Puerto Rico.

That means some states still saw more deaths even as the national number fell. State-level car accident statistics should come from each state's own final data, which NHTSA publishes through FARS once the annual file is complete.

Driving also kept growing. The same report said vehicle miles traveled in 2024 rose by about 32.3 billion miles, roughly 1%, based on preliminary Federal Highway Administration data. More driving with fewer deaths is what pushes the rate per mile down.

## The Recent Trend

The same 2024 early estimate report shows how deaths moved over the past decade, using the figures available at the time.

| Year | Deaths | Rate per 100 Million Miles |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 36,355 | 1.11 |
| 2020 | 39,007 | 1.34 |
| 2021 | 43,230 | 1.38 |
| 2022 | 42,721 | 1.34 |
| 2025 (estimate) | 36,640 | 1.10 |

Deaths jumped in 2020 and 2021 even as driving fell during the pandemic. They have declined each year since. The 2025 estimate is close to the 2019 level, and its rate is slightly lower.

## Seasons and Quarters

Deaths are not spread evenly through the year. NHTSA's 2024 early estimate report breaks the year into quarters.

| 2024 Quarter | Estimated Deaths | Rate per 100 Million Miles |
|---|---:|---:|
| January to March | 8,595 | 1.13 |
| April to June | 10,085 | 1.19 |
| July to September | 10,565 | 1.23 |
| October to December | 10,100 | 1.24 |

Summer and fall carry the most deaths. The first quarter had about 2,000 fewer deaths than the third, and a lower rate per mile driven. These quarterly car accident statistics are estimates from April 2025, so they differ slightly from the final file.

The same report noted that the fourth quarter of 2024 was the 11th straight quarterly decline in deaths, starting in the second quarter of 2022. It also said the second quarter of 2021 saw the highest quarterly percentage increases in FARS recorded history.

## A Longer View

The 2024 early estimate report includes a decade of yearly totals, using final FARS data through 2022.

| Year | Deaths | Rate per 100 Million Miles |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2013 | 32,893 | 1.10 |
| 2014 | 32,744 | 1.08 |
| 2015 | 35,484 | 1.15 |
| 2016 | 37,806 | 1.19 |
| 2017 | 37,473 | 1.17 |
| 2018 | 36,835 | 1.14 |

The 2025 estimated rate of 1.10 matches 2013 and sits just above 2014's 1.08. That puts the recent improvement in context: it returns the country to rates last seen about a decade ago. NHTSA reports fatality data going back to 1975.

## Which Rate Should You Use?

Each rate answers a different question.

1. **Per 100 million miles:** risk relative to how much people drive.
2. **Per 100,000 people:** the burden on the whole population.
3. **Per 100,000 licensed drivers:** risk relative to the driving population.
4. **Per 100,000 registered vehicles:** risk relative to the vehicle fleet.

The 2025 rate of 1.10 does not mean a driver faces a 1.10% risk. It means about 1.10 deaths occurred for every 100 million miles driven nationwide.

## Who Uses These Numbers

Different readers lean on different parts of the data.

- **Families and crash victims:** National figures give context, but a claim depends on the facts of one crash and the law of one state.
- **Lawyers:** Person-type and rate data help explain risk in injury and wrongful-death cases.
- **Insurers and analysts:** The rate per mile driven is the fairest way to compare years.
- **Policymakers:** Regional and state data show where safety programs may be needed most.

## Common Mistakes With Car Accident Statistics

Pages that rank for car accident statistics often repeat the same errors.

1. **Calling estimates final.** The 2025 figure is a projection. Label it as one.
2. **Mixing crashes and deaths.** Fatal crashes and deaths are different counts.
3. **Using raw counts across years.** More driving means more exposure. Use the rate per mile driven for fair comparisons.
4. **Ignoring classification changes.** FARS moved motorized bicycles from motorcyclists to pedalcyclists in 2022.
5. **Quoting fatal data as all crashes.** FARS covers fatal crashes only. Injury and property-damage crashes need other sources.

## What These Data Cannot Show

National fatality data cover deaths. They do not count the far larger number of injury crashes or crashes that damage only property. They also cannot tell you who was at fault in a given crash.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Many People Died in Car Accidents in 2025?

NHTSA estimates 36,640 traffic deaths in 2025. This is an early estimate covering all motor vehicle traffic deaths.

### How Many Fatal Crashes Were There in 2024?

FARS recorded 36,297 fatal crashes in 2024, which killed 39,254 people.

### Are Traffic Deaths Going Down?

Yes. Deaths have declined each year since 2021, and the 2025 estimate is down 6.7% from 2024.

### What Is the Traffic Fatality Rate?

The estimated 2025 rate is 1.10 deaths per 100 million vehicle miles traveled. The final 2024 rate was 1.19.

### Which Part of the Year Has the Most Traffic Deaths?

In NHTSA's 2024 estimates, July to September had the most deaths, about 10,565, while January to March had the fewest, about 8,595.

### Is the 2025 Number Final?

No. The 2025 figure is an early estimate. NHTSA revises it after states finish reporting, and the 2024 estimate moved by 91 deaths once the final data arrived.

### How Many Pedestrians Die in Traffic Crashes?

FARS recorded 7,080 pedestrian deaths in 2024.
