# What Is a Law Clerk and What Does the Role Involve

What is a law clerk? A law clerk supports legal work through research, analysis, and writing. A judicial clerk helps a judge, while a clerk in an attorney's office supports that office's work. Administrative court clerks handle a different set of records and procedural responsibilities. Read the employer's description to identify the role.

The distinction affects qualifications, applications, and pay comparisons. A federal judicial appointment does not establish the requirements for every position containing clerk. A law-student role and a career judicial position can differ substantially.

Start with the employer and the person receiving the work. Then identify the output, review process, and appointment terms. That approach gives the title a more useful meaning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Judicial clerks support judges:** Research and writing form central parts of many chambers assignments.
- **Attorney-office work differs:** The employer's legal work determines the clerk's tasks and required preparation.
- **Court administration is separate:** Records, docketing, and procedural support do not define the same occupation.
- **Pay measures differ:** A national occupational median cannot substitute for a vacancy's grade and locality rules.

## Identify Which Clerk Role the Employer Means

The employer's work explains the position more reliably than the word clerk. [O*NET classifies judicial law clerks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/23-1012.00) as workers supporting judges through research or legal-document preparation. It separately describes administrative court occupations.

The following table summarizes the distinctions. It describes role categories rather than fixed requirements for every vacancy.

| Role Category | Main Recipient of Work | Work to Investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Judicial law clerk | Employing judge or chambers team | Research, legal analysis, proposed judicial documents |
| Attorney-office clerk | Supervising lawyer or legal team | Research, evidence review, briefs, or other assigned legal support |
| Administrative court clerk | Court office and court users | Filing records, dockets, fees, and procedural administration |

A court can employ both judicial and administrative staff. An applicant should therefore identify the department, supervisor, and duties. The courthouse address alone does not resolve the distinction.

The same care applies to professional authorization. The [difference between a title and permission to practice](/legal/lawyer-vs-attorney/) remains relevant. A clerk title alone does not establish which legal services someone may independently provide.

## What Judicial Law Clerks Do

Judicial clerks prepare research and analysis for a judge's consideration. [OSCAR's official duties information](https://oscar.uscourts.gov/duties_of_federal_law_clerks) describes research, bench memoranda, proposed orders and opinions, and citation verification. The employing judge determines the duties.

A proposed opinion remains a draft for the judge. Preparing analysis does not transfer the judge's decision-making responsibility. Readers should keep that distinction when describing a clerk's influence or career experience.

### Research and Written Analysis

An assignment may require identifying authorities addressing an issue before the court. The clerk must work within the assignment and relevant record. A useful analysis separates the parties' positions from the legal question under review.

For a hypothetical motion assignment, a clerk could prepare a short issue memorandum. It might identify relevant authorities, disputed points, and questions requiring further review. This example illustrates work organization rather than a mandatory judicial method.

The judge or chambers team establishes the expected format. Applicants should not assume every judge wants the same memo structure. Ask how assignments, feedback, and revisions operate in that chambers.

### Drafting and Chambers Support

Drafting can involve proposed opinions, orders, or other judicial documents. OSCAR also describes procedural communication and assistance during courtroom proceedings. Some judges assign administrative work as well.

That mix challenges another oversimplification: substantive work and administrative responsibilities do not occupy separate workplaces. A judicial clerk may perform both. The position announcement should explain the actual balance.

Read administrative duties carefully without treating them as disqualifying evidence. The more useful question asks how they fit the appointment. Confirm the research, writing, scheduling, and operational expectations together.

## Attorney-Office Clerks Work on a Different Side

Clerks working for lawyers support the office's representation or legal assignments. The employer's client responsibilities differ from a judge's role. Job advertisements should identify the intended work and supervision. Clerk roles sit inside a wider legal workforce, and [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/) show how many legal employers are small offices.

An original government example makes that difference concrete. The Southern District of California US Attorney's Office describes law-student clerk work involving motions and appellate briefs. It also describes evidence review and trial preparation. This is an attorney-office example, not a private law firm.

The example shows why law clerk does not always mean a postgraduate judicial appointment. It does not establish every employer's education requirements. Read each vacancy's eligibility criteria and application instructions.

When evaluating a law-firm role, ask the firm about its own assignments. Does it seek current students, graduates, or experienced lawyers? Which research, drafting, or case-support outputs will the person prepare?

Do not infer independent client authority from the title. Ask who reviews work and approves external communication. Any special permissions require verification under the applicable rules.

## Administrative Court Clerks Form a Separate Category

Administrative court work focuses on the court office's records and procedural operations. O*NET's court, municipal, and license-clerk profile includes filing documents, recording dispositions, and preparing dockets. It is a broader administrative classification, not a judicial-law-clerk salary category.

This distinction helps applicants search accurately. A vacancy involving records and public inquiries may require different preparation from a judge-facing research position. Neither description establishes the other's qualification standard.

Compare a vacancy using four questions.

- **Office:** Does the role sit in chambers, a legal team, or a clerk's office?
- **Output:** Does the employee prepare legal analysis or maintain procedural records?
- **Reviewer:** Who approves the work and answers substantive questions?
- **Eligibility:** What education, experience, or admission does the advertisement require?

Use the answers together. One research task in an administrative job does not establish a judicial appointment. Similarly, clerical duties inside chambers do not turn every law-clerk position into general court administration.

## Qualifications Depend on the Appointment

Qualifications vary with the position and appointment system. [OSCAR's federal qualification information](https://oscar.uscourts.gov/qualifications_salary_benefits) distinguishes education, postgraduate legal experience, and bar membership for salary placement. That federal framework does not govern all state or private-employer jobs.

For the described federal law-clerk grades, JSP-11 includes qualified graduates without legal work experience. JSP-12 and JSP-13 involve additional postgraduate experience and bar membership. The appointing judge determines placement within the applicable framework.

A specific vacancy can require more. A District of Utah career-clerk announcement requires a JD and bar membership. Treat those conditions as that vacancy's requirements, not a universal statement about everyone called clerk.

These sources also show why two common claims need correction. Not every clerk is an unlicensed new graduate. Not every role uses the same class-rank threshold or experience requirement.

Before applying, separate mandatory criteria from preferences. A preferred academic record differs from an absolute minimum. Ask the employer to clarify ambiguous wording instead of assuming flexibility.

## Term and Career Roles Need Different Questions

Appointment terms determine how long the role lasts and how employment conditions apply. OSCAR describes career, term, and temporary arrangements. Applicants should read the category identified by the actual employer.

A fixed-term appointment requires a conversation about its start, end, and any stated extension conditions. A career role requires a different discussion about continuing responsibilities. Do not assume clerkship means the same short period everywhere.

Ask these questions before comparing offers.

1. **Appointment type:** What category appears in the written announcement?
2. **Duration:** What start date, end date, or renewal condition applies?
3. **Work location:** What attendance or remote-work arrangements does the employer state?
4. **Benefits:** Which terms apply to that appointment rather than another category?
5. **Progression:** Does the employer describe changes in duties or salary placement?

Record the answers beside the vacancy. That keeps employment conditions connected to the role you are considering. Avoid applying one court's benefits description to another employer.

## Compare Occupational Wages with Advertised Pay Carefully

National wage data and individual job advertisements answer different questions. O*NET reports a $64,920 median annual wage for judicial law clerks in 2025. It identifies BLS as the wage-data source.

The same profile reports approximately 14,000 employees in that occupation in 2025. That figure concerns judicial law clerks, rather than every private-office position using clerk. Keep the occupation attached when citing it.

The Utah career-clerk vacancy lists $74,678–$125,776 across JSP-11 through JSP-14. Qualifications and experience affect placement. Its advertised range does not replace the national occupational median.

The comparison below keeps the measures separate.

| Pay Evidence | Verified Measure | What It Does Not Establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O*NET, citing BLS | $64,920 annual median, 2025 judicial-clerk occupation | A guaranteed federal starting salary |
| Utah career vacancy | $74,678–$125,776 advertised JSP-11–14 range | A national range for every clerk position |
| A different employer's offer | Terms supplied for that specific vacancy | Pay at another court or private office |

Do not calculate a promotion expectation by subtracting these figures. Their populations and purposes differ. For an actual offer, verify grade, step, locality, schedule, and benefits with the employer.

## A Hypothetical Assignment from Research to Review

Consider a fictional judge-facing research assignment involving a disputed procedural issue. The supervisor asks for an issue memorandum using identified materials. The example describes a possible workflow, not court procedure or legal advice.

The clerk first records the question and the scope of the materials. Next, the clerk identifies authorities and separates competing arguments. The memorandum flags an unresolved factual point instead of treating it as established.

The handoff identifies what the clerk reviewed and what remains uncertain. The judge receives analysis for consideration. The example does not imply that the clerk decides the issue or independently communicates a ruling.

That distinction also separates the role from [delegated paralegal work](/legal/what-does-a-paralegal-do/). Some research skills overlap, but the recipient, assignment, and employment framework differ. Compare those features rather than infer seniority from titles.

## Prepare Applications Around the Actual Announcement

A strong application follows the employer's instructions and supports the required qualifications. Research and writing experience should match the position's assignments. The question "what is a law clerk" provides a starting point. The vacancy supplies the criteria your application must address.

The Utah example requests a letter, resume, transcript, writing sample, and recommendations. Other employers may request a different package. Confirm the current announcement before preparing or submitting materials.

When choosing a sample, check permission and authorship requirements. Do not disclose confidential work or present someone else's editing as independent writing. Explain your contribution accurately where the employer permits context.

Use the interview to ask about supervision and revisions. How does a draft move through chambers or the legal team? What feedback helps a new employee meet the expected standard?

## Keep an Application Record for Each Employer

Prepare a separate checklist for every position you pursue. Record the announcement's date, required materials, submission method, and any stated deadline. Note whether the employer requests references directly or asks applicants to supply recommendation letters.

Then compare the writing-sample instructions with the material you can legitimately share. If a sample needs editing or permission, resolve that before submission. Keep a record of your own contribution and the version you sent.

During an interview, use the announcement to ask focused questions. Ask which assignments a new clerk receives first and how revisions work. Clarify anything the posting leaves unresolved about appointment length or attendance.

This proposed record helps keep multiple applications consistent. It does not replace the employer's instructions or establish a standard application package. Update the checklist when the employer supplies new information.

## Read the Role Before Comparing the Career

Answering "what is a law clerk" requires identifying the employer, supervisor, and work product. Check qualifications and appointment terms next. Only then compare compensation or career options. Clerkships also expose students to many fields, so a guide to the [types of lawyers and their practice areas](/legal/types-of-lawyers/) can help a clerk compare the paths that follow.

For the next vacancy you review, save the actual announcement. Write down its duties, eligibility criteria, and open questions. That record supports a more informed decision than a broad clerkship label.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Law Clerk Compared with a Court Clerk?

A judicial law clerk supports a judge's legal work. Administrative court clerks handle a separate set of records and procedural responsibilities.

### Must Every Law Clerk Have Passed the Bar?

No universal rule covers every position using the title. Check the specific appointment and employer requirements.

### Does Every Clerkship Last One Year?

No. Employers use different appointment arrangements, including term and career judicial positions.

### Does a Clerk Decide Cases?

A judicial clerk prepares analysis and drafts for the judge. The judge retains responsibility for the decision.
