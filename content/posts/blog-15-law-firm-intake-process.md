# Law Firm Intake: Close the Gaps Between First Contact and Attorney Review

An inquiry can receive a friendly response and still stall. Reception may believe an attorney owns the next step. The attorney may believe intake is collecting more information. Meanwhile, the person who asked for help hears nothing.

A reliable law firm intake process makes those handoffs visible. It states what each stage means, who owns the record, and what must happen before the inquiry advances.

This blog provides a proposed workflow, not a description of a consenting practice's operations. No private client files or firm-specific performance data were supplied. The examples are designed for adaptation and attorney approval.

## Key Takeaways

- **Assign One Current Owner:** Every open inquiry should have someone responsible for its next action.
- **Collect Information in Stages:** Ask only what is needed for the current decision.
- **Separate Contact From Acceptance:** A conversation, consultation, and signed engagement represent different stages.
- **Record the Exit:** Declines, withdrawals, duplicates, and unresolved inquiries need clear statuses.

## Draw the Workflow Before Choosing Software

Start with the real path an inquiry can take. Include telephone, website, email, referrals, and any other channel the practice uses.

Ask each team member what they do next and what information they expect from the prior stage. Differences between their answers reveal the handoff gaps.

For law firm intake, a diagram or table should include exceptions rather than only the ideal route. A caller may be unreachable, a conflict review may stop the process, or the practice may lack capacity.

Do not buy automation to accelerate an undefined workflow. A fast handoff to nobody is still a failed process.

The [client-acquisition reporting framework](/law-firms/law-firm-client-acquisition-statistics/) can help separate contact volume from later engagement outcomes.

## Define Stages With Observable Entry and Exit Rules

In law firm intake, a stage name should describe something the team can verify. "Warm lead" is difficult to audit unless the practice defines it.

The following table is a suggested starting model. It is not a legal determination of when representation begins.

| Stage | Observable Entry | Example Exit Condition | Suggested Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Received | Inquiry reaches an approved channel | Assigned for initial response | Intake coordinator |
| Initial contact | Staff attempts a suitable reply | Contact established or follow-up scheduled | Assigned intake staff |
| Preliminary review | Minimum required facts are available | Referred for approved screening | Intake lead |
| Attorney review | Responsible lawyer receives the record | Accept, decline, or request more information | Reviewing attorney |
| Engagement pending | Approved terms are sent | Agreement completed or opportunity closed | Designated engagement owner |
| Closed or transferred | Decision and next action recorded | Record retained under policy | Process owner |

The firm should adapt these stages to its work. A small practice may combine roles, but it should not leave ownership implicit.

## Collect Less at the First Contact

The first contact often needs a name, a safe contact route, and a broad description of the request. The actual information requirements depend on the practice and its approved screening process.

Avoid asking for a complete case history through an unrestricted website form. More information is not automatically better information.

Professional-conduct rules can create duties concerning prospective-client information. The practice must check the governing jurisdiction's rules and decide how those duties shape its process.

Writers and administrators should not invent a disclaimer and assume it resolves every intake issue. An attorney should approve the information request and the explanation shown to visitors.

Keep sensitive submissions out of ordinary marketing tools. A source field can support reporting without carrying a detailed legal narrative.

## Make the Reception-to-Attorney Handoff Explicit

A handoff needs a receiving owner, a complete record, and an expected next action. Sending an email is not proof that the receiving person accepted responsibility.

Use a visible queue or acknowledgment mechanism appropriate to the practice. The important feature is that unanswered handoffs remain discoverable.

### Specify What the Reviewer Needs

Create a short attorney-approved handoff standard. It might identify the requested service, relevant jurisdiction, safe contact method, and screening status.

Do not require staff to reach legal conclusions beyond their role. If a question needs attorney judgment, the record should say so.

A handoff should distinguish facts reported by the inquirer from facts independently verified. That separation reduces the chance that an early assumption becomes an accepted case detail.

### Specify What Happens When the Reviewer Is Unavailable

Name a backup and escalation route. Court attendance, leave, and workload should not make an inquiry disappear from the queue.

The backup arrangement does not need to promise an immediate legal assessment. It needs to preserve responsibility and communicate the next step accurately.

A deadline concern should follow the practice's approved escalation policy. Staff should not infer a legal deadline from a marketing checklist.

## Write Responses That Explain the Next Step

A useful initial reply acknowledges the inquiry and tells the person what happens next. It should reflect the firm's actual availability.

Clio's [2024 secret-shopper findings](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-powered-legal-practices-surge-clios-latest-legal-trends-report-reveals-major-shift-302268966.html) report that only 33% of 500 firms emailed responded, and 40% of firms answered calls. This historical study provides context, not a current performance estimate for every practice.

The operational lesson is narrower than a promise about conversion: a process needs to check whether a reply occurred at all.

Do not confuse an automated acknowledgment with a completed assessment. State whether a team member will contact the person and how the process works.

Avoid outcome promises or claims that the practice will accept the work. Lawyer-advertising rules restrict misleading service claims, and the governing jurisdiction's rules control.

## Create a Follow-Up Policy With an End Point

Follow-up should be consistent enough to audit and flexible enough to respect the person's preferences.

Decide which channels the practice may use, when another attempt is appropriate, and when the inquiry should close. Obtain any required permissions through an approved process.

Record the reason an inquiry closes. "No response," "not a service fit," and "declined after review" describe different outcomes.

Do not label every closed record as a sales failure. A responsible screening process should decline work the practice cannot appropriately accept.

The following checklist is a proposed operational control, rather than a universal contact schedule.

- **Contact Preference:** Use the safe route the person provided or approved.
- **Attempt Record:** Preserve the time, channel, and result of each attempt.
- **Next Action:** Set an owner and review date after an unsuccessful contact.
- **Closure Rule:** State when follow-up stops and how reopening is handled.
- **Escalation:** Route urgent or uncertain situations to the designated reviewer.

A clear end point prevents a stale queue from hiding the inquiries that still need attention.

## Evaluate Software Against the Handoffs

A law firm intake software demonstration often follows an ideal scenario. Test the exceptions before deciding whether the system supports the practice.

Use invented test records. Include a duplicate inquiry, a missing contact field, an unavailable reviewer, and an inquiry that closes before engagement.

Check permissions as well as screens. A receptionist, attorney, marketing contractor, and administrator may need different access.

The [MyCase workflow assessment](/legal-tech/mycase-legal-software/) and [Clio cost framework](/legal-tech/clio-legal-software-total-cost/) offer useful procurement context. The decision should still follow the firm's own process requirements.

Do not assume an integration transfers every field correctly. Verify the record after the handoff, including status, owner, source, and next action.

A successful test should produce an inspectable history without exposing unnecessary confidential information.

## Measure the Process Without Rewarding the Wrong Behavior

Choose measures that reveal gaps rather than encourage staff to close records prematurely.

A response-time measure should distinguish a receipt message from a useful reply. A conversion measure should use a defined cohort and denominator.

The following proposed scorecard keeps operational stages separate. It does not supply industry targets.

| Process Question | Suggested Measure | Review Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are inquiries assigned? | Unassigned open records | Exclude legitimate test records |
| Are replies happening? | Inquiries with recorded initial response | Separate automation and human contact |
| Are reviews stalling? | Age of attorney-review queue | Consider case complexity |
| Are records closing clearly? | Closed records with documented reason | Do not force an inaccurate reason |
| Is engagement progressing? | Pending agreements by age | Keep cohorts and dates consistent |

Review counts beside rates. A small practice may have too few inquiries for stable weekly percentages.

For law firm intake, the purpose of measurement is to locate a repairable process issue. It should not pressure staff to skip review.

## Separate Marketing Reporting From Restricted Intake Records

Marketing needs to know whether channels produce suitable inquiries. It rarely needs the personal narrative behind each request.

Use aggregate counts or approved identifiers. Keep access to identifying records restricted to the appropriate team.

Inspect integrations before enabling them. Google's [Analytics privacy guidance](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/6366371) warns against sending personally identifiable information. URLs and event parameters deserve particular attention.

A form may appear private while its fields enter analytics, email notifications, or external automation logs. Ask the technical owner to test the actual data path.

Do not use live client details in training screenshots or vendor demonstrations. Invented test records can reveal workflow defects without creating that exposure.

## Pilot the Workflow Before Rolling It Across the Practice

Choose a limited service or team and run the proposed law firm intake process under supervision. The pilot should test ownership, handoffs, and exceptions.

Before starting, define what counts as a successful record. It should have a current owner, a clear status, and a documented next action or closure.

Meet briefly with the people doing the work. Ask which fields they duplicate, where they wait, and which instructions remain unclear.

Revise the process before automating more of it. A redundant field or unclear approval can become harder to remove after several integrations depend on it.

For this work, the practical outcome is a repeatable process that the team can explain. Growth can then build on a workflow that preserves responsibility instead of adding more volume to an invisible queue.

## Handle Duplicate Inquiries Without Losing Their History

One person may telephone after submitting a form. Two staff members can then create separate records for the same request.

Define how the team identifies a likely duplicate and who may merge records. Preserve the original contact events so response timing remains interpretable.

Do not combine records solely because names look similar. Different people can share names, and one person can have separate requests.

The approved process should identify which record remains active and which owner takes responsibility. Any merged record should retain a traceable history.

This also affects reporting. Two contacts from one person are not automatically two prospective engagements. A stable counting rule prevents the funnel from growing merely because the same inquiry used another channel.

## Plan Reopening as Well as Closure

A closed inquiry can return later. The person may supply missing information, change their availability, or ask about a different service.

Decide when staff should reopen the original record and when a new record is appropriate. Attorney review may be needed if circumstances or screening requirements changed.

Keep the prior closure reason intact. Reopening should not erase the fact that the earlier process ended.

A clear reopening rule helps staff find context without assuming that an old approval remains current. It also prevents the same person from being treated as a wholly new marketing success each time they contact the office.

Give staff a short explanation of every required field. If nobody can explain how a field supports screening, routing, engagement, or required recordkeeping, review whether it belongs in the first step. Removing unnecessary collection can make the process clearer while reducing the amount of sensitive information passed between systems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Should a Law Firm Intake Process Include?

It should define receipt, response, screening, attorney review, engagement, follow-up, and closure, with owners and transition rules appropriate to the practice.

### Does an Inquiry Automatically Become a Client?

Do not use that assumption operationally. An attorney should define the engagement process under the applicable rules and circumstances.

### Should the First Form Request Every Detail?

Only request information needed for the approved initial step. More sensitive information may need a later, controlled channel.

### Can One Person Own Several Stages?

Yes. A small practice may combine roles, provided responsibility and backup arrangements remain clear.

### What Should Happen to Unanswered Inquiries?

Keep them visible with a next action, owner, and closure rule. Do not let an acknowledgment message hide the lack of substantive follow-up.
