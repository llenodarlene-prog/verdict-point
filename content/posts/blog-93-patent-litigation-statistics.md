# Patent Litigation Statistics: Court Filings and PTAB Activity

US district courts received 4,075 patent cases in fiscal year 2025, up 758 cases or 23% from 2024, according to the Administrative Office of the US Courts. At the same time, the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) instituted fewer challenges. Its petition-level institution rate fell from 68% in fiscal 2024 to 50% in fiscal 2025 and 41% in fiscal 2026.

Those two trends point in different directions, and both matter. Patent litigation statistics cover more than lawsuits. They also include administrative challenges at the US Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) and appeals to the Federal Circuit. Each forum counts different things over different periods.

This page keeps the forums separate and names the period behind each figure. We checked every number against the court and USPTO source pages on November 12, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **Court Filings Rose:** District courts received 4,075 patent cases in fiscal 2025, up 23%.
- **Broader IP Growth:** Intellectual property cases rose 16% to 17,234, with trademark cases up 25%.
- **PTAB Institution Fell:** The petition-level institution rate dropped from 68% in fiscal 2024 to 41% in fiscal 2026.
- **Fewer USPTO Appeals:** Federal Circuit appeals from the USPTO fell 26% to 368 in the year ending March 31, 2025.
- **Periods Differ:** Court data use fiscal years or March year-ends; PTAB uses USPTO fiscal years.

## How Many Patent Lawsuits Are Filed in Federal Court?

The judiciary's [Judicial Business 2025 report for the district courts](https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/reports/statistical-reports/judicial-business-united-states-courts/judicial-business-2025/us-district-courts-judicial-business-2025) gives the main fiscal-year count. Patent cases went up 23%, an increase of 758 cases, to 4,075.

The same report puts patents inside a larger category. Intellectual property rights cases rose 16%, up 2,362 cases to 17,234, as trademark cases increased 25%. The Northern District of Illinois had the largest numeric growth in trademark filings, a rise of 599 cases to 1,555.

| District Court Measure | Fiscal Year 2025 | Change From 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent cases | 4,075 | +758, or +23% |
| All intellectual property cases | 17,234 | +2,362, or +16% |
| Trademark cases | Not stated here | +25% |

Patent cases were about 24% of all IP filings that year, by our calculation (4,075 divided by 17,234).

## The March Year-End Series

The judiciary also publishes [Federal Judicial Caseload Statistics](https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/reports/statistical-reports/federal-judicial-caseload-statistics/federal-judicial-caseload-statistics-2025), which compare the year ending March 31, 2025, with the same period a year earlier. That series shows the same direction with different numbers.

| Caseload Measure, Year Ending March 31, 2025 | Change | Total |
|---|---:|---:|
| Intellectual property filings | +1,266, or +9% | Not stated here |
| Patent cases | +722, or +24% | Not stated here |
| Trademark cases | +235, or +7% | 3,656 |
| Patent cases on abbreviated new drug applications | +64, or +26% | 311 |

These two series overlap but end six months apart. That is why patent filings rose 24% in one and 23% in the other. Neither number is wrong. Always name the period when you quote patent litigation statistics, because "patent cases rose in 2025" could mean either series.

Abbreviated new drug application (ANDA) cases deserve a note. They are disputes in which brand-name drug makers sue generic makers seeking approval. They follow their own business cycle, tied to drug patent expirations.

The March series also shows how fast IP work grew overall. Intellectual property filings rose by 1,266 cases, and patents made up 722 of that increase, by far the largest share. Trademark cases added 235, and the remaining 309 came from the other IP categories the report tracks, by our subtraction. For anyone tracking demand for IP lawyers, that mix matters as much as the headline total.

## What PTAB Institution Statistics Mean

The PTAB offers another route to challenge a patent. A company can file a petition asking the Board to review whether certain claims should have been granted. The Board first decides whether to institute a trial.

The USPTO's [PTAB trial statistics for October 2026](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Trial_Stats_October_2026.pdf) show institution decisions by petition across five fiscal years.

| USPTO Fiscal Year | Instituted | Denied | Institution Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 752 | 375 | 67% |
| 2024 | 740 | 347 | 68% |
| 2025 | 616 | 619 | 50% |
| 2026 | 379 | 552 | 41% |
| 2027 through October | 18 | 6 | 75% |

The drop is steep. In fiscal 2024, about two of every three decided petitions went to trial. By fiscal 2026, fewer than half did. The number of decisions also fell, from 1,087 in fiscal 2024 to 931 in fiscal 2026, by our arithmetic.

The fiscal 2027 row covers only October 2026, with 24 decisions. It should not be read as a reversal of the trend.

## Petitions Filed and Technology Mix

The same report shows what kinds of petitions arrived in October 2026, the first month of fiscal 2027. There were 33 petitions in total.

| Measure, October 2026 | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inter partes review (IPR) petitions | 27 | 82% |
| Post-grant review (PGR) petitions | 6 | 18% |
| Electrical and computer | 14 | 42% |
| Mechanical and business methods | 13 | 39% |
| Chemical | 5 | 15% |
| Bio and pharma | 1 | 3% |

One month is a small sample, so treat these shares as a snapshot. The USPTO's monthly chart also shows that IPR filings varied widely across fiscal 2026, from 15 to 90 petitions in a month.

The report adds detail on how decisions are made. For October 2026, 26 petitions went through Director discretionary review: 21 were referred on to the merits and 5 were denied. Of 19 decided on merits or other grounds, 18 were granted.

## Institution Is Not Invalidation

An institution decision only starts a PTAB trial. It does not decide whether any claim is valid. A final written decision comes later and can uphold some claims and cancel others.

So a falling institution rate means fewer patents are being sent to trial. It does not mean more patents are being upheld after a full review. Those are separate outcomes with separate statistics.

The USPTO also notes that it revised its by-patent institution rate so that it counts only decisions made in the current fiscal year. Rates by petition and by patent use different denominators. One patent can face several petitions, so the two rates can differ.

## Federal Circuit Appeals From the USPTO

Appeals of USPTO decisions go to the US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit. In the year ending March 31, 2025, filings involving the USPTO had the largest numeric decrease of any source, falling 126 appeals, or 26%, to 368. Total Federal Circuit filings held steady at 1,459, up less than 1%.

The fiscal-year series tells a similar story. Federal Circuit filings dropped 13% to 1,270 in 2025, and appeals from USPTO decisions were the largest group, at 349 cases. Appeals from district courts followed at 309.

Do not combine the 1,459 and 1,270 totals. They cover different 12-month periods.

## Courts and PTAB Moved in Opposite Directions

Put the two main series side by side and a clear pattern appears. District court patent filings rose in fiscal 2025. PTAB institution fell over the same years.

| Indicator | Earlier Period | Later Period | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| District court patent cases | 3,317 in FY2024 | 4,075 in FY2025 | Up 23% |
| PTAB institution rate by petition | 68% in FY2024 | 41% in FY2026 | Down 27 points |
| Federal Circuit appeals from the USPTO | 494 a year earlier | 368, year to March 2025 | Down 26% |

The 3,317 and 494 figures are our subtractions from the stated changes (4,075 minus 758, and 368 plus 126). Treat them as derived values and cite the source pages for the official counts.

These patent litigation statistics do not prove cause and effect. A lower chance of getting a PTAB trial could make district court the more attractive forum for some disputes. Other factors could explain the rise just as well, such as new products, funding for patent suits, or a few busy filers. The data show the two trends together. They do not show why.

What the comparison does show is that no single number describes patent disputes. A reader who sees only the court count would think patent challenges were rising everywhere. A reader who sees only the PTAB rate would think the opposite. Good patent litigation statistics report both.

## How the Stages of a Patent Dispute Fit Together

Patent disputes can pass through several forums, and each stage produces its own count:

1. **Complaint:** A district court case begins civil litigation.
2. **Petition:** A PTAB petition asks for administrative review of claims.
3. **Institution:** The Board decides whether to hold a trial.
4. **Final Decision:** A final written decision resolves the instituted claims.
5. **Appeal:** The Federal Circuit reviews qualifying decisions.

One dispute can appear at several stages. A defendant sued in district court may also file a PTAB petition. Adding court cases and petitions together would count that dispute twice.

The timing also differs by stage. A PTAB petition filed in one fiscal year may get its institution decision in the next. A district court case filed in 2025 may not reach judgment for years. So a single year of patent litigation statistics mixes disputes that started at very different times.

For trend work, pick one stage and one series, then follow it over several years. Switching between stages from year to year produces a trend line that measures nothing consistent.

## Why Filing Counts Do Not Measure Patent Quality

A rise in lawsuits can reflect enforcement strategy, new products, litigation funding, or venue choices. It does not prove that patent quality rose or fell.

A falling institution rate can reflect changes in the petitions filed, in discretionary practice, or in legal standards. The rate alone cannot identify the cause.

Damages are also poorly summed up by one average. A few large verdicts can dominate the mean, and awards can be cut or overturned on appeal. Any damages figure needs a defined set of cases and an outcome date.

## How to Read Patent Litigation Statistics

Before you quote any patent litigation statistics, check four things:

- **Forum:** District court, PTAB, or Federal Circuit.
- **Period:** Fiscal year, March year-end, or USPTO fiscal year.
- **Unit:** Cases, petitions, decisions, or appeals.
- **Status:** Final year or year to date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Many Patent Lawsuits Were Filed in 2025?

US district courts recorded 4,075 patent cases in fiscal year 2025, up 758 from the year before.

### Did Patent Litigation Increase in 2025?

Yes. Patent cases rose 23% in the fiscal-year series and 24% in the year ending March 31, 2025.

### What Was the PTAB Institution Rate in 2026?

The USPTO's petition-level rate was 41% for fiscal 2026, with 379 petitions instituted and 552 denied.

### Does Institution Mean a Patent Is Invalid?

No. Institution only starts a trial. A final written decision later decides the challenged claims.

### Where Do Appeals From the USPTO Go?

They go to the US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, which received 368 such appeals in the year ending March 31, 2025.
