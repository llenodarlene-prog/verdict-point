# General Counsel Roles and Responsibilities

A general counsel leads an organization's legal function and advises its leadership on legal matters. The position combines legal judgment with responsibility for how the organization obtains and manages legal support. Its actual authority depends on the employer's structure and assigned duties.

The title alone cannot tell you whether the lawyer manages compliance, acts as corporate secretary, or supervises other lawyers. Those details belong in the role description and governance arrangements. They matter for both hiring decisions and everyday work with the legal department.

For a US business, start with the organization's needs and the identity of the client. Then define the reporting line, decision authority, and resources. The framework below helps leaders ask those questions without inventing a universal job specification.

## Key Takeaways

- **Client Identity:** Organizational representation concerns the organization, subject to the applicable professional rules and engagement arrangements.
- **Role Scope:** Identify which legal work the leader handles personally, delegates, or obtains from outside specialists.
- **Decision Authority:** Separate legal advice from authority to approve spending, contracts, settlements, or other business decisions.
- **Reporting Access:** Define how significant issues reach leadership and the appropriate governing body.

## What the Legal Leader Leads

The legal function includes both legal work and decisions about delivering that work. The [Association of Corporate Counsel's role template](https://jobline.acc.com/career/general-counsel/job-descriptions) describes strategic advice to management on contracts, compliance, and risk, along with managing and leading the legal team. Treat that template as a starting point, not every employer's actual specification.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes in-house counsel as lawyers serving a single organization. It identifies advice on business activities, including contracts and patents. Managing those agreements after signature is its own process, covered in [contract lifecycle management](/legal-business/contract-lifecycle-management/). That broad description does not assign every legal issue to one individual.

When assessing a particular role, ask where responsibility starts and ends. Does the lawyer negotiate significant agreements personally? Do specialist lawyers handle employment, litigation, or regulatory work? Who coordinates their advice and communicates the decision leadership needs?

Record those answers before comparing candidates or reorganizing the team. A broad title with a narrow assignment differs from responsibility for an entire legal department. Neither arrangement becomes clear through title alone.

## Define Responsibilities Through Decisions and Records

A useful role description names the work, the expected contribution, and the authority requiring clarification. This helps colleagues distinguish obtaining legal advice from obtaining business approval. It also identifies where another function must participate.

The following matrix is an editorial planning framework. It proposes questions and records, rather than mandatory duties or a standard governance model.

| Work Area | Legal Leadership Contribution to Define | Authority or Record to Clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial agreements | Identify legal issues and coordinate negotiation support | Business owner, signing authority, exception record |
| Governance | Support the governing body's legal work | Meeting responsibilities and escalation route |
| Compliance | Define legal advice and any assigned program oversight | Program owner, reporting line, review process |
| Disputes | Coordinate legal strategy and outside specialists | Settlement authority, deadlines, reporting expectations |
| Legal resources | Organize internal work and external advice | Hiring authority, budget approval, engagement scope |
| Significant projects | Assess legal implications before commitment | Decision owner, unresolved issues, approval record |

Use each row to start a discussion with the responsible leaders. For example, identify who can sign a supplier agreement after legal review. Do not assume that reviewing the document also grants authority to commit the company.

Likewise, distinguish responsibility for legal advice on compliance from ownership of every operational control. Ask who performs the control, monitors exceptions, and reports problems. A vague assignment to ensure compliance leaves those practical questions unanswered.

## Identify the Client Before Addressing Conflicts

[ABA Model Rule 1.13(a)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_13_organization_as_client/) identifies the organization as the client, acting through authorized constituents. Paragraph (f) addresses clarifying that identity when interests are adverse. Paragraph (g) makes any additional representation of individuals subject to conflict rules.

These are model provisions. The applicable jurisdiction's rules and the actual representation require separate assessment. Working closely with an executive does not, by itself, establish that executive's personal representation.

For employees and managers, the practical question is straightforward: whom does the lawyer represent in this matter? Ask that question before treating the company's legal department as personal counsel. If individual advice is needed, clarify the arrangement with an appropriate lawyer.

For leadership teams, make the same distinction visible in investigations and sensitive discussions. Establish who will explain the representation and handle potential conflicts. Avoid allowing a familiar working relationship to supply an answer that nobody has confirmed.

## Compare Titles Without Assuming a Fixed Hierarchy

General counsel and chief legal officer can describe overlapping leadership roles. Taylor Root's [comparison of the two titles](https://www.taylorroot.com/career-advice/what-is-the-difference-between-a-general-counsel-and-chief-legal-officer/) acknowledges the absence of a universally agreed separation. Some employers distinguish their scope, while others use the titles interchangeably.

Ask for the organization chart and written assignment before deciding that one title outranks another. Examine which position leads the function, attends leadership discussions, and has access to the governing body. Then compare the responsibilities with the resources available.

The same caution applies to deputy, associate, and senior counsel positions. Ask which team, region, entity, or specialty the position serves. Do not infer a complete career ladder from the wording on a business card.

Broader employment and lawyer pay context appears in the [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/). For compensation comparisons, establish scope before selecting a benchmark. Comparing [corporate legal salary evidence by role and employer](/legal/corporate-lawyer-salary/) also requires consistent pay definitions. A leadership cash-compensation figure does not describe every in-house lawyer's base salary.

## What Reporting Research Can and Cannot Tell You

Reporting lines reveal one aspect of a role's position within an organization. They do not, by themselves, establish its complete authority or the quality of its advice. Use survey findings as context while examining the employer's actual arrangements.

The 2026 ACC Chief Legal Officers Survey, run with FTI Consulting, draws on 1,049 chief legal officers across 43 countries. It is a global survey of legal leaders, not a US-only census of legal departments. Treat its findings on reporting lines as context rather than a required structure.

For a hiring discussion, ask about the proposed reporting line and access arrangements directly. Who receives regular updates? How can the lawyer raise an urgent concern? What happens when the issue involves the usual reporting manager?

Those answers describe the job more precisely than assuming a typical arrangement applies. Put them in the mandate before the position starts.

## Preserve Independent Advice and an Escalation Route

Independent judgment is part of the job. A general counsel gives candid advice, and business context matters without replacing professional judgment.

Paragraph (b) of Model Rule 1.13 addresses specified organizational misconduct and substantial-injury conditions. It generally requires appropriate internal escalation, subject to the provision's qualifications. This is more specific than a rule to report every concern to the board.

Ask the responsible lawyer to explain the applicable rules and internal escalation arrangements. Do not turn that discussion into a blanket instruction to disclose information outside the organization. Different provisions and exceptions govern those questions.

A practical issue note can help leadership understand a decision without burying it in legal terminology. As a recommended format, identify the proposed action, material legal issue, available alternatives, and unresolved facts. State who needs to decide and when.

Keep the legal recommendation visible even when the business chooses among permissible alternatives. If the facts change, request updated advice. A prior assessment of one proposal should not silently become approval of a different transaction.

## Manage the Team and Outside Advice Deliberately

Leading a legal function includes deciding how to obtain the expertise the organization needs. Spencer Stuart's legal leadership analysis names strong and tested judgment as the first attribute companies want in a general counsel.

Translate those responsibilities into a workable assignment. Identify which matters internal lawyers will handle and when external expertise requires approval. Set expectations for reporting, budgets, and changes to scope before the work expands.

For each outside engagement, ask for the following practical details.

- **Assignment:** Describe the issue, expected deliverable, and work that falls outside the current scope.
- **Responsibility:** Name the internal lawyer coordinating instructions and the business owner providing necessary facts.
- **Financial Terms:** Record the fee basis, budget assumptions, expense treatment, and process for approving changes.
- **Review Points:** Agree when the adviser should report progress, uncertainty, and decisions requiring internal attention.

Do not measure legal leadership solely by how much work stays inside the company. Ask whether the chosen resources match the issue and available capacity. Document the reasoning behind significant staffing or adviser decisions.

For a small team, identify backup arrangements and urgent coverage. For a larger department, clarify delegation and supervision across specialties. Treat those as design questions, without assuming company size dictates a single structure.

## Write a Hiring Brief Around the Actual Need

Before advertising, describe the work leadership needs the position to perform. Replace broad phrases about protecting the company with defined assignments and boundaries. Candidates can then assess the role against the authority and support offered.

Use the following questions to build the brief.

- **Business Priorities:** Which planned activities or unresolved matters need sustained legal leadership?
- **Required Experience:** Which specialties and industry knowledge connect directly with those assignments?
- **Authority:** Which decisions can the position make, recommend, or escalate?
- **Resources:** What team, outside-adviser budget, systems, and administrative support will be available?
- **Access:** Which executives and governing-body representatives will receive advice and significant issue reports?

Ask candidates for evidence tied to those needs. Request an explanation of a difficult decision, available alternatives, and their own contribution. Separate team outcomes from work the candidate personally led.

Protect confidential information during that discussion. Ask candidates to describe methods without revealing another client's sensitive details. Evaluate whether they explain uncertainty and limits, rather than rewarding unsupported claims of eliminating every risk.

Also resolve practical working arrangements before making an offer. Identify entities served, locations involved, and expectations for travel or availability. Have qualified advisers check the relevant practice and employment requirements for the actual assignment.

## Agree on Early Work and Review Measures

A new general counsel needs a clear initial mandate, with priorities leadership can review. The following 90-day sequence is illustrative editorial guidance. It is not survey evidence, a legal requirement, or a promised timetable for every business.

Adapt the sequence to existing deadlines and the organization's actual circumstances.

- **First 30 Days:** Meet key leaders, confirm the mandate, and identify urgent matters and missing records.
- **Days 31 Through 60:** Propose responsibilities, escalation routes, and priorities for internal and outside legal work.
- **Days 61 Through 90:** Review progress with leadership and agree the next resource and process decisions.

For each priority, define what completion means. A reviewed contract process might require named approvers and documented exceptions. A matter inventory might require an owner, next deadline, and current status for each entry.

Choose review measures that connect with those assignments. Consider whether urgent matters have owners, whether advice reaches decision-makers, and whether budgets explain significant changes. Establish a baseline before claiming improvement.

Avoid treating fewer reported concerns as automatic evidence of lower risk. Ask whether reporting changed, whether the underlying work changed, and what supporting records show. Keep measured observations separate from assumptions about the legal team's effect.

Before approving the position, agree its mandate, authority, resources, and review process in writing. Use that document when evaluating candidates and working with the eventual hire. Revisit it when the business or assignment changes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the Company's Lawyer Personally Represent Every Executive?

Organizational representation does not automatically establish personal representation. Clarify client identity and any separate arrangement under the applicable rules.

### Must the Top Lawyer Also Manage Compliance?

The assignment requires confirmation at the particular employer. Compliance oversight varies by employer, so it is not a universal obligation.

### Is Chief Legal Officer Always a Higher Position?

No universal title hierarchy places the chief legal officer above the general counsel. Compare the actual responsibilities, reporting line, and authority.

### Does Legal Review Give Someone Authority to Sign?

Do not assume signing authority from document review alone. Check the organization's applicable approvals and delegation arrangements.

### Must a General Counsel Cover Every Specialty Personally?

Define the required work and access to additional expertise. The role's leadership responsibilities include organizing suitable support for its mandate.
