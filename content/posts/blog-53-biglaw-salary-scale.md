# BigLaw Salary Scale by Associate Year

The BigLaw salary scale needs an employer name and an effective date to make sense. Milbank's June 2026 memo announced annual base salaries from $235,000 to $455,000, effective July 1. Other published employer schedules show different amounts.

A class-year table answers one question: what annual base rate does that schedule assign? It does not establish everyone's bonus, calendar-year earnings, or future promotion. Those details need separate evidence.

The comparison below preserves each employer's dates and class labels. It also explains how to check an offer without combining unrelated compensation figures.

## Key Takeaways

- **Employer Scope:** Milbank's published memo and Ropes & Gray's recruiting disclosure contain different base schedules.
- **Effective Dates:** A midyear annual rate does not automatically describe twelve months of actual earnings.
- **Class Mapping:** Match the employer's class-year designation before comparing individual salary rows.
- **Compensation Scope:** Check bonuses, benefits, and any eligibility conditions separately from annual base salary.

## How Associate Years Match the Table

Start with the class designation the employer uses. Ropes & Gray labels the 2025 class first year and the 2024 class second year. That mapping belongs to its dated schedule. Moving the same labels into a later calendar year without checking would create confusion.

### Class Year and Time with the Employer

An associate's hiring date alone does not identify the correct row. A lateral candidate should ask which compensation class the offer assigns. Then compare that designation with the firm's current schedule.

Keep the question specific: which class controls base pay on the start date? Ask separately when the next compensation review occurs. This avoids treating an informal description, such as midlevel associate, as a precise salary classification.

Record any recognized clerkship or prior-service credit in writing if the employer offers it. Do not assume that another firm's classification carries over unchanged. The comparison should follow the actual offer terms.

### Associate Pay and Other Lawyer Roles

Milbank's memo treats special associates and counsel separately, with individual determinations. Its associate rows therefore cannot establish those employees' rates. A counsel offer needs its own compensation evidence.

Likewise, this table supplies no partner compensation figures. Comparing an associate's base salary with a partner profit measure would require different definitions. Keep role, payment type, and period consistent before judging the difference.

For recruiting teams, preserve the employer's exact role label in the comparison file. A broad label such as senior lawyer can conceal different employment categories. Use the detailed title and written pay terms instead.

## Effective Dates Change Calendar Year Earnings

An annual base rate describes a rate of pay. Actual earnings also depend on the period during which that rate applies. Milbank's memo makes this distinction important because its increase takes effect halfway through 2026.

The BigLaw salary scale should therefore remain separate from a calendar-year earnings calculation. A table can show the correct annual rate while overstating actual earnings if readers assume January effectiveness.

### A Calculation Using the Published Rates

Consider only the two published rates for Milbank's 2025 class. The memo lists $225,000 before the increase and $235,000 afterward. A simple half-year calculation gives $230,000 in base pay for the full calendar year.

The arithmetic is $225,000 multiplied by one-half, plus $235,000 multiplied by one-half. This calculation assumes continuous full-time employment and exactly half a year at each rate. It excludes bonuses, leave adjustments, and payroll-specific timing.

Treat $230,000 as that calculation's result, not a verified individual's earnings. The assumptions are essential. Someone starting later, changing schedules, or receiving another adjustment needs a different calculation.

### Annualized Rates and Payroll Records

For a real comparison, split the year whenever the applicable rate changes. Use the employer's payroll method for partial periods. Add the resulting base amounts only after checking employment dates and working arrangements.

Do not divide by twelve and describe the result as take-home pay. That produces a monthly gross equivalent under a simplified annualization. Taxes, deductions, payment frequency, and benefits require separate information.

Keep the supporting memo beside the calculation. A dated source makes it easier to distinguish a compensation change from an arithmetic error. It also shows whether a later announcement requires revision.

## Base Salary Does Not Establish Bonus Income

A base-pay announcement cannot prove a bonus amount or entitlement. Milbank's June memo sets base salaries. It does not supply a year-end bonus schedule. Adding an earlier year's bonus would introduce another period and another set of terms.

Build a compensation comparison from separate components. Preserve the evidence supporting each one before adding any totals.

- **Base Salary:** Record the applicable annual rate, start date, and any change during the year.
- **Year-end Award:** Identify the award year and obtain the employer's eligibility and payment terms.
- **Special Payment:** Keep a separate bonus or signing payment distinct from recurring base compensation.
- **Benefits:** Review the actual plan documents and employee costs rather than estimating their value from salary.

Ask whether the bonus terms address hours, performance, start dates, or continued employment. These are questions to resolve, not conditions to assume every firm imposes.

An unsigned projection should stay outside a confirmed compensation total. If an offer guarantees a payment, retain the written guarantee and its conditions. If the employer describes it as discretionary, preserve that wording.

This approach also prevents comparisons between different payment years. A bonus earned in one year and paid in another needs clear labeling. Choose the comparison period first, then apply it consistently.

## How the Scale Differs from Survey Medians

A named employer's salary schedule and a survey median answer different questions. The schedule reports that employer's published rates. The median summarizes observations within the survey's participating group. Neither should replace the other's definition.

The [National Association for Law Placement's 2025 analysis](https://www.nalp.org/0625research) illustrates the difference. It reported a $200,000 median first-year associate base salary as of January 1, 2025. Approximately 87% of respondents came from offices in firms exceeding 250 lawyers.

That is historical survey context. It predates Milbank's July 2026 increase and does not measure later adoption. It also does not describe a representative sample of every U.S. legal employer. Firms with 500 or more employees are a small share of all law firms, as the [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/) show.

Use the BigLaw salary scale for a matching employer and compensation class. Use a survey only after checking its respondent group and reference date. A national lawyer wage series would introduce a broader occupation again, and in-house pay follows other measures covered in [corporate lawyer salary by role and employer](/legal/corporate-lawyer-salary/).

For an associate comparing offers, the written offer remains the more direct evidence. For a compensation committee, the survey can add context. Keep the two sources visible rather than averaging their figures into a new benchmark.

## Questions to Resolve Before Comparing Offers

Two offers need matching definitions before their amounts become comparable. Start with written terms, then identify any missing information. Avoid filling gaps with assumptions about what market pay usually means.

Use the following sequence when preparing an offer comparison.

- **Confirm the Role and Class:** Record the formal title and the compensation class the employer assigns.
- **Identify the Office and Schedule:** Ask which location or employment arrangement the quoted rate covers.
- **Check the Effective Date:** Establish the rate on your start date and any announced later change.
- **Separate Variable Payments:** Record each bonus independently, including its year and written eligibility terms.
- **Review the Full Package:** Compare employee benefit costs, leave terms, and any repayment obligations in the documents.

Do not convert unanswered questions into zeroes. An unknown bonus is not necessarily a zero bonus. An undocumented benefit is not a confirmed benefit. Mark each item unresolved until the employer answers.

Bring conflicting documents back to recruiting or human resources. Ask which document governs the offer and request a written correction where necessary. A screenshot from another website cannot resolve contradictory employer terms.

The same discipline helps existing associates review an announcement. Check whether the memo covers your role and class. Then confirm its application through the employer's compensation or payroll team.

## Compare the Same Class Before Assessing a Difference

A salary difference needs a consistent comparison before it supports a decision. Use the same class row and distinguish annual rates from actual earnings. The two employer columns make that exercise possible without estimating anyone's bonus.

For the 2025 class, subtracting $225,000 from $235,000 gives a $10,000 annual-rate difference. For the 2021 class, subtracting $365,000 from $385,000 gives $20,000. These calculations compare the published schedules above, not matched employees' total compensation.

That distinction becomes especially important for a move during the year. An annual-rate difference does not establish the additional dollars someone receives after joining. The start date and actual payroll periods determine the applicable portion.

Keep a separate column for any payment the new employer confirms in writing. Leave an unconfirmed payment unresolved. Do not use an assumed bonus to cancel a known base-pay difference or inflate one offer's total.

Also compare the time periods attached to benefits and review dates. Ask when benefit coverage starts and when the next salary review takes place. Read any signing-payment repayment provision before treating that payment as unrestricted income.

The goal is a comparison someone else can reproduce from the documents. If a colleague cannot trace a number to a source or calculation, clarify it. This protects the distinction between an employer's stated compensation and your own planning assumptions.

## Keep the Comparison Useful After a Pay Change

A reliable pay reference should retain the evidence behind each revision. Changing a heading's year without checking its amounts creates false freshness. Update the schedule only when a dated source supports the change.

For readers maintaining their own comparison, keep a short record with each employer. This makes later changes easier to trace.

- **Source:** Save the employer disclosure or original memorandum and its announcement date.
- **Coverage:** Note the role, class, geography, and effective date shown in that document.
- **Change:** Record whether the revision affects base rates, bonuses, or another compensation component.
- **Open Question:** Identify any conflicting or incomplete terms requiring confirmation from the employer.

Retain an earlier schedule when calculating earnings across a raise. Replacing every old amount with the latest rate would erase information needed for that calculation. Historical rates and current rates serve different purposes.

The BigLaw salary scale becomes useful when readers can trace its figures and limits. Before relying on a number, match it to the employer, class, and payment period. Resolve the remaining terms with the employer before accepting a compensation comparison as complete.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Every Large Law Firm Pay the Same Associate Salary?

No single biglaw salary scale binds every firm. The two employer documents above show different schedules. Confirm the applicable rate with the individual firm.

### Does the Table Include Bonuses?

No, this biglaw salary scale comparison shows annual base salary only. Bonus amounts and eligibility require separate employer documentation.

### Does a July Raise Apply to the Entire Calendar Year?

Milbank's cited memo specifies July 1, 2026 effectiveness. It does not make that rate effective from January.

### Can a Lateral Associate Use Graduation Year Alone?

Ask the hiring firm to confirm the compensation class assigned in the offer. Use that designation when reading any biglaw salary scale.

### Does the Highest Associate Row Establish Counsel Pay?

Milbank's memo says it determines special associate and counsel increases individually. Its associate table cannot establish those individual rates.
