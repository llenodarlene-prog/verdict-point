# 7 Free Legal AI Tools: Uses, Limits, and Free-Plan Rules

Free legal AI can explain a legal term, summarize a document, draft a starting point, or help frame research. It cannot make an output reliable merely because it sounds precise. Users still need to confirm the governing jurisdiction, source, date, facts, and professional review required for the task.

This article compares seven tools with an ongoing free plan or a defined free function. It excludes products that offer only a short trial. We checked the vendors' public pages on October 25, 2026. We did not test their answers, accuracy, or privacy controls.

## Key Takeaways

- **Free Means Different Things:** A plan, a daily allowance, a preview, and a no-cost document review are not equivalent.
- **Jurisdiction Matters:** A plausible answer can cite law that does not govern the user's situation.
- **Sources Need Inspection:** Open the cited authority and check the proposition, court, date, and status.
- **Confidentiality Comes First:** Do not enter client information until authorized users approve the vendor and workflow.
- **A Tool Is Not Counsel:** Vendor disclaimers commonly describe outputs as information rather than legal advice.

## How We Classified Free Access

The comparison uses three labels. We counted free legal AI only when the free access had no stated end date or covered a defined function.

- **Ongoing Free Plan:** The vendor publishes a $0 tier without a stated end date.
- **Free Function:** A named task can be completed without payment, although other tasks cost money.
- **Free Preview:** The user can generate or inspect an output, but a usable download or another step requires payment.

This distinction matters because "try free" can mean a time-limited trial. Paxton, for example, advertises a seven-day trial and a paid professional plan. It does not qualify for the list below.

## 1. LawConnect

LawConnect offers free legal navigation. A user explains a situation in ordinary language, receives legal-specific information and possible next steps, and can choose to connect with a lawyer. The site's FAQ says its AI legal assistant is completely free to use.

This structure may help a consumer organize an unfamiliar issue before contacting counsel. It can also create a summary for a later conversation. Its geographic pages differ, so select the correct country before relying on the stated process.

The site says its content is informational and should not replace legal advice. Treat its response as an intake aid. Verify deadlines, filing requirements, and legal conclusions with the correct authority or a qualified lawyer.

## 2. Justee

Justee provides a free document-review function without signup. It accepts PDF, Word, or pasted text up to 10 MB. The site says the review covers any US state's law or the law of England and Wales.

The tool may help a freelancer, business owner, or legal professional create a first list of contract questions. It can identify clauses for closer reading and explain why a provision may deserve attention. The result should not decide whether a user signs or rejects an agreement.

Justee says documents receive encryption and that it does not train AI on user data. It also reserves "Full Private Mode" for paid plans. That difference deserves review before anyone uploads sensitive material. Read the current privacy policy and terms, identify subprocessors, and obtain firm approval for client data.

## 3. GenieAI

GenieAI's pricing page lists a $0 plan without a credit card. It includes document creation, Q&A, review and editing, more than 500 templates, a DOCX editor, and PDF import.

This free legal AI option focuses on contracts. It may help a user structure a first draft, compare wording, or ask questions about an uploaded agreement. Its templates and editor can be useful when the task is defined and the user already knows the governing jurisdiction.

The vendor states that documents are encrypted and that it does not train models on contracts. Even with those statements, a legal team should review the operative terms and security material. Confirm retention, deletion, access, and any restrictions that differ between the free and business plans.

For every proposed clause, check the legal effect and consistency with the rest of the agreement. Contract language can be internally coherent and still fail to match the parties' deal.

## 4. TheLawGPT

TheLawGPT publishes a "free forever" plan at $0 per seat. It allows three legal questions per day, file uploads up to 1 MB, and access to case-law search. The detailed plan table shows one level of follow-up depth on the free tier.

The tool may help frame a research question or identify authorities to inspect. It can also provide a preliminary explanation for a person deciding what professional help to seek.

The pricing page contains a potential ambiguity. Summary bullets near the top mention document reviews and generations, while the detailed comparison shows dashes for those free-plan features. We therefore do not count them as verified free functions. A user should check the live account before assuming access.

The vendor says the product provides information rather than legal advice and tells users to verify official sources. Follow that instruction. Read each cited case or statute and check subsequent history.

## 5. Lawyerz Libra

Lawyerz describes Libra as a free AI lawyer and legal copilot. Its public pages say users can ask questions, conduct research, receive checklists, and create contracts or other documents.

Libra may be useful for generating an issue list or first-pass structure. A student might use it to identify concepts for independent research. A consumer might use it to prepare questions for a lawyer.

The public pages reviewed did not state a clear daily allowance, document cap, or guaranteed response scope. That absence is itself a buying fact. Do not build a workflow around an allowance the vendor has not published. Review the live terms and privacy policy before sharing any personal or client information.

## 6. Aux.ai

Aux.ai provides free conversational legal information across subjects including personal injury, child custody, divorce, criminal law, real estate, and wills. The site says the chat is available around the clock.

The service may help a consumer name the subject of a problem and collect general questions. Its broad subject coverage makes it an orientation tool rather than a substitute for jurisdiction-specific advice.

Aux expressly states that the chat does not create an attorney-client relationship and provides informational content only. The public page reviewed did not publish a detailed free allowance. Users should not infer that the answer has been reviewed for their facts, court, or deadline.

## 7. AI Lawyer

AI Lawyer offers a guided document generator. Users describe a document, answer questions, and preview the result. The site includes agreements such as leases, nondisclosure agreements, contractor agreements, and powers of attorney.

This is a free preview rather than a complete free plan. The vendor says answering questions and previewing a document costs nothing. Downloading, emailing, or printing requires a one-time purchase or subscription. A free trial may also be available.

The tool may help someone see which facts a document normally requests. That does not establish that the form meets a user's legal needs. The site calls itself a self-help preparation tool and recommends review by a licensed attorney before signing or relying on a document.

## What the Current Market Data Shows

Legal AI adoption is meaningful but incomplete. The ABA's [2024 Artificial Intelligence TechReport](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-artificial-intelligence-techreport/) analyzed answers from 512 attorneys. It reported that 30.2% said their offices used AI-based technology tools. The highest rate was 47.8% at firms with at least 500 lawyers. More adoption figures appear in [AI in law firms statistics](/legal-tech/ai-in-law-firms-statistics/) and [legal technology statistics](/legal-tech/legal-technology-statistics/).

Those figures do not measure the accuracy of any product. They also do not show that 30.2% used generative AI for substantive legal work. The report's definition covered AI-based technology broadly.

Reliability research supplies a different warning. A [preregistered empirical study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362) evaluated three proprietary AI legal research tools. Depending on the product and test, the authors found hallucination rates from 17% to 33%. That range belongs to the study's named tools and design. It should not be assigned to every free product.

The practical lesson is narrower: legal specialization and retrieval do not remove the need to verify an answer.

## A Five-Step Verification Method

Use the same checks for every generated legal response. Use these steps on every free legal AI answer before relying on it.

### 1. Identify the Jurisdiction

Write down the country, state, court, and relevant date. If the tool does not ask, supply them. A general answer can combine rules from different places.

### 2. Separate Facts from Assumptions

List the facts the response used. Mark any invented dates, parties, procedures, or contract terms. Ask the tool to state its assumptions, but verify that list yourself.

### 3. Open Every Authority

Do not rely on a citation string. Open the case, statute, regulation, or official page. Check that it exists and supports the sentence attached to it.

### 4. Check Date and Status

Look for amendments, appeals, negative treatment, jurisdictional limits, and later guidance. A real source can still be obsolete or inapplicable.

### 5. Obtain the Required Human Review

Escalate deadlines, filings, negotiations, signed documents, client work, and high-impact decisions. The reviewer should have the authority and knowledge the task requires.

## Confidentiality and Professional Responsibility

Lawyers have obligations that extend beyond clicking a privacy checkbox. Duties such as competence, confidentiality, and supervision still apply when a tool is free.

[Model Rule 1.6](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_6_confidentiality_of_information/) protects information relating to representation. Its comments call for reasonable efforts against unauthorized access and disclosure. State rules and opinions may differ, so the controlling jurisdiction needs a separate check.

Before a firm uses a no-cost AI service with client information, it should answer these questions:

Does the vendor use prompts or files for training?

How long does it retain conversations and uploaded documents?

Which subprocessors can access the information?

Can the user delete and export data?

Does the free tier differ from a business tier in privacy controls?

Can the firm restrict accounts, sharing, and downloads?

Has the client consented where the applicable rules require it?

Until those answers are approved, use fictional or public information.

## When Free Access Is Enough

A free tier can be sufficient for learning the interface, structuring a low-risk question, or testing a public document. It may also help a team decide whether a paid evaluation deserves time. Lawyers who need verified authority should compare paid [legal research software](/legal-tech/legal-research-software/).

Paid access may become relevant when the work requires larger files, team permissions, audit logs, a data-processing agreement, deeper source coverage, or service commitments. Price still does not establish accuracy. The same verification steps apply.

Select free legal AI by task, not by the broadest marketing claim. Record the plan limit, jurisdiction, data rule, output type, and reviewer before use. That short record prevents a trial experiment from becoming an uncontrolled production process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Free Legal AI Legal Advice?

The tools in this list generally describe their output as information or self-help material. A user's legal rights and deadlines may require advice from a qualified lawyer.

### Can a Lawyer Put Client Documents into a Free Tool?

Only after reviewing the applicable professional duties, client instructions, vendor terms, privacy controls, and firm approval. Use fictional or public information during an initial test.

### How Can I Check an AI-Generated Citation?

Open the original authority. Confirm the citation exists, read the relevant text, check the jurisdiction and date, and investigate later treatment.

### Is a Free Trial the Same as a Free Plan?

No. A trial ends after a stated period. An ongoing $0 tier continues but may impose usage, file, or feature limits.
