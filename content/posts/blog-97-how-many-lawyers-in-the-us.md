# How Many Lawyers Are in the US? 2026 Lawyer Statistics

The United States had 1,374,720 resident active lawyers in 2025, according to the American Bar Association (ABA). That is up from 1,355,963 in 2024, a 1.38% increase. Over the past ten years, the count grew by 73,363, or 5.6%.

So the short answer to how many lawyers are in the US is about 1.37 million licensed, active lawyers. The ABA builds the total from state licensing data, so it counts people with active law licenses, not every person working in a legal job.

This page shows the national count, the states with the most lawyers, where the profession is growing, and how the ABA figure differs from federal job data. We checked each ABA figure against the live ABA news page on November 13, 2026, using screenshots of that page because the ABA site blocks automated readers.

## Key Takeaways

- **National Count:** The ABA counted 1,374,720 resident active lawyers in 2025.
- **Steady Growth:** The total rose 1.38% in one year and 5.6% over ten years.
- **Top States:** New York had 190,015 lawyers and California had 181,048.
- **Southern Growth:** The South's lawyer population grew 11% over ten years, while the Midwest fell 1.4%.
- **Gender Shift:** Women were 41% of lawyers in the 2025 demographic data, up one point from 2024.

## The National Lawyer Count

The ABA's [2025 Profile of the Legal Profession announcement](https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2025/12/aba-2025-profile-of-the-legal-profession-report/) reports the latest totals from its National Lawyer Population Survey.

| Year | Resident Active Lawyers | Change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2024 | 1,355,963 | Not shown here |
| 2025 | 1,374,720 | +18,757, or +1.38% |
| Ten-year change to 2025 | Not applicable | +73,363, or +5.6% |

The 18,757 increase is our subtraction of the two published totals.

The ABA links the rise to the law graduate pipeline. It notes that the 2024 graduating class was nearly 12% larger, when it entered law school, than any other class since 2012, citing the National Association for Law Placement.

The ABA quotes Rachel Ellett, associate director of research and evaluation at its Center for Bar Leadership, as saying the survey data suggest the labor market was able to absorb 2024's large graduating class. The ABA also notes that NALP recorded the highest overall employment rate for that class, as well as the highest rate in jobs that require bar admission.

In plain terms, a bigger class of new lawyers entered the profession, and most of them appear to have found legal work quickly. That helps explain why the 2025 increase stood out, and why the ABA paired its lawyer count with graduate employment data.

The ABA has collected lawyer counts since 1878. It says overall growth over those 147 years has been consistently positive, with short bursts of rapid expansion after World War II, in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and in the early 1990s.

New York and California together hold 371,063 lawyers, about 27% of the national total, by our calculation.

The District of Columbia shows why raw counts need care. It is a single city, yet it ranks fifth, ahead of Illinois and Pennsylvania. A per-person comparison would answer a different question and needs population data from the same year.

## How Concentrated the Profession Is

The top ten jurisdictions hold 846,848 lawyers, about 61.6% of the national total, by our calculation. The top four states alone, New York, California, Texas, and Florida, hold about 40%.

That concentration matters when you ask how many lawyers are in the US near you. A national count of 1.37 million spreads very unevenly. Large states and business centers hold most licensed lawyers.

Texas and Florida together hold 180,843 lawyers, about 13.2% of the total. Both also appear among the faster-growing places in the ABA's ten-year data, which fits the South's 11% regional growth.

## Where the Lawyer Population Is Growing

The ABA reports growth at both the state and regional level.

- **Fastest One-Year Growth:** Arkansas, Delaware, Idaho, Massachusetts, Oklahoma, Puerto Rico, and Utah each grew more than 5% in the last year.
- **Highest Percentage Growth Since 2024:** Oklahoma, Arkansas, Delaware, Utah, and Massachusetts led.
- **Ten-Year Growth Above 15%:** Arkansas, the District of Columbia, Idaho, North Carolina, Texas, and the Virgin Islands.
- **Biggest One-Year Declines:** Montana, Vermont, Wyoming, and West Virginia.

The ABA says the ten-year growth was likely driven by growing demand for legal services in metro areas. That is the ABA's reading. The survey itself does not measure why lawyers moved.

| Census Region | Ten-Year Change |
|---|---:|
| South | +11% |
| West | +7% |
| Northeast | About +3% |
| Midwest | -1.4% |

The ABA also notes how concentrated some regions are. As of 2025, 61% of resident active lawyers in the West are in California. In the South, 21% are in Texas and 17% are in Florida.

## Lawyer Demographics

The ABA's 2025 survey shows women at 41% of lawyers and men at 58%, a one-point rise in the female share since 2024. The ABA also reports a small uptick in African American, Asian, and Hispanic lawyers, and a small decline in Native American and Hawaiian or Pacific Islander lawyers.

These shares are not a full census. The ABA notes that the number of states reporting race and ethnicity data has stagnated in the last three years. National percentages therefore rest on the states that report, not on every lawyer.

## Licensed Lawyers Versus Lawyer Jobs

The ABA count and federal job data measure different things.

| Measure | What It Counts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Resident active lawyers | People with active licenses, by residence | ABA National Lawyer Population Survey |
| Lawyer employment and pay | Jobs classified as lawyers | Bureau of Labor Statistics |
| Law firms | Businesses in the Offices of Lawyers industry | Census Bureau |

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics lawyer profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/lawyers.htm) reports a median annual wage of $159,670 for lawyers in May 2025. Its job counts cover a different population than the ABA, because a licensed lawyer may work outside a lawyer job, be self-employed, or keep an active license while retired.

A lawyer licensed in several states can also appear in more than one state's records. The ABA asks for resident active counts to reduce that double counting, but it cannot remove it entirely.

## Lawyers Versus Law Firms

The number of lawyers is not the number of law firms. The Census Bureau counted 156,583 firms in the Offices of Lawyers industry in 2022, and 74.5% of them had fewer than five employees. Those firms also employ paralegals and other staff.

Many lawyers work outside firms entirely, in companies, government, courts, or nonprofits. Our [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/) cover firm counts, employment, and receipts in detail.

## The Wider Legal Workforce

Lawyers are only part of the people who work in legal services. Census data for 2022 show that the Offices of Lawyers industry employed 1,078,148 people across 166,972 locations. That count includes paralegals, assistants, and office staff, and it leaves out lawyers who work in companies or government.

The same data show how the industry earns its money. Offices of Lawyers reported $356.2 billion in receipts in 2022. The 203 firms with 500 or more employees took in about 34.9% of that total, even though they were only 0.13% of firms.

So asking how many lawyers are in the US gives one answer, about 1.37 million licensed lawyers. Asking how many people work in law firms gives a different one, about 1.08 million jobs. Both are correct for their own question.

## Why Lawyer Counts Differ Between Sources

You may see different answers to how many lawyers are in the US. Here is why.

1. **Licenses or jobs.** The ABA counts active licenses. BLS counts jobs. A licensed lawyer working as a business executive appears in one but not the other.
2. **Residence.** The ABA asks state licensing bodies for resident active lawyers, and each state keeps its own records.
3. **Duplicates.** A lawyer licensed in several states can appear more than once, though the resident-active method reduces this.
4. **Year.** Pages often quote older ABA editions. Always check which survey year a figure comes from.

## The Pipeline Into the Profession

New lawyers come from law schools, so admissions data hint at where the count is heading. AccessLex reports that law schools awarded 36,340 J.D. degrees in 2025. LSAC reports that 82,487 people applied to law school in the 2026 cycle, up 7.9% from the year before.

Not every graduate becomes a resident active lawyer. Some never take the bar, some work outside law, and some are licensed in more than one state. Still, a larger pipeline usually means more licensed lawyers a few years later. The ABA itself linked the 2025 rise to the large 2024 graduating class.

If application growth continues, the next answers to how many lawyers are in the US are likely to keep rising. But that is a projection, not a published figure, and the ABA count should be checked each year.

## How to Use the Lawyer Count

Different readers use the ABA figure in different ways.

- **Students:** Compare state counts with your target market, then look at local job data before choosing where to practice.
- **Law firms:** Use state and regional growth to see where competition for lawyers and clients may be rising.
- **Journalists and researchers:** Quote "resident active lawyers" and the survey year, and do not mix ABA counts with BLS job counts.
- **Policymakers:** Pair the count with population data from the same year before drawing conclusions about access to lawyers.

## What the Count Can and Cannot Show

The ABA count can support comparisons across years, states, and regions, and it shows broad demographic direction.

It cannot show how many lawyers actively serve clients, how many work full time, how many practice in a given specialty, or whether people can find a lawyer when they need one. Those questions need other data.

Demand for lawyers shows up in other legal statistics. Federal courts handled 621,111 [bankruptcy filings](/legal/us-bankruptcy-statistics/) in the year to September 2026, and the EEOC received 88,201 charges covered in our [employment discrimination statistics](/legal/employment-discrimination-statistics/). The supply side starts with admissions, where [law school acceptance rates](/legal/law-school-acceptance-rates/) fell to 65% in 2025.
