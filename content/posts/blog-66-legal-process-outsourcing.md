# Legal Process Outsourcing: 9 Services Firms Can Compare

Legal process outsourcing assigns a defined legal or support workflow to an outside provider. Common examples include document review, contract support, legal research, and eDiscovery operations. The retaining lawyer still needs to control scope, protect information, supervise the work, and take responsibility for the legal service.

This article compares nine service categories rather than naming one "best" provider. Provider capabilities differ, and professional duties depend on the jurisdiction and matter. A firm should obtain written answers, test a bounded project, and retain the judgment that cannot be delegated.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define the Work Unit:** Specify inputs, outputs, authority, exclusions, and escalation points.
- **Retain Legal Judgment:** Outsourcing support does not transfer the lawyer's responsibility to the client.
- **Inspect Data Handling:** Trace access, storage, transfer, deletion, and subcontractors.
- **Measure Quality:** Use written instructions, sampling, error categories, and acceptance criteria.
- **Price Change Work:** Revisions, exceptions, rush work, and reprocessing can alter the quoted total.

## What the Term Includes

The market uses several overlapping labels. An LPO provider usually delivers repeatable legal work or support through managed people, process, and technology. An alternative legal services provider may also offer staffing, consulting, software, or legal operations. The broader ALSP category should not be treated as identical to LPO. Corporate legal departments buy these services too, and [in house counsel](/legal-business/in-house-counsel/) often decide which work stays internal.

The [Alternative Legal Services Providers 2025 Report](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/january/alternative-legal-services-providers-2025-report-shows-segment-comprises-28-billion-of-the-legal-market.html), issued by the Thomson Reuters Institute, Georgetown Law's Center on Ethics and the Legal Profession, and Oxford's Saïd Business School, estimated the market at $28.5 billion. Thomson Reuters said that size reflects 18% compound annual growth from 2021 to 2023.

Those figures show the scale of alternative delivery. They do not represent LPO-only revenue, and they do not prove that outsourcing saves money in every matter.

## Nine Service Categories to Compare

The table summarizes legal process outsourcing services by output and control question.

| Service | Typical Defined Output | Primary Control Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal research support | Research memo and source file | Which jurisdictions and authorities are included? |
| Document review | Coded documents and issue log | How are calls sampled and escalated? |
| eDiscovery operations | Processed, hosted, or produced data | Who controls chain, format, and production decisions? |
| Contract support | Reviewed, extracted, or drafted contract material | Which clauses or deviations require lawyer review? |
| Litigation support | Chronology, summary, index, or filing support | Which tasks remain with admitted counsel? |
| Due diligence | Structured findings and exception list | What is material and what is outside scope? |
| Compliance support | Monitoring record, checklist, or alert | Who interprets ambiguous regulatory requirements? |
| IP support | Docket, search, or portfolio record | Which jurisdiction and filing authority apply? |
| Administrative support | Intake, transcription, billing, or records | What information can staff access or change? |

The same provider may sell several categories. Compare each workflow separately because risk, expertise, and evidence needs differ.

## 1. Legal Research Support

Research outsourcing can cover a defined question, jurisdiction, and date range. A useful deliverable includes the search scope, authorities reviewed, links or copies, negative findings, and unresolved issues.

The firm should state whether the assignment includes cases, statutes, regulations, agency material, secondary sources, or legislative history. It should also specify citation format and treatment checks. A memo without its source trail is difficult to verify.

The supervising lawyer must test the authorities and analysis. Ask who performs the research, which databases they use, and how the provider handles a question that crosses jurisdictions. Set a point where the team stops and escalates instead of guessing.

## 2. Document Review

Managed document review can classify records for relevance, responsiveness, issues, confidentiality, or privilege. Providers such as Integreon and UnitedLex publicly describe managed-review services for law firms and legal departments.

A review protocol should define each code, examples, precedence rules, quality checks, and escalation. The provider should report disagreements and changes to the protocol. The firm should retain decisions about privilege, production, and litigation strategy.

Compare reviewer qualifications, supervision ratios, sampling methods, re-review triggers, and error reporting. A low hourly rate can become expensive when unclear instructions create rework.

## 3. eDiscovery Operations

eDiscovery support can include collection coordination, processing, hosting, analytics, review administration, and production preparation. These steps affect the evidentiary record and often involve large volumes of sensitive information.

Define who controls preservation, collection decisions, deduplication, processing exceptions, search terms, privilege handling, and production specifications. Record the technology, versions, time zone, and data location.

The provider's work should fit the firm's discovery plan. Technical staff can operate the workflow, while counsel retains legal decisions and representations to a court or opposing party.

## 4. Contract Review and Management

Contract outsourcing can cover intake, abstraction, review against a playbook, drafting, negotiation support, obligation tracking, and repository cleanup. Elevate and Integreon describe managed contract services on their current sites.

The playbook should define approved language, fallback positions, prohibited changes, monetary thresholds, and escalation. It should also state who may communicate with the counterparty and who approves a final agreement.

Compare contract types rather than total volume alone. A standardized nondisclosure workflow differs from a negotiated data-processing or strategic supply agreement. Price the exceptions and lawyer review the operating model requires.

## 5. Litigation Support

Litigation support may include chronologies, deposition summaries, medical-record summaries, exhibit indexes, cite checking, docket monitoring, or filing preparation. These outputs help counsel organize facts and procedure.

Specify the source record and required links. For a chronology, require page references for each event. For a transcript summary, define whether the provider uses a page-line digest, topic summary, or both.

The engagement must also respect authorization rules. A provider can assist with a task without taking over legal representation. Counsel should decide strategy, sign submissions, communicate positions, and perform any activity reserved to authorized practitioners.

## 6. Transaction Due Diligence

Due-diligence support can extract and classify information from contracts, corporate records, licenses, or other deal documents. The deliverable may be a matrix, red-flag report, or structured data set.

Before work starts, define materiality thresholds, document populations, issue taxonomies, and missing-document rules. A provider should distinguish "not found" from "not applicable" and "not reviewed." Those labels affect what the deal team can conclude.

Use a lawyer-approved sample before scaling. Compare the provider's treatment of amendments, conflicting terms, handwritten material, foreign-language records, and incomplete files.

## 7. Compliance and Regulatory Support

Compliance support can include monitoring, policy mapping, due-diligence checks, regulatory inventories, record administration, or recurring reports. The provider may collect and organize information while counsel or compliance leaders interpret requirements.

Define the jurisdictions, sources, update frequency, and effective-date rules. Ask how the team handles conflicting or ambiguous material. An alert that lacks the underlying authority gives the reviewer too little to assess.

The engagement should name the person who accepts each update and changes a policy or control. Outsourcing the monitoring step does not assign internal responsibility for the organization's response.

## 8. Intellectual Property Support

IP support may include docketing, portfolio data cleanup, prior-art or trademark searches, filing preparation, renewal administration, and record updates. Work varies by right and jurisdiction.

Accuracy and deadlines are central. Define the source of each date, the verification process, reminders, backup responsibility, and escalation for conflicting records. Confirm who may file or communicate with an intellectual property office.

Compare language capacity, jurisdictional coverage, system integration, and quality controls. A portfolio cleanup project and ongoing deadline administration need different service levels.

## 9. Legal Administrative Support

Administrative outsourcing can cover intake, scheduling, transcription, billing support, document formatting, records organization, and status updates. These tasks can contain confidential information even when they do not require legal analysis.

Set access by role. An intake worker may need contact details and conflict information without access to every matter document. A billing team may need time and invoice records without authority to alter legal work.

Check training, supervision, call or email scripts, authentication, and change logs. For transcription, define audio deletion and accuracy review. For billing, define adjustments, write-offs, trust information, and approval rights.

## Professional Duties Stay with the Firm

A firm can outsource support work, but it keeps responsibility for the legal service. Supervision, confidentiality, communication, conflicts, and fees all stay on the firm's checklist.

[Comment 3 to Model Rule 5.3](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_5_3_responsibilities_regarding_nonlawyer_assistant/comment_on_rule_5_3/) recognizes outside nonlawyer assistance. It calls for reasonable efforts to ensure compatible conduct. Relevant factors include education, experience, reputation, the service, information-protection terms, and the legal and ethical environment.

[Model Rule 5.5](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_5_5_unauthorized_practice_of_law_multijurisdictional_practice_of_law/) addresses unauthorized practice. State rules and ethics opinions can differ from the ABA models. Review the rules that govern the lawyer, client, matter, and provider location.

Client communication also depends on circumstances. Sending protected information to people outside the firm can raise consent and disclosure questions. Do not substitute a standard clause for a jurisdictional analysis.

## How to Compare Providers

Send each candidate the same request for information. Keep the work sample fictional or properly approved. Compare every legal process outsourcing proposal on the same five areas.

### Scope and People

Who performs the work, and where are they located?

What training and experience apply to this assignment?

Who supervises them, and at what ratio?

Can the provider change personnel or subcontract work?

Which questions require escalation?

### Information and Security

Where will data be stored, processed, and backed up?

Which staff, systems, and subprocessors can access it?

How are users authenticated and permissions logged?

What incident notice and cooperation terms apply?

How will the provider return and delete information?

### Conflicts and Independence

What names and relationships enter the conflicts process?

How does the provider separate competing clients and teams?

Can the provider work for an adverse party later?

Which financial or referral relationships require disclosure?

### Quality and Evidence

What written protocol controls the work?

How does the provider sample and measure accuracy?

Which error categories trigger rework?

Does every conclusion link to a source record?

Who approves a changed instruction?

### Price and Change Control

Is pricing hourly, per item, per document, per matter, or fixed?

What assumptions underlie the volume estimate?

How are exceptions, rush requests, and rework priced?

Which technology, hosting, transfer, and management fees apply?

What happens when the client changes the scope?

## Run a Bounded Pilot

Select a representative, reversible work set. Provide the same protocol and expected output to each finalist. Measure turnaround, error types, escalation quality, reviewer effort, and total cost. A pilot tests legal process outsourcing before a firm commits real matters.

A pilot should not ask only whether the provider finished on time. Measure how much internal work was required to make the output usable. If the firm's lawyer must reconstruct sources or correct recurring classifications, the nominal provider rate understates the cost.

Record the accepted workflow after the pilot. Name the matter owner, provider lead, escalation channel, quality sample, data location, reporting cycle, and exit process. Those controls turn legal process outsourcing from an informal handoff into a managed service.

## When Outsourcing Fits

The model can fit defined, repeatable, measurable work with known escalation points. It can also supply temporary capacity or skills the firm does not need full time. Small firms often weigh outsourcing against better systems, so compare [legal practice management software](/legal-tech/legal-practice-management-software/) before adding a provider. Firm-size context appears in [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/).

Keep work inside when the task depends heavily on tacit client knowledge, immediate strategic judgment, an authorization the provider lacks, or information the firm cannot disclose under the proposed arrangement. A project can also split. The provider completes a structured first pass, while the firm's lawyer decides exceptions and final positions.

Legal process outsourcing should begin with the service design. A provider brand comes later. That order makes proposals easier to compare and reduces the risk of buying a capability that does not solve the firm's actual workflow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Outsourcing Legal Work Ethical?

Legal process outsourcing can be ethical, subject to the applicable rules and facts. ABA guidance emphasizes competence, supervision, confidentiality, communication, conflicts, fees, and retained responsibility.

### Does LPO Always Mean Offshore Work?

No. A provider may deliver work onshore, offshore, nearshore, or through a mixed team. Location still affects privacy, access, time zones, and the legal environment.

### What Work Should a Small Firm Outsource First?

Begin with a bounded, repeatable task that has written inputs, outputs, and quality checks. Avoid starting with an assignment that depends on undocumented judgment.

### How Should a Firm Price an LPO Proposal?

Compare total delivered cost. Include management, technology, hosting, transfer, revisions, exceptions, internal review, and exit work.
