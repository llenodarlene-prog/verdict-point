# Law Firm Online Reviews: Protect Confidentiality Before Publishing a Reply

A negative review can feel like an invitation to correct the record. The lawyer may know facts that would make the criticism seem unfair. Publishing those facts can create a more serious problem than the review itself.

A responsible law firm online reviews policy starts with a pause. It asks who the reviewer may be, what information a reply would reveal, and whether any public response is necessary.

This blog provides a proposed workflow for a U.S. legal practice. It is general editorial guidance. The governing jurisdiction's rules and advice on the specific situation control the actual response.

## Key Takeaways

- **Preserve Before Responding:** Record the review and its context through an approved internal process.
- **Do Not Debate the File Publicly:** A reviewer's allegations do not create blanket permission to disclose information.
- **Separate Removal and Response:** A platform report is a different action from a public reply.
- **Require Approval:** Staff and vendors need a clear boundary on publishing responses.

## Recognize the Difference Between Reputation and Confidentiality

An ordinary business may want to rebut a complaint with transaction details. A law practice has additional duties concerning information about representation.

The fact that a reviewer mentioned an event does not mean the firm may publish everything it knows. A response can reveal information indirectly, including through confirmation of a relationship or details that identify another person.

For law firm online reviews, the initial question is therefore not which wording sounds most persuasive. It is whether a reply can be made appropriately at all. Asking for reviews follows separate rules in a [law firm reviews process](/legal-marketing/law-firm-reviews-process/).

The [ABA's Formal Opinion 496](https://www.cobar.org/Portals/COBAR/Repository/CBA/Ethics22221/aba-formal-opinion-496.pdf), issued January 13, 2021, explains that a negative review alone does not justify disclosure under the model rule's self-defense exception. State authorities remain controlling.

Use that boundary as a reason to route the issue to an accountable reviewer.

## Preserve the Record Without Broadening Access

Save the review URL, date, visible wording, and relevant platform context. Follow the practice's retention and access rules.

Do not circulate the review with a full client file attached. The people making an initial routing decision may not need the underlying case details.

Keep public material separate from restricted internal analysis. A marketing contractor might need the review text and the approved action, rather than the complete narrative.

If the content raises a credible threat, an allegation requiring formal handling, or another urgent concern, follow the appropriate escalation process. A marketing reply should not become the default response to every problem.

Preservation also helps if the review changes. Record updates without altering the original capture.

## Classify the Situation Before Choosing an Action

The review may concern a current client, former client, prospective client, opposing party, mistaken identity, or an unknown person.

Those categories can affect the analysis. Staff should not make a confident classification from a username alone.

The following triage table is a proposed routing tool. It does not determine whether disclosure is legally permitted.

| Situation | Initial Question | Proposed Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known client or former client | Would any reply reveal representation information? | Attorney review |
| Possible prospective client | What duties arose during the contact? | Attorney review |
| Unknown reviewer | Can identity be verified without public speculation? | Restricted fact check |
| Apparent wrong business | What public, nonconfidential evidence supports the mismatch? | Platform-policy review |
| Possible policy violation | Which specific platform rule applies? | Documented reporting decision |

An uncertain identity should remain uncertain. Do not publish an accusation of fraud simply because the name is unfamiliar.

## Consider No Public Reply as a Deliberate Option

With law firm online reviews, silence can be an intentional decision, rather than a failure to manage reputation.

A response may give the review more attention or invite an extended exchange. The firm should weigh that possibility alongside its professional obligations.

The ABA opinion identifies not responding as an option. It also discusses limited responses that avoid revealing protected information.

Do not promise that a short disclaimer is safe in every situation. The surrounding review and the practice's knowledge can change what a reader infers.

The Florida Bar's Opinion 21-1 illustrates why jurisdiction-specific guidance deserves attention. It addresses responses involving nonclients and confidentiality boundaries under Florida's rules.

A national marketing template should not flatten those differences into a universal permission.

## Draft Only What the Firm Can Approve

A proposed reply should be brief, restrained, and free of case-specific facts. That does not mean a stock template can be published without review.

An illustrative response might say: "Professional obligations limit what we can discuss publicly. Please contact our office through the established private channel."

This example is not approved legal wording. The practice must assess whether it fits the situation and whether the private contact route is appropriate.

Avoid references to fees, strategy, appointments, evidence, behavior, or results that reveal information about a representation.

For law firm online reviews, restraint also applies to tone. Sarcasm, threats, and point-by-point rebuttals can deepen the dispute even when no confidential fact is stated.

The objective is an appropriate action, not winning a public argument.

## Separate Platform Reporting From Disagreement

Among law firm online reviews, an unfavorable review is not automatically a policy violation. Identify the actual rule before submitting a removal request.

Google's [contributed-content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) addresses categories such as fake engagement and prohibited content. A firm should not misuse reporting tools to remove honest criticism.

Prepare a concise explanation supported by evidence the practice may appropriately disclose. Attorney review may be necessary even for material sent privately to a platform.

Record what was submitted and the platform's decision. Do not promise the review will disappear.

A denied report does not establish that the review is accurate. It means the platform did not remove it through that process.

Likewise, removal does not prove the underlying legal dispute was resolved.

## Keep Private Resolution Separate From Public Editing

A private discussion may help address a genuine service concern. It needs the same care with roles, confidentiality, and appropriate communication.

Do not make a benefit conditional on changing or removing a review. Google prohibits incentives for revisions or removal under its policy.

The FTC's [review-rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) also discusses deceptive suppression and unfounded threats. Its rules and a platform's policies are distinct requirements.

A service remedy should be considered on its own merits. The practice should not turn a complaint into a negotiation over public sentiment.

Record the operational issue and the approved response. A billing explanation, missed appointment, or communication failure may warrant internal repair regardless of what happens to the review.

## Give Vendors a Clear Publishing Boundary

A reputation vendor may offer automatic responses or broad account access. Inspect those settings before enabling the service.

A generic retail response engine can be inappropriate for a legal practice. It may confirm a client relationship or repeat details from the review.

The contract and operating instructions should state who drafts, who approves, and who may publish. The firm should retain access to the account and its history.

Use fictional reviews to test the workflow. Include a review with identifying facts, an unfamiliar name, and a complaint that tempts a detailed rebuttal.

The [legal-technology adoption framework](/legal-tech/legal-technology-statistics/) helps distinguish access from actual, governed use. Installing a tool does not establish that its outputs are reviewed.

Disable any automatic route that bypasses the approved decision process.

## Handle Positive Reviews With Similar Care

A positive review can also invite disclosure. A friendly reply should not add the case type, outcome, or relationship detail. Reviews also appear among the channels in the [law firm marketing statistics](/legal-marketing/law-firm-marketing-statistics/).

Staff may recognize the person and want to personalize the response. The practice should define what acknowledgment is appropriate before that impulse reaches the public account.

Reusing praise in advertising is a separate decision. Check permission, accuracy, platform terms, and professional rules in the new context.

A statement about helpful reception should not become a claim about legal results. An excerpt should preserve the original meaning.

The [website conversion framework](/legal-marketing/law-firm-website-statistics/) can help separate testimonial display from measured inquiries. Do not claim that a positive review caused an engagement without evidence.

For this work, a consistent policy covers praise as well as criticism.

## Create a Response Register With Minimal Information

A register helps the practice avoid duplicate actions and unapproved replies. Keep it limited to what the process requires.

The following fields form a proposed internal control. Access should match the sensitivity of the record.

| Register Field | Purpose | Information Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review URL and date | Locate the public record | No unnecessary file attachment |
| Assigned reviewer | Establish responsibility | Appropriate staff only |
| Proposed action | Record reply, report, private contact, or no action | Avoid speculative allegations |
| Approval record | Show who authorized publication | Preserve the approved wording |
| Follow-up date | Check platform or service action | Do not promise removal |
| Closure reason | Explain the final process decision | Keep confidential analysis restricted |

The register is not a public evidence file. A marketing dashboard can show counts without exposing the internal reasoning.

## Train the Team Before the Next Difficult Review

A law firm online reviews policy becomes useful when staff can apply it under pressure. Walk through examples before an emotionally charged situation occurs.

Ask the team to identify what a response would reveal. Include indirect disclosures, such as confirming that the reviewer had a consultation.

Make the escalation route easy to find. A staff member should know whom to contact when the responsible lawyer is unavailable.

Keep account permissions narrow enough to support the policy. Someone who can schedule routine posts does not necessarily need authority to answer reviews.

The [client-acquisition evidence](/law-firms/law-firm-client-acquisition-statistics/) may inform marketing priorities, but it does not override confidentiality.

A successful policy gives the team a defensible decision and a clear record. It does not require every review to receive a public answer.

## Review Indirect Disclosures in the Entire Reply

A reply can reveal information without naming a case or quoting a file. A reference to a missed appointment, a payment conversation, or the date of a consultation may confirm a relationship.

Read the proposed response together with the original review. Consider what a reader could infer from the combination.

The same sentence can carry a different implication under a different review. That is why approval should attach to the specific use, rather than only to a reusable template.

Ask the reviewer to remove unnecessary personalization. A public username should not be expanded into a full name from internal records.

Do not paste the draft into a public writing tool with confidential context. Use the practice's approved drafting environment and access controls.

If the reply cannot be made useful without revealing protected information, the appropriate decision may be no public reply. Record that decision rather than forcing an answer.

## Correct an Unapproved Reply Through the Escalation Process

If a staff member or vendor publishes without approval, preserve the record and notify the responsible reviewer promptly.

The reviewer should decide the appropriate correction, removal, and any further steps. A marketing contractor should not improvise a second explanation that repeats the disclosure.

Review the permission failure afterward. The repair may require changing account access, approval routing, or vendor settings.

A written policy helps only when the publishing controls support it. Use the incident to improve that process without circulating the confidential content more widely.

Review account activity periodically with the person responsible for approvals. Check that the published wording matches the approved version and that any later edits were authorized. A correct initial approval does not prevent a vendor or staff member from changing the reply afterward. Preserve the final public version in the register.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Firm Publicly Rebut Law Firm Online Reviews?

A reply requires careful review under the governing rules. A negative review alone does not create blanket permission to disclose representation information.

### Does a Reviewer Waive Confidentiality by Posting First?

Do not assume that. The practice must assess its own duties before responding.

### Is No Reply an Acceptable Decision?

Yes, it can be a deliberate choice after review. Record the decision and any appropriate internal follow-up.

### Can a Vendor Respond Automatically?

The proposed policy requires accountable approval. Automation should not publish case-specific or unreviewed replies.

### Will Reporting a Review Guarantee Removal?

No. The platform decides under its process and policies. Disagreement with the review does not itself establish a violation.
