# MyCase Legal Software and Client Communication

Comparing practice-management software by monthly fee is easy. The harder question is how a prospective client moves from first inquiry to a signed engagement, an active matter, and a paid invoice, and which tools carry each step.

MyCase legal software has three U.S. plans with public prices: Basic, Pro, and Advanced. They differ in price and in how much of that client journey they support. Basic includes a limited client portal. Pro adds two-way texting, chat, intake forms, and workflows. Advanced adds tools such as Open API access for firms with heavier integration needs. This is a desk-researched buying guide built from MyCase's public pages as of October 2, 2026, not a hands-on product review.

## Key Takeaways

These are the pricing and workflow points to confirm before choosing a plan.

- **Three Plans Have Public Prices:** With annual billing, Basic is $50, Pro is $100, and Advanced is $130 per user per month. Monthly billing costs $10 to $20 more per user.
- **The Portal Differs by Plan:** MyCase labels the Basic client portal as limited, so test the exact client tasks the firm needs.
- **Pro Is the Communication Step-Up:** Pro adds two-way texting, chat, a call log, intake forms, a legal CRM, workflows, and unlimited eSignature.
- **Accounting Costs Extra:** MyCase Accounting is a $39 per user per month add-on, not part of the base subscription.
- **Clients Are Never Billed as Users:** MyCase says firms are never charged for clients and contacts given portal access.
- **Prepaid Terms Are Not Refunded:** A canceled subscription runs to the end of its term, and MyCase does not refund unused time.

## Map the Client Journey Before Comparing Plans

A feature list is only useful once it is tied to the work a client and the firm must complete. For most firms, that journey starts before a matter is opened and ends after the final payment.

Table: The client journey and what to verify at each stage {.cost}
| Client Stage | Client Action | Firm-Side Need | What to Verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inquiry | Shares contact and matter details | Intake ownership and response time | Form routing and alerts |
| Qualification | Answers follow-up questions | Conflict and fit review | What stays outside the portal |
| Engagement | Reviews and signs documents | Templates and approvals | eSignature by plan |
| Active matter | Exchanges messages and files | Permissions and staff ownership | Portal, chat, and text records |
| Billing | Reviews an invoice | Billing setup and approval | Accounting add-on needs |
| Payment | Pays online | Reconciliation and trust controls | Processing rates in writing |
| Matter close | Retrieves records | Retention and export | Access after the matter ends |

Mapping these stages first prevents a common mistake: picking a plan because a feature name sounds familiar, without checking how it behaves in the firm's real process. "Client portal" can sound complete until a buyer asks whether the client can do every required task on the chosen tier.

Intake deserves special attention because software cannot fix an undefined qualification process. Firms tracking response speed and conversion can use [law firm client acquisition statistics](/law-firms/law-firm-client-acquisition-statistics/) to decide which handoffs to test first. The website is often where that journey starts, so the same benchmarks used in [law firm website statistics](/legal-marketing/law-firm-website-statistics/) help define what the intake form needs to capture.

| 8am IQ writing and document assistant | Not listed | Included | Included |
| Open API and conflict check tracking | Not listed | Not listed | Included |

The gaps between tiers are easier to judge plan by plan, starting with the entry tier.

### Basic Covers a Narrower Starting Point

Basic may suit a firm that needs case management, time entry, invoicing, and trust accounting, and can work within a limited portal. Treat "limited" as a prompt for specific demo questions. Ask MyCase to show exactly what a client can view, upload, sign, and send on Basic.

### Pro Adds Communication and Intake Tools

Pro is where texting, chat, intake forms, and workflows appear. During a trial, test how a new inquiry becomes a contact or matter, how duplicates are handled, who receives alerts, and where each message is stored for later review.

### Advanced Supports More Complex Connections

Advanced adds Open API access, conflict check tracking, split billing, a desktop drive, advanced document automation, and the 8am IQ case and discovery assistants. Open API access matters when a firm needs MyCase legal software to exchange data with other systems, but API availability alone does not mean an integration is built, supported, or inexpensive. Before paying for Advanced, name the connection, the data moving in each direction, the system of record, and who will build and maintain it.

## Decide What Belongs in the Portal

A portal gives clients one place to exchange information, but good communication still depends on house rules. The firm must decide which messages and documents belong there and when a call or meeting is the better channel.

Start with ownership. Every portal request needs an owner, a response target, and a backup. Without those, a shared inbox becomes one more place where work waits. Next, test permissions. A business client may have several contacts who need different access, and some matter documents should not be visible to everyone. Finally, confirm where portal messages, texts, call logs, and signed documents appear in the matter record, and how they can be exported if the firm ever changes systems.

Portal rules matter more than the portal itself, because MyCase legal software cannot decide who answers a client. The broader point applies to every legal tool: availability is not the same as use. As [legal technology statistics](/legal-tech/legal-technology-statistics/) show, firms often license features that staff rarely open, so the portal rollout plan matters as much as the plan tier.

## Add Accounting and Payments to the Cost Model

The base subscription is not the whole software cost. MyCase lists its accounting product as a $39 per user per month add-on. A firm considering it should confirm which staff need access and whether it replaces or duplicates current accounting software.

```chart
mycase-ten-user-annual-cost
```

For 10 users, accounting adds $4,680 a year if every user needs it. On Basic, that add-on is close to the cost of the subscription itself, so the seat count for accounting deserves its own decision.

LawPay payments are listed with no monthly fee, but MyCase describes the processing rates only as "competitive" on the pricing page. Get the card, eCheck, and chargeback terms in writing for the exact account being offered, and model them against the firm's real payment volume.

## Confirm Seats, Terms, and Cancellation Before Signing

The help article says the subscription rate depends on the number of active firm users, and that firms are never charged for the clients and contacts given portal access. That keeps client access from turning into a seat charge, but the firm still has to define who needs a paid login: attorneys, paralegals, intake staff, billing staff, and administrators.

Cancellation terms matter just as much. MyCase says a canceled subscription stays active until the end of the current term and that it does not refund either plan for unused time. A firm choosing annual billing for the discount is committing to the full year.

## Which MyCase Legal Software Plan Fits?

The right plan is the smallest one that completes the firm's client journey without leaving costly gaps. Basic may cover firms that rely on phone and email and need only a limited portal. Pro fits firms that want texting, chat, intake forms, and automated workflows in one place. Advanced makes sense when Open API access, conflict check tracking, or its added tools solve a defined need.

Whichever plan wins, the final comparison should include the billing term, every paid user, accounting, payment processing, retained third-party tools, and the cancellation terms. With those visible, the monthly price of MyCase legal software becomes one part of a sound decision instead of the whole decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These answers cover the MyCase legal software pricing and licensing points buyers most often misread.

### How Much Does MyCase Cost in 2026?

With annual billing, Basic is $50, Pro is $100, and Advanced is $130 per user per month. Monthly billing is $60, $120, and $150.

### Does MyCase Charge for Client Portal Users?

No. MyCase says firms are never charged for the clients and contacts they give portal access to. Only active firm users count toward the subscription.

### Is MyCase Accounting Included?

No. MyCase Accounting is a $39 per user per month add-on. Decide which staff need it before pricing the plan.

### Can a Firm Cancel MyCase at Any Time?

Yes, but the subscription stays active until the end of the paid term, and MyCase does not refund unused time on either billing plan.
