# MyCase Legal Software: Which Plan Fits the Client Journey?

The price of practice-management software is easy to compare when the only question is the monthly fee. The decision becomes harder when a firm asks how a prospective client moves from intake to an active matter, where messages are stored, who can sign documents, and what happens when an invoice is ready.

MyCase legal software currently has three U.S. plans with public prices: Basic, Pro, and Advanced. The price gap is substantial, but the plans also support different parts of the client communication workflow. Basic includes a limited portal, while Pro adds broader communication and intake tools. Advanced introduces capabilities such as Open API access for firms with more demanding integration needs.

This is a desk-researched buying guide, not a hands-on product review. It uses current public documentation to help a firm map its workflow before requesting a demonstration or starting a trial.

## Key Takeaways

The following points capture the main pricing and workflow differences that a buyer should verify.

- \*\*Three Publicly Priced Plans:\*\* Annual billing is listed at $50 per user per month for Basic, $100 for Pro, and $130 for Advanced. Monthly billing costs more.  
- \*\*The Portal Is Not Identical Across Plans:\*\* The current pricing grid labels Basic client-portal access as limited, so buyers should test the exact tasks their clients need to complete.  
- \*\*Pro Is the Main Communication Step-Up:\*\* Pro lists two-way texting, chat, call logging, intake forms, legal CRM, workflows, and unlimited eSignature and document generation.  
- \*\*Accounting Is an Add-On:\*\* MyCase lists accounting at $39 per user per month rather than including it automatically in every subscription.  
- \*\*Portal Clients Are Not Paid Firm Users:\*\* MyCase’s help documentation says clients, contacts, and portal users are not charged as active firm users.  
- \*\*Payment Processing Still Has a Cost:\*\* Integrated LawPay is shown with no monthly subscription fee, but transaction fees still apply.

## Map the Client Journey Before Comparing Plans

A feature list becomes more useful when it is tied to the work a client and the firm must complete. For most firms, the journey begins before a matter is opened and continues through payment and document retention.

| Client Stage | Client Action | Firm-Side Requirement | Point to Verify |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| Initial inquiry | Shares contact and matter details | Intake ownership and response process | Form routing and response alerts |  
| Qualification | Answers follow-up questions | Conflict and fit review | What remains outside the portal |  
| Engagement | Reviews and signs documents | Template and approval workflow | eSignature availability by plan |  
| Active matter | Exchanges messages and files | Permissions and staff responsibility | Portal, chat, text, and record handling |  
| Billing | Reviews an invoice | Billing setup and approval | Accounting add-on requirements |  
| Payment | Pays through an available method | Reconciliation and trust controls | Transaction fees and settlement workflow |  
| Matter close | Retrieves or receives records | Retention and export process | Access after the matter ends |

This sequence prevents a common buying mistake: selecting a plan because it contains a familiar feature name without testing how that feature behaves in the firm’s actual process.

For example, “client portal” may sound complete until the buyer asks whether a client can perform every required task under the selected tier. The same applies to texting. A firm needs to know which employees can send messages, where the record appears, how responsibility is assigned, and what happens when the assigned person is unavailable.

The intake stage also deserves attention. Better software cannot correct an undefined qualification process. Firms evaluating their response and conversion stages can use \[client-intake measurement\](https://verdictpoint.org/law-firms/law-firm-client-acquisition-statistics/) to decide which handoffs need to be tested during the trial.

## Compare MyCase Prices on the Same Billing Basis

The \[current MyCase pricing page\](https://www.mycase.com/pricing/) lists rates for annual and monthly billing. Both columns matter because a low annualized rate should not be compared with another product’s flexible monthly price.

| Plan | Billed Annually | Billed Monthly | Difference per User per Month |  
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |  
| Basic | $50 | $60 | $10 |  
| Pro | $100 | $120 | $20 |  
| Advanced | $130 | $150 | $20 |

MyCase also advertises a 10-day trial without requiring a credit card. A trial can help the team test the workflow, but it does not replace a written cost review. The final budget should state the seat count, billing term, selected plan, add-ons, payment-processing costs, taxes where applicable, and any tools that remain outside the platform.

Consider a 10-user firm choosing Pro. At the public annual-billing rate, the subscription arithmetic is $1,000 per month, or $12,000 over 12 months. At the monthly-billing rate, the same seat count is $1,200 per month. That is a $2,400 difference over a full year before add-ons, taxes, or transaction charges.

\> Pricing Calculation Box  
\> 10 Pro users billed annually: 10 × $100 × 12 = $12,000  
\> 10 Pro users billed monthly: 10 × $120 × 12 = $14,400  
\> Annual difference: $2,400  
\> This is arithmetic based on published rates, not a contract quote.

The comparison should use the same method for competing systems. For instance, \[Clio’s full cost structure\](https://verdictpoint.org/legal-tech/clio-legal-software-total-cost/) separates public subscription pricing from payments, integrations, migration, and internal implementation time. Applying the same categories to both products produces a fairer decision than comparing their lowest advertised rates.

## Match the Plan to the Communication Workflow

MyCase legal software does not present the same communication package in every tier. The Basic portal is marked limited on the pricing grid, while Pro adds a broader group of client-facing and staff workflow tools. Advanced builds on that package with capabilities intended for more complex operations.

### Basic Covers a Narrower Starting Point

Basic may suit a firm that needs core practice-management functions and can operate within the plan’s limits. However, the word “limited” should trigger specific demonstration questions rather than assumptions.

The firm should prepare two or three realistic client scenarios and ask MyCase to show what the client can see, upload, download, sign, and send. It should also confirm which notifications are available and how the team sees an unanswered client action.

### Pro Adds Communication, Intake, and Workflow Tools

The current pricing grid lists two-way texting, chat, and a call log under Pro. It also lists intake forms, legal CRM, workflows, and unlimited eSignature and document generation.

Those tools cover more of the journey, but availability does not prove that the workflow will fit every team. During the trial, a firm should test how a new inquiry becomes a contact or matter, how duplicate information is handled, who receives alerts, and where communication records can be found later.

### Advanced Supports More Complex Connections

Advanced includes Open API access, which may matter when the firm needs to connect MyCase with other systems or build a more tailored data flow. API availability alone does not confirm that an integration is ready, supported, or inexpensive.

A buyer should identify the required connection, the data moving in each direction, the system of record, the implementation owner, and the expected support model. That turns “Open API” from a feature label into a practical integration requirement.

## Decide What Belongs in the Portal

A portal can give clients one place to exchange information, but good client communication still depends on operating rules. The firm must decide which messages, documents, and requests belong there and which situations require a phone call, meeting, email, or another approved channel.

Start by assigning responsibility. Every portal request should have an owner, a response expectation, and a backup person. Without those decisions, a centralized inbox can simply become another place where work waits.

Permissions also need testing. A business client may have several contacts with different access needs. An individual matter may involve documents that should not be visible to every participant. The demonstration should show how access is granted, reviewed, changed, and removed.

Finally, confirm how the record is preserved. The firm should understand where portal messages, texts, call records, signed documents, and shared files appear in the matter. It should also ask how information can be exported if the firm changes systems.

MyCase’s \[official product overview\](https://www.mycase.com/) describes the platform’s broader practice-management position. That is useful for orientation, but a plan-specific demonstration remains the better place to confirm which client-facing actions are available under the proposed subscription.

## Add Accounting and Payments to the Cost Model

The base subscription is not necessarily the entire software cost. The current pricing page lists accounting as a $39-per-user monthly add-on. A firm considering it should confirm which employees need access and whether the add-on replaces or duplicates its existing accounting workflow.

Integrated LawPay is shown with a $0 monthly subscription fee, but that does not mean payments are free. Transaction charges still apply. The budget should model the firm’s expected payment volume and method mix rather than inserting a generic monthly estimate.

This distinction is also important when comparing \[LawPay payment fees\](https://verdictpoint.org/legal-tech/lawpay-legal-payments-fees/). Terms presented inside an integrated product should not automatically be replaced with the price of a separate standalone arrangement. The buyer needs the charges that apply to the exact account and workflow being offered.

\> Payment Cost Check  
\> Confirm the integrated subscription charge, transaction rates, chargeback terms, ACH treatment, settlement timing, trust-account workflow, taxes, and any compliance-related fees directly in the proposed agreement.

## Confirm Seats, Terms, and Cancellation Before Signing

MyCase’s \[current cost help page\](https://supportcenter.mycase.com/en/articles/9369812-how-much-does-mycase-cost) says there is no setup fee and that clients, contacts, and users invited to the Client Portal are not charged as active firm users. That keeps client access from becoming a seat charge, but the firm still needs to define its paid user count.

Potential users can include attorneys, paralegals, administrators, intake employees, billing staff, and other people who need firm-side access. The correct number depends on the proposed workflow, so it should be confirmed in writing rather than inferred from attorney headcount.

The same help page says cancellation remains subject to the paid term and that tax may apply. A buyer should therefore ask when cancellation takes effect, what happens to prepaid amounts, how renewal works, and how data can be retrieved before access ends.

## Use the Trial as a Workflow Acceptance Test

A 10-day trial is short enough that the firm should decide what success looks like before it begins. A prepared test is more useful than allowing each employee to explore the menus independently.

Use one sample matter and walk it through the stages that matter most:

1. \*\*Create the Intake Record:\*\* Enter a sample inquiry and confirm routing, ownership, duplicate handling, and required fields.  
2. \*\*Invite a Sample Client:\*\* Review the invitation, sign-in process, permissions, and the client’s view on desktop and mobile.  
3. \*\*Exchange a Document:\*\* Upload, share, revise, and retrieve a file while checking notifications and matter records.  
4. \*\*Test Communication:\*\* Send an approved sample message through each planned channel and confirm where it is stored.  
5. \*\*Complete an eSignature:\*\* Generate and sign a sample document under the proposed plan.  
6. \*\*Create an Invoice:\*\* Confirm approvals, accounting dependencies, and what the client sees.  
7. \*\*Test a Payment Handoff:\*\* Review the available payment path without using live client or banking information.  
8. \*\*Export the Record:\*\* Confirm how the firm retrieves documents, contacts, matter details, billing records, and communication history.

Record the result for each step as passed, failed, partly supported, or not tested. Note the plan, staff owner, client action, notification, permission check, and follow-up question. This creates a decision record that can be compared with another platform on the same basis.

## What Should a Firm Decide Before Buying?

MyCase legal software is easiest to evaluate when the firm begins with the client journey rather than the longest feature list. Basic, Pro, and Advanced differ in price and in how much of that journey they support.

The decision should identify the smallest plan that completes the required workflow without creating costly gaps. For some firms, Basic may cover the essentials. Others may need Pro’s broader communication, intake, workflow, and document functions. Advanced becomes more relevant when API access or its additional capabilities solve a defined operating need.

The final comparison should include the billing term, all paid users, accounting, payment charges, retained third-party tools, implementation time, and contract conditions. Once those items are visible, the advertised monthly price becomes one part of a defensible buying decision instead of the whole decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These answers address the pricing and licensing points buyers are most likely to misunderstand.

### How Much Does MyCase Cost in 2026?

MyCase lists Basic, Pro, and Advanced at $50, $100, and $130 per user per month with annual billing. Monthly rates are $60, $120, and $150.

### Does MyCase Charge for Client Portal Users?

MyCase’s help documentation says clients, contacts, and Client Portal users are not charged as active firm users. Firms should confirm the treatment in their quote.

### Does Every MyCase Plan Include the Same Client Portal?

No. The current pricing grid labels Basic portal access as limited. Buyers should test the exact client actions they require under the proposed plan.

### Is MyCase Accounting Included in the Subscription?

Not automatically. MyCase currently lists accounting as a $39-per-user monthly add-on, so the seat count and workflow should be priced separately.

### Is Integrated LawPay Free With MyCase?

The pricing page shows no monthly subscription fee for integrated LawPay payments, but transaction fees still apply. Confirm the live payment terms before signing.

### Can a Firm Cancel MyCase at Any Time?

The help page says cancellation is subject to the paid term. Buyers should confirm the effective date, renewal conditions, prepaid amounts, and data-export process.
