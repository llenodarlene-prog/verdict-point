# 15 Types of Lawyers and Their Practice Areas

Types of lawyers usually describe practice areas, such as family law, tax, or commercial litigation. They can also describe employment settings, including government and in-house work. Those categories overlap. A corporate lawyer may specialize in securities, while an employment lawyer may advise either workers or employers.

The fifteen areas below explain the work behind common labels in the United States. They are a practical selection, not an official count of every legal specialty. Specific services and authorization still depend on the lawyer and jurisdiction.

For career planning, examine the work product as well as the subject. Drafting an agreement, investigating a dispute, and preparing an appeal involve different assignments. A familiar title does not describe the entire working day.

## Key Takeaways

- **Practice areas overlap:** One transaction or dispute can involve several legal specialties.
- **Employer setting differs:** In-house, government, and law-firm roles describe where someone works.
- **Client side changes the assignment:** Two lawyers in one field may represent opposing interests.
- **Salary needs compatible evidence:** An occupation-wide median does not rank earnings across specialties.

## Read Practice Labels as Descriptions of Work

Practice labels identify a field of work rather than a complete job description. A lawyer's authorization requires a separate check. The [distinction between professional titles and authorization](/legal/lawyer-vs-attorney/) remains relevant when reading biographies or career advertisements. Most lists of types of lawyers mix subject labels with workplace labels.

Practice labels also divide into narrower areas. Business law alone can include transactions, financing, disputes, and regulated activities. A useful career comparison therefore asks what the lawyer does within that field.

Use the numbered explanations to compare typical subjects and possible assignments. The examples illustrate work categories, not legal advice about a particular situation. They do not establish that every lawyer offers every listed service.

## Fifteen Practice Areas to Compare

These practitioners handle different legal subjects, although their assignments can intersect. Each description identifies the field and a practical distinction to investigate.

### 1 Corporate and Business Lawyers

Corporate lawyers work on business structures, governance, and transactions. Their matters often involve company formation, financing, commercial contracts, and acquisitions.

A possible assignment involves reviewing documents for a business purchase. Another involves preparing corporate approvals. The exact work depends on the transaction and the client's instructions.

For career research, ask whether the role emphasizes advisory work, drafting, or deal execution. Corporate practice does not necessarily mean employment inside a company. A law firm can provide corporate legal services to outside clients.

### 2 Commercial Litigation Lawyers

Commercial litigators handle business disputes. The ABA Litigation Section includes business and commercial cases within its civil-litigation subjects.

An illustrative assignment could involve assessing a contract dispute and preparing the record for a hearing. The work may include research, factual analysis, and written arguments. Settlement discussions and procedural preparation can also form part of a dispute assignment.

Do not treat litigation as a promise of constant courtroom appearances. Ask which stage the employer handles and what junior lawyers prepare. A trial role and a document-heavy discovery role can sit within the same field.

### 3 Criminal Defense Lawyers

Criminal defense lawyers represent people or organizations accused of crimes. Their client-facing role differs from the prosecutor's role in pursuing government charges. The [BLS lawyer profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/lawyers.htm) distinguishes those positions.

The practice can involve reviewing evidence and preparing responses to the government's allegations. The actual case determines the issues and procedure. Avoid assuming that one criminal-defense role covers every offense or court.

For career planning, distinguish private defense work from public-defense employment. Both concern representation, but employer structure and assignments require separate research. The job description should identify the setting and expected responsibilities.

### 4 Labor and Employment Lawyers

Labor and employment lawyers address workplace relationships and related disputes. The ABA's subject map includes discrimination, employment agreements, benefits, workplace investigations, and collective labor issues.

A role may support an employer, individual workers, or a union. That client side changes the questions the lawyer investigates. Advising on a policy and litigating a dismissal dispute are also different assignments.

Ask whether the work emphasizes counseling, negotiation, investigation, or litigation. Avoid treating employment law as one uniform career. A benefits specialist and a workplace-discrimination litigator may need different day-to-day knowledge.

### 5 Family Lawyers

Family lawyers work on legal issues involving family relationships. The ABA Family Law Section covers adoption, divorce, parentage, and related subjects.

Possible assignments include reviewing proposed agreements or preparing material for a family-court proceeding. The relevant jurisdiction and circumstances determine the legal requirements. A general description cannot establish an outcome for an individual family.

When comparing careers, ask about client communication and dispute-resolution work. Also identify the mix of advisory and contested assignments. The same subject area can involve document preparation, negotiation, and court proceedings.

### 6 Estate Planning and Probate Lawyers

Estate lawyers work on planning and administration involving property after death and related arrangements. The ABA trust-and-estate subjects include wills, trusts, estate planning, and administration.

Planning documents and administering an estate are separate kinds of work. A disputed estate adds another dimension. Do not assume every practitioner handles planning, tax questions, and litigation equally.

For a hypothetical career comparison, ask whether the position drafts planning documents or supports estate administration. Then ask which specialists review tax or contested issues. The practice label alone leaves those responsibilities unresolved.

### 7 Real Estate Lawyers

Real estate lawyers address property transactions and related legal issues. The ABA real-property topics include residential transactions, commercial property, financing, land use, and leasing.

A lease review differs from a development approval or ownership dispute. Those assignments may involve different records and participants. The property's location also affects the legal framework requiring review.

For career research, identify the transaction type and client group. A commercial-development practice may differ considerably from residential conveyancing work. Ask what documents the role prepares and which negotiations or proceedings it supports.

### 8 Tax Lawyers

Tax lawyers address tax-related legal questions and disputes. The ABA Taxation Section covers business structures, international activity, employment taxes, and other tax subjects.

An assignment might involve analyzing a proposed transaction's tax issues. Another might involve responding to a dispute with a revenue authority. These examples identify work categories without recommending a tax position.

Ask whether the role involves advisory work, controversy, or a specialist tax area. Do not equate every tax-lawyer position with preparing routine returns. The employer should explain the analysis, documentation, and representation the position requires.

### 9 Intellectual Property Lawyers

Intellectual property lawyers work with rights involving inventions, brands, and creative material. The ABA intellectual-property subjects distinguish copyright, patent, and trademark work.

Protecting a brand differs from negotiating a technology license or litigating an infringement claim. These subjects can involve different procedures and qualifications. Read the specific role before treating IP as one interchangeable specialization.

A career discussion should identify the rights involved and the work's purpose. Ask about advisory, registration-related, licensing, or dispute assignments. Verify any specialized admission requirement separately rather than inferring it from the broad title.

### 10 Securities Lawyers

Securities lawyers work on legal issues involving investments and securities transactions. Disclosure forms part of that environment. The SEC's Corporation Finance division describes its role concerning information for investment and voting decisions.

An illustrative assignment involves reviewing disclosures for a company raising capital. Other work can involve ongoing reporting or securities disputes. The client and transaction determine the lawyer's precise responsibility.

For career planning, separate transactional securities work from enforcement or litigation. Also distinguish an issuer, investor, intermediary, and regulator. Similar subject knowledge can serve very different client positions.

### 11 Immigration Lawyers

Immigration lawyers address legal questions involving immigration status and related proceedings. The ABA Commission on Immigration's representation projects illustrate asylum, removal-defense, and agency-related work. The assignment determines which application, evidence, or procedure needs review.

For a career comparison, distinguish employment-related work from family-based or contested proceedings. An employer seeking help with workforce immigration has a different objective from an individual facing removal proceedings.

Ask which authorities and forums the practice uses. Do not assume one professional title establishes authorization for every form of representation. This overview identifies the practice area without assessing anyone's eligibility for an immigration benefit.

### 12 Bankruptcy and Restructuring Lawyers

Bankruptcy lawyers address debt-related proceedings, while restructuring work may also involve negotiations outside court. [US Courts' Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics) explains the federal bankruptcy framework for individuals and businesses.

The client's position changes the assignment. Representing a debtor differs from representing a creditor or another participant. Reviewing a proposed reorganization also differs from analyzing a particular claim.

For career research, identify the kinds of clients and proceedings the employer handles. Do not turn a category description into advice about filing. Specific decisions require a review of circumstances and applicable requirements.

### 13 Environmental Lawyers

Environmental lawyers address legal issues involving environmental regulation, resources, and related disputes. The ABA environment section covers air, chemicals, energy, water, and other subjects.

A position may involve regulatory counseling or responding to an enforcement matter. Another may involve litigation or a transaction containing environmental questions. Government, businesses, and advocacy organizations can approach the same subject from different positions.

Ask which laws, activities, and geographic areas the role addresses. Environmental work is broader than one industry. A useful job description should identify the actual regulatory or dispute work expected.

### 14 Personal Injury Lawyers

Personal injury lawyers work on civil claims involving harm to people. [Cornell's legal reference](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/personal_injury) distinguishes personal injury from harm to property rights. It also describes several legal bases for claims.

An assignment can involve investigating a factual record, assessing evidence, or preparing a civil dispute. The relevant facts and law determine the issues. No general career description can establish liability or compensation.

For career comparison, identify which side the employer represents and the kinds of claims involved. Ask about investigation, negotiation, and litigation responsibilities. Avoid inferring a lawyer's work solely from consumer advertising.

### 15 Antitrust and Competition Lawyers

Competition lawyers address legal issues involving market competition and business conduct. The ABA Antitrust Law Section includes mergers, pricing, joint conduct, and litigation or enforcement.

A merger-related assignment differs from defending a conduct investigation. Advisory work can also involve evaluating proposed business arrangements. These examples explain the field without deciding whether a particular arrangement is lawful.

For a career discussion, ask whether the role emphasizes transactions, investigations, or disputes. Then identify the client and jurisdiction. Those details explain the assignment more clearly than the competition label alone.

## Separate Specialty from Employment Setting

Employer settings describe how legal work is organized, not another exclusive list of specialties. A firm, company, government agency, or public-interest organization can employ lawyers working in overlapping fields. Company legal departments are one such setting, and [in house counsel roles and outside-counsel work](/legal-business/in-house-counsel/) explains how those lawyers serve an organization as the client. Treat the types of lawyers below as subjects, and the employer as a separate choice.

Use these questions when comparing opportunities.

- **Subject:** Which area of law does the team handle?
- **Client:** Whose interests does the lawyer represent?
- **Setting:** Does the role sit inside an organization or serve external clients?
- **Output:** Does the lawyer prepare advice, agreements, submissions, or litigation material?

Comparing types of lawyers this way avoids mixing unrelated labels. In-house describes an employment setting. Tax describes a subject. One position can satisfy both descriptions.

## What National Workforce Data Can Establish

BLS reports a $159,670 median annual lawyer wage for May 2025. Its wage measure excludes self-employed workers and owners or partners of unincorporated businesses. It does not provide a comparable salary for each specialty listed here. For counts of firms and employees by size, see [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/).

The same profile projects 5% employment growth over 2025–2035. It projects about 28,700 annual openings across the decade, including replacement needs. Those figures cannot establish that each practice area grows at that rate.

Use the figures as occupational context. Compare a specific role with compatible employer and location evidence before assessing compensation. Avoid ranking specialties using a mixture of job advertisements and unrelated salary surveys.

## Compare the Work Before Choosing a Field

Start a career shortlist with assignments you want to investigate. Select a few practice areas and read original employer descriptions. Record the documents, clients, and review responsibilities each role involves. Reading about types of lawyers is a start, but daily tasks matter more than labels.

Then seek opportunities to learn about those assignments through appropriate study or professional conversations. Keep the question concrete: what does a junior lawyer prepare, and who reviews it? That comparison gives types of lawyers a useful meaning beyond a title list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Many Types of Lawyers Exist?

There is no single exhaustive number used here. Practice areas divide into smaller fields and overlap across assignments.

### Is In-House Counsel a Separate Legal Specialty?

It describes an employment setting. An in-house lawyer can also specialize in contracts, tax, employment, or another field.

### Does Every Lawyer Regularly Appear in Court?

No. Advisory and transactional work can involve different responsibilities from litigation.

### Which Specialty Pays the Most?

The cited occupation-wide data cannot answer that question. A defensible comparison needs compatible specialty, location, and experience measures.
