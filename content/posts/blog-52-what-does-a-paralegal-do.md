# What Does a Paralegal Do in a Law Firm

What does a paralegal do beyond keeping files organized? A paralegal performs delegated legal work under a lawyer's supervision. That work can include research, document preparation, factual investigation and case coordination. The exact assignment depends on the practice and the lawyer responsible for it.

For a firm, the useful question concerns the output each assignment should produce. A file summary, evidence index and draft document serve different purposes. Clear instructions help the paralegal deliver useful work while keeping legal advice and responsibility with the appropriate professional.

## Key Takeaways

- **Substantive Work Defines the Role:** Paralegals support legal matters through delegated work that requires more than routine clerical handling.
- **Supervision Remains Central:** A lawyer directs the assignment and retains responsibility for the legal work.
- **Tasks Vary by Setting:** Litigation support, transactional work and an in-house legal team may require different outputs.
- **Client Contact Has Boundaries:** Gathering information or sharing an approved update differs from giving independent legal advice.
- **A Usable Work Product Helps the Team:** Good research, organized evidence and clear handoffs support the lawyer's review.

## What Is a Paralegal

The [American Bar Association's current definition](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/profession-information/current_aba_definition_of_legal_assistant_paralegal/) emphasizes specifically delegated substantive legal work. It recognizes qualifications through education, training or experience. A lawyer remains responsible for the work described in that definition.

That distinction gives firms a practical starting point. A paralegal's contribution should relate to a legal assignment and its required judgment. The lawyer decides what to delegate and how to supervise it.

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics occupation profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/paralegals-and-legal-assistants.htm) describes research, document preparation and case-related support. It also explains that responsibilities vary with practice area and employer size. A small office may distribute tasks differently from a large litigation team. Most law firms are small, as the [legal industry statistics by firm size](/legal/legal-industry-statistics-firm-size/) show, and BLS notes that paralegals at smaller firms may work on all phases of a case.

When assessing a vacancy, read the actual responsibilities. The title alone will not reveal whether the position emphasizes document review, investigations or transaction support. Ask what the person should produce and who will review it.

## What Does a Paralegal Do During a Matter

A matter can generate many different assignments. The [National Association of Legal Assistants describes common duties](https://nala.org/what-do-paralegals-do/) including research, interviews, document drafting and summaries. It also emphasizes supervision and clear disclosure of the paralegal's nonlawyer status.

The following examples show how a firm could turn those tasks into reviewable outputs. They illustrate possible assignments rather than mandatory duties for every position.

### Research and Factual Investigation

A lawyer might ask a paralegal to identify authorities addressing a defined issue. A useful assignment states the jurisdiction, question and deadline. The paralegal can then organize the material for review.

For factual work, the output might be a chronology. Each entry could identify a date, event and supporting document. Missing records should remain visible rather than disappear inside a smooth narrative.

For example, two emails might describe the same meeting differently. The chronology should preserve both accounts and identify their sources. The lawyer can then assess the conflict instead of inheriting an unexplained conclusion.

### Drafting and Document Preparation

A delegated drafting assignment might involve correspondence, a document summary or an initial pleading draft. Instructions should identify the purpose, source material and review stage.

A useful handoff separates completed content from unresolved questions. If a required fact remains missing, flag it for review. Do not fill the gap with an assumption simply to make the draft look finished.

Version control also helps the reviewing lawyer. Label the current draft clearly and identify the source of significant changes. Keep the record understandable for another team member who joins the assignment later.

### Coordination and Matter Organization

Some assignments focus on making the matter usable for the team. Examples include arranging interviews, preparing exhibit indexes or organizing records for a hearing.

These tasks benefit from a defined output. An exhibit index might identify the file, description and review status. An interview schedule might identify the participants, purpose and outstanding preparation.

A calendar entry alone may leave important questions unanswered. Record where the date came from and who must confirm it. The firm's lawyer should establish the process for reviewing legal deadlines.

## Tasks and Work Products to Discuss with a Supervisor

The table below turns broad responsibilities into concrete assignment examples. It also identifies questions a firm should settle before work begins.

| Delegated Task | Illustrative Work Product | Review Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research a defined issue | Source list and short research summary | Does the research address the correct question and jurisdiction? |
| Organize a factual record | Chronology with document references | Which facts conflict or lack supporting material? |
| Prepare a draft | Review copy with unresolved points identified | What must the lawyer assess before anyone uses it? |
| Support an interview | Question outline and factual notes | Which questions require legal judgment or follow-up? |

The review question matters as much as the task label. It gives the paralegal a clearer target and the lawyer a useful starting point. A firm can adapt this matrix to its actual practice.

## Work That Requires a Clear Boundary

Delegation does not transfer every professional responsibility. The ABA's guidance for lawyers on using paralegal services identifies limits around legal advice, establishing the client relationship and setting legal fees. The applicable jurisdiction's rules control the actual arrangement.

For an ordinary supervised paralegal role, a practical boundary separates information handling from independent legal judgment. Asking a client for a missing document differs from advising how that document changes their legal position.

The ABA Litigation Section's discussion of paralegal work and supervision reinforces the lawyer's responsibility for appropriate oversight. A firm should make that responsibility visible in its workflow.

A supervisor can clarify these points before assigning work.

- **Purpose:** Explain what the work should help the lawyer assess or complete.
- **Authority:** Identify decisions the paralegal may make and questions requiring escalation.
- **Review:** State who must check a draft before it leaves the team.
- **Communication:** Explain which updates may go to clients and which questions need a lawyer.
- **Uncertainty:** Provide a route for reporting missing evidence, conflicting instructions or an unfamiliar requirement.

Other legal-service roles may have separate authorizations under particular rules. Do not assume those permissions apply to an ordinary paralegal position. Check the actual role and jurisdiction before expanding an assignment.

## Client Contact Without Independent Legal Advice

Paralegals may gather information and help coordinate communication within their assigned responsibilities. The firm should distinguish that contact from advice about what a client should do.

Consider a hypothetical request for missing invoices. A paralegal can ask the client to supply the records requested by the lawyer. A question about the legal significance of a missing invoice should go through the agreed review process.

The same distinction helps with status updates. Explaining that a meeting has moved differs from recommending a settlement position. Firms should define the boundary before a time-sensitive conversation occurs.

A useful communication note records the following details.

- **The Client's Question:** Capture the issue accurately without adding an answer that nobody approved.
- **Information Received:** Identify documents, dates and factual statements separately from conclusions.
- **The Next Owner:** State who will review the question or provide the substantive response.
- **The Promised Follow-up:** Record what the team agreed to do, including any stated timing.

This structure can help the lawyer review the exchange. It also gives the client a clearer explanation of who will answer the question.

## How Duties Change Across Practice Settings

The same title can describe different working days. The legal matter determines which information and documents the team needs.

### Litigation Support

A litigation assignment might focus on a factual chronology, discovery materials or hearing preparation. The paralegal could organize records and identify gaps for the lawyer's attention.

One useful practice is to separate source documents from working summaries. That allows the lawyer to trace a statement back to the original record. It also makes later corrections easier to review.

### Transactional and in House Support

A transactional assignment might involve organizing agreement versions, tracking requested documents or maintaining a closing checklist. An in-house assignment might focus on gathering information from different business teams.

For these examples, the handoff should distinguish completion from approval. Receiving a document does not establish that a lawyer has accepted its legal terms. A checklist should show which review remains outstanding.

The [difference between professional titles and legal authorization](/legal/lawyer-vs-attorney/) also applies when describing the supervising professional. Record the responsible person and actual duties instead of relying on an ambiguous label.

## A Hypothetical Assignment from Start to Review

Suppose a lawyer wants a chronology before reviewing a supplier dispute. The firm has email messages, invoices and several agreement versions. This example describes an organizational workflow, not advice about handling a dispute.

The lawyer first states the question and the materials available. The paralegal then prepares an index and a draft chronology. Each entry includes a source reference so the lawyer can check it.

During the review, the paralegal finds an invoice without a matching email. Another document contains a date that conflicts with the client's account. Both issues should remain visible in the handoff.

The paralegal can prepare a short question list. It might ask which agreement version governed the transaction or whether another record exists. The lawyer then decides what those uncertainties mean for the matter.

Before closing the assignment, the team can check the following points.

- **Source Coverage:** Identify the material reviewed and any unavailable files.
- **Traceability:** Confirm that each material chronology entry points to a source.
- **Unresolved Questions:** Separate factual gaps from issues requiring legal analysis.
- **Review Status:** Identify the lawyer who will assess the work and what remains pending.

This example answers what does a paralegal do through a specific output. It shows how useful support makes uncertainty easier to assess. It does not require the paralegal to decide the legal merits independently.

## Skills and Education to Evaluate

Employers should assess the skills needed for the actual assignment. A research-heavy position may require different experience from a role centered on transaction coordination.

BLS describes several educational routes into the occupation, with employer requirements varying. Applicants should check each position's stated requirements rather than assume one qualification guarantees eligibility everywhere.

For a practical interview, ask a candidate to explain a previous work process. Keep the discussion free of confidential client information. A short fictional exercise can also show how the candidate organizes an unfamiliar record.

Evaluate the output against clear criteria. Can another person locate the supporting document? Does the summary distinguish facts from unresolved questions? Has the candidate identified what requires supervision?

Those questions connect recruitment to the firm's work. A long software list or a broad claim about attention to detail provides less evidence. Ask how the person would make an assignment usable for the reviewer.

## Handle Matter Information Within Firm Rules

Assignments may involve sensitive documents and personal information. NALA's professional ethics code addresses client confidentiality. A firm should explain its procedures before giving anyone access to a matter.

For example, identify the approved location for working files. Explain how the team shares a draft with its reviewer. Set a process for reporting an accidental disclosure or an unexpected request for information.

Paralegals should ask for clarification when those instructions conflict or remain incomplete. A convenient personal account should not become the default destination for client material. The team should use its approved process instead.

Review these arrangements when an assignment ends or responsibilities change. Confirm who needs continued access and who will keep the final record. These checks make the handoff clearer without assuming software alone protects confidentiality.

## Agree on the Output Before Delegating

Before the next assignment, define the result you need. Identify the source material, review point and questions requiring escalation. Give the paralegal a practical route for clarifying uncertainty.

For candidates, use the same approach when reviewing a vacancy. Ask what work products the role produces and how supervision operates. Compare the offer against [paralegal salary data by state and employer](/legal/paralegal-salary/) rather than a single national figure. Those answers reveal more about the position than the title alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Does a Paralegal Do Compared with a Lawyer?

In short, what does a paralegal do? A paralegal performs delegated work under supervision. The lawyer retains responsibility for the legal work and decisions reserved to licensed professionals.

### Can a Paralegal Give Independent Legal Advice?

An ordinary paralegal role does not authorize independent legal advice. Check the applicable rules before treating a separately authorized legal-service role as equivalent.

### Is Every Legal Assistant a Paralegal?

Titles and responsibilities vary between employers. Read the substantive duties, qualifications and applicable rules instead of assuming every position uses the terms identically.

### Can Paralegals Communicate with Clients?

Yes, within appropriate assigned responsibilities and supervision. Client contact is part of what does a paralegal do in many firms, but it stays within the lawyer's instructions. Client contact does not authorize independent legal advice.

### Do All Paralegals Perform the Same Tasks?

No. The answer to what does a paralegal do changes with practice area, employer size and the lawyer's assignment.
