# Employment Discrimination Statistics: EEOC Charges and Outcomes

The EEOC received 88,201 charges of employment discrimination in fiscal year 2025, close to the 88,531 it received in fiscal 2024. Retaliation was the most common basis, cited in 54,350 charges, or 61.6%. Disability followed at 41.1%, then race at 33.3%.

Those percentages add up to far more than 100%. One charge can name several bases, such as race and retaliation. That overlap is the key to reading employment discrimination statistics correctly, and many pages get it wrong.

This page shows the latest charge counts by basis, how the EEOC resolved charges, and what it recovered for workers. We matched every figure to EEOC tables and reports on November 13, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **88,201 Charges:** The EEOC received 88,201 discrimination charges in FY2025, about even with FY2024.
- **Retaliation Leads:** Retaliation appeared in 61.6% of charges, up from 47.8% the year before.
- **Disability Is Second:** Disability charges reached 36,256, or 41.1% of all charges.
- **$660 Million Recovered:** The EEOC reports $660 million recovered for 17,680 people in FY2025.
- **Mediation Works Often:** The EEOC resolved 7,929 of 11,346 mediations, about 70%.

The table's notes say the sum across bases will exceed the actual total, because charges filed under several bases are counted under each one. Never add these rows together to estimate unique cases.

## Retaliation Jumped in FY2025

Retaliation charges rose by 12,049 in one year, from 42,301 to 54,350, by our calculation. That moved retaliation from 47.8% of charges to 61.6%, even though total charges barely changed.

Retaliation means an employer punished a worker for a protected act, such as complaining about discrimination or joining an investigation. It often travels with another claim. A worker who reports harassment and is then fired may file a charge citing both.

The table shows the jump but does not explain it. A change in how charges are taken or coded could play a part, as could real changes in workplace behavior. Treat the one-year swing with care until more years confirm it.

## Long-Term Trends

The longer view puts FY2025 in context. Total charges peaked near 100,000 a year around FY2010 to FY2012, then fell to 61,331 in FY2021.

| Fiscal Year | Total Charges | Retaliation | Disability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2010 | 99,922 | 36,258 | 25,165 |
| 2015 | 89,385 | 39,757 | 26,968 |
| 2021 | 61,331 | 34,332 | 22,843 |
| 2023 | 81,055 | 46,047 | 29,160 |
| 2025 | 88,201 | 54,350 | 36,256 |

The FY2021 low of 61,331 was the smallest total in the table since it began in FY1997. Charges then rose for three straight years, to 73,485 in FY2022, 81,055 in FY2023, and 88,531 in FY2024, before leveling off in FY2025. Anyone comparing employment discrimination statistics across years should note that the recent rise starts from that unusual low point.

Disability charges have grown steadily, from 25,165 in FY2010 to 36,256 in FY2025. Color charges more than doubled over the same span, from 2,780 to 6,738.

The EEOC's notes flag one odd year. Religion charges spiked to 13,814 in FY2022, which the table attributes to a significant increase in vaccine-related charges filed on the basis of religion. They fell back to 3,710 by FY2025.

Pregnancy accommodation is the newest category. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act took effect on June 27, 2023, so the EEOC counts only a partial year for FY2023. Charges under it reached 3,448 in FY2025.

Comparing FY2015 with FY2025 shows which complaints are growing and which are shrinking. All counts come from Table E1a.

| Race | 31,027 | 29,338 | -5.4% |
| Sex | 26,396 | 26,941 | +2.1% |
| Age | 20,144 | 16,353 | -18.8% |
| National origin | 9,438 | 7,856 | -16.8% |
| Color | 2,833 | 6,738 | +137.8% |
| Religion | 3,502 | 3,710 | +5.9% |
| Genetic information | 257 | 449 | +74.7% |
| Equal Pay Act | 973 | 928 | -4.6% |

The percentage changes are our calculations. Total charges fell slightly over the same decade, from 89,385 to 88,201.

Three patterns stand out in these employment discrimination statistics. Retaliation and disability grew by more than a third. Color charges more than doubled, from a small base. Age and national origin charges fell by roughly a sixth.

Shares moved too. Retaliation rose from 44.5% of charges in FY2015 to 61.6% in FY2025, according to the table. Disability rose from 30.2% to 41.1%. Age fell from 22.5% to 18.5%.

## Inquiries Versus Charges

Not every contact becomes a charge. The EEOC reports nearly 270,000 inquiries in FY2025 against 88,201 charges. That is roughly three inquiries for every charge, by our calculation.

Inquiries include questions, intake interviews, and contacts that turn out to fall outside the agency's reach. Some people simply want to understand their rights. Others decide, after talking with the agency, to handle the problem inside their company, or file with a state agency instead, which the federal tables do not count. Comparing the two numbers shows how much screening happens before a formal charge exists. It also shows why charge counts alone understate how many workers seek help.

## How the EEOC Resolved Charges

The EEOC's [FY2025 agency performance report](https://www.eeoc.gov/fy-2027-agency-performance-plan-app-and-fy-2025-agency-performance-report-apr) describes the year's results. It resolved 90,743 charges, a 4% increase. It cut the private-sector charge inventory 4%, to 49,807.

Resolving more charges than it received meant the backlog shrank. Resolutions can exceed receipts because the EEOC also closes charges carried over from earlier years.

Mediation stood out. The agency resolved 7,929 of the 11,346 mediations it conducted, about 70%. Those settlements produced almost $245.3 million in benefits for charging parties.

## Money Recovered for Workers

The EEOC's [FY2025 results release](https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/eeoc-highlights-record-breaking-results-agency-reports) totals what the agency obtained across all channels.

| Channel, FY2025 | Amount | People |
|---|---:|---:|
| All recoveries | $660 million | 17,680 |
| Pre-litigation enforcement | $528 million | Not stated here |
| Federal sector | $104.6 million | 1,824 |
| Systemic investigations | $55 million | Not stated here |
| Conciliation | $52.5 million | Not stated here |
| Litigation | $27 million | 2,505 |

The release lists these channels separately and does not show how they combine into the $660 million total. Do not add the rows together, since some channels may overlap. Pre-litigation recoveries rose 12% from FY2024, according to the release.

The EEOC also handled nearly 270,000 inquiries, up almost 9%. Inquiries are first contacts, and many never become formal charges.

## EEOC Lawsuits

Litigation is a small slice of EEOC work. Its Office of General Counsel reports filing 94 merits suits in FY2025. By statute, 54 were under Title VII, 35 under the Americans with Disabilities Act, and 9 under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act. Some suits cite more than one law.

Sex or pregnancy was alleged in 42 suits, disability in 35, and retaliation in 31. The agency reports a successful outcome in 96.5% of merits suit resolutions, excluding voluntary dismissals without prejudice.

## Federal Employees Have a Separate Process

Federal employees use a separate complaint process, and the EEOC reports their results apart from the charge tables.

The EEOC's FY2025 release reports $104.6 million obtained for 1,824 federal employees and applicants. Those results come through federal-sector hearings and appeals, not the private-sector charge system, so they should be reported separately from the 88,201 charges.

## How Workers and Employers Can Use These Numbers

Employment discrimination statistics give context. They do not predict what will happen in any single workplace.

- **Workers:** Retaliation is now the most common basis. Keep records of complaints you make and how your employer responds.
- **Employers:** Rising disability and pregnancy accommodation charges point to the value of clear accommodation processes.
- **Researchers:** Use the basis table for trends, but never add the rows. Pair it with survey data to estimate incidents that never become charges.
- **Journalists:** Report the fiscal year and say "charges," not "cases" or "incidents."

## Systemic Cases

Some EEOC work targets patterns that affect many workers at once. The FY2025 performance report says the agency resolved 444 systemic investigations and obtained over $55 million.

In court, the Office of General Counsel reports resolving 13 systemic suits that recovered over $10.8 million for an estimated 2,270 people. Systemic cases are few, but each one can reach far more workers than an individual charge.

These figures belong in any full set of employment discrimination statistics. A count of individual charges can miss a single investigation that covers hundreds of workers.

## Which Laws the Charges Fall Under

Each basis in Table E1a ties to a federal law the EEOC enforces. Race, color, religion, sex, and national origin claims fall under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act. Disability claims fall under the Americans with Disabilities Act. Age claims fall under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act. Equal pay, genetic information, and pregnancy accommodation each have their own statutes.

Retaliation is different. It can arise under any of these laws, which is one reason it appears in so many charges. A worker who complains about age discrimination and is then demoted may file an age charge and a retaliation charge at once.

## What Charges Do Not Prove

A charge is an allegation. Most charges end without any finding that discrimination occurred. Counts also miss workers who never file, claims filed only with state agencies, and private lawsuits.

Employment discrimination statistics from the EEOC measure reports to one federal agency. They do not measure how much discrimination exists across the labor market.

## How to Read Employment Discrimination Statistics

Before you quote employment discrimination statistics, check these points:

- **Unique or Overlapping:** basis counts overlap; the total does not.
- **Period:** the EEOC fiscal year ends September 30.
- **Unit:** inquiries, charges, resolutions, or lawsuits.
- **Recovery Channel:** pre-litigation, litigation, or federal sector.
