# Car Accident Settlement Calculator

List your losses after a car accident, then test how shared fault, an insurance policy limit, attorney fees, and medical liens change what reaches you. The calculator shows every step. It is a way to organize your numbers, not a prediction of what an insurer or court will pay.

```tool
car-accident
```

## How the Car Accident Settlement Calculator Works

1. **Economic losses.** Medical bills, future care that a provider has estimated, lost wages, future lost earnings, vehicle and property damage, and other costs are added together. These are the losses you can usually document with bills and pay records.
2. **Pain and suffering.** You enter any amount you want to test. Many online calculators multiply medical bills by a fixed number. This one does not, because no fixed multiplier reflects how claims are actually valued.
3. **Shared fault.** If you were partly at fault, the total is reduced by your share.
4. **Policy limit.** If you enter the at-fault driver's policy limit, the scenario caps what that policy pays. Other coverage, such as your own underinsured motorist coverage, may pay more.
5. **Net to you.** The attorney fee, case costs, and medical liens you enter are subtracted.

## Why Shared Fault Matters

States have different rules on shared fault, and in some states your share of fault can reduce or even bar recovery. The calculator applies a simple reduction by your share. Check the rule in your state before relying on a result.

## Worked Example

A driver has $18,500 in medical bills, $4,000 in estimated future care, $6,200 in lost wages, $9,800 in vehicle damage, and $650 in other costs, for $39,150 in economic losses. Testing $25,000 for pain and suffering brings the claim to $64,150.

At 20% shared fault, the claim falls to $51,320. The at-fault driver's $50,000 policy limit caps the scenario at $50,000. A 33% fee ($16,500), $1,500 in case costs, and $7,000 in medical liens leave $25,000 before taxes. Press "Use example" to load these numbers.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **It does not value your claim.** Liability, evidence, injuries, treatment, and insurance all affect outcomes.
- **Liens are often negotiable.** Health insurers, Medicare, and Medicaid may have repayment rights. The amount you enter is your own estimate.
- **Deadlines apply.** Every state sets a time limit to file an injury claim. Use the [legal deadline calculator](/tools/legal-deadline-calculator/) to count days once you know your limit.
- **Fees vary.** Compare arrangements with the [contingency fee calculator](/tools/contingency-fee-calculator/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is a Car Accident Settlement Calculated?

Most claims start with documented economic losses: medical bills, lost income, and property damage. Pain and suffering, shared fault, insurance limits, fees, and liens then change the amount that reaches you.

### Does the Calculator Use a Pain and Suffering Multiplier?

No. You enter the figure you want to test. A fixed multiplier can make a result look precise when it is not.

### What Happens if the Other Driver's Insurance Is Too Low?

The scenario stops at the policy limit you enter. Your own underinsured motorist coverage or other sources may pay more. Ask your insurer or a lawyer.

### Why Is My Net Amount Much Lower Than the Claim Value?

Fees, case costs, and medical liens come out of the recovery. The breakdown shows each deduction.

### Does the Calculator Store My Figures?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to Verdict Point.

## Method

Claim value equals economic losses plus the pain and suffering figure you enter, reduced by your share of fault and capped at any policy limit you enter. Net equals claim value minus the fee, case costs, and liens. Amounts round to whole dollars. More tools are on the [settlement calculators](/tools/settlement-calculators/) page.
