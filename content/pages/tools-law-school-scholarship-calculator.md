# Law School Scholarship Calculator

The bigger scholarship is not always the cheaper school. This calculator compares the full net cost of two offers, including living costs, and shows what happens if a conditional scholarship is lost after the first year.

```tool
scholarship
```

## How the Calculator Works

- **Net cost** = (tuition − scholarship) × years + living costs × years.
- **Stress test:** For a scholarship with conditions, such as a minimum GPA, it also shows the cost if you keep it for year 1 only and pay full tuition after that.

## Worked Example

School A charges $62,000 a year and offers $40,000, with $22,000 a year in living costs. That is $132,000 over three years. School B charges $48,000, offers $15,000, and costs $18,000 a year to live. That is $153,000. School A costs $21,000 less. But School A's scholarship has conditions. If it is lost after year 1, School A would cost $212,000, or $59,000 more than School B.

## Ask About Conditions

Each ABA-approved law school publishes data on conditional scholarships, including how many students kept them, in its Standard 509 disclosure. Ask each school for its numbers before you decide.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **Tuition often rises** each year. Enter an average if you expect increases.
- **Outcomes** such as bar passage and jobs matter too.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Conditional Scholarship?

A scholarship you keep only if you meet a condition, such as a minimum GPA or class rank.

### Should I Negotiate My Scholarship?

Many students ask schools to reconsider their offer, often by sharing a competing offer. Policies vary by school.

### Do Living Costs Really Matter?

Yes. A cheaper city can offset a smaller scholarship over three years.
