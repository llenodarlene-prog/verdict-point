# NDA Generator

A nondisclosure agreement, or NDA, lets people share private information while agreeing to keep it confidential. This generator drafts a plain-English NDA from your choices. Copy it, edit it, and have a lawyer review it before anyone signs.

```tool
nda
```

## What the Draft Covers

- **Parties and purpose:** who is sharing, and why.
- **Definition and exclusions:** what counts as confidential, and the usual exceptions, such as public information.
- **Duties:** use only for the purpose, reasonable care, and limited sharing.
- **Required disclosure:** what happens if a court orders disclosure.
- **Term:** how long the duties last, with longer protection for trade secrets.
- **Return of information:** returning or destroying the information on request.
- **Remedies and governing law.**

## The Federal Whistleblower Notice

The Defend Trade Secrets Act protects people who report suspected illegal activity. Under [18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833), an employer "shall provide notice of the immunity" in any contract with an employee that governs trade secrets or other confidential information. Contractors and consultants count as employees for this rule. If the notice is missing, the employer may not be awarded exemplary damages or attorney fees under the Act against that employee. Check the employee box to add the notice.

## Mutual or One-Way?

Use a mutual NDA when both sides will share information, such as in a joint venture. Use a one-way NDA when only the first party shares, such as when pitching to an investor or hiring a contractor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an NDA Legally Binding?

A signed NDA is a contract, and courts generally enforce reasonable terms. Terms that are too broad, or that hide illegal conduct, may not be enforced.

### How Long Should an NDA Last?

Many business NDAs last two to five years. Trade secrets can be protected for as long as they stay secret.

### Do I Need the Whistleblower Notice?

If an employee or contractor signs an agreement governing trade secrets or confidential information, 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3) requires it. Without it, the employer cannot recover exemplary damages or attorney fees under the Act against that person.
