# Law Firm Name Generator

A law firm name is a legal statement as well as a brand. Bar rules limit what a firm can call itself. This generator builds name ideas from your own surnames, practice area, and city. It then flags any idea that could break common naming rules, so you can rule out risky names early.

```tool
firm-name
```

## The Rules Behind the Checks

Each state bar sets its own rules, but many follow the same ideas. The generator checks against two official sources.

### California Rule 7.5

California's [Rule of Professional Conduct 7.5](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/legal-professionals/rules/rules-professional-conduct/current-rules-professional-conduct/chapter-7-information-about-legal-services) says a lawyer may not use a firm name, trade name, or other professional designation that is false or misleading under Rule 7.1. A lawyer in private practice may not use a name that "states or implies a relationship with a government agency or with a public or charitable legal services organization." A comment adds that logos, letterheads, URLs, and signature blocks count too.

### Florida Bar Rule 4-7.21

Rule 4-7.21 of the [Rules Regulating The Florida Bar](https://www.floridabar.org/rules/rrtfb/) sets out these limits:

- **Trade names:** A lawyer may practice under a trade name if it is not deceptive and does not imply a connection with a government agency or a public or charitable legal services organization.
- **One name everywhere:** A lawyer who advertises under a trade name must use that same name on letterhead, business cards, office signs, fee contracts, and pleadings.
- **No false partnerships:** A name may not imply that lawyers practice as a partnership or business entity when they do not.
- **Public officers:** A lawyer holding public office cannot appear in the firm name while not actively practicing with the firm.

## What the Generator Flags

- **Government-sounding words:** Words like "State," "County," "Federal," "Legal Aid," or "Public Defender" get a caution.
- **"& Associates" for a solo practice:** It suggests associate lawyers who are not there.
- **Two surnames for a solo practice:** Use more than one name only if each person is a lawyer in the firm.
- **"Group" or "Partners":** These can suggest several lawyers or a partnership.
- **Trade names:** Each one gets a reminder to confirm that your state allows it and to use it consistently.

## Worked Example

Two family lawyers named Rivera and Chen plan a PLLC in Tampa Bay. The generator suggests names such as "Rivera & Chen, PLLC," "Rivera Family Law, PLLC," and "Law Office of Rivera & Chen, PLLC." It also suggests trade names, such as "Tampa Bay Family Lawyers," each with a reminder about Florida's trade name rules.

If one lawyer practiced alone, "Rivera & Associates" and "Rivera & Chen" would get cautions.

## Limits of the Generator

- **Two states' rules only.** Your state bar may be stricter or looser. Read its advertising and firm name rules before you choose.
- **No availability check.** Search your state's business entity registry, domain names, and the USPTO trademark database.
- **Entity names must match registration.** Endings like PLLC or P.A. must match how you register with the state.
- **Not legal advice.** Some bars review firm names or ads. Ask yours if you are unsure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Law Firm Use a Trade Name?

In many states, yes, if the name is not misleading. Florida allows trade names that are not deceptive and do not imply a government connection. Some states restrict them more tightly, so check your state bar rules.

### Can a Solo Lawyer Use "& Associates"?

It is risky. The phrase suggests the firm has associate lawyers. If it does not, the name could be misleading under rules like California Rule 7.1 and Florida Rule 4-7.21.

### Can a Law Firm Name Include a City?

Often, as a trade name, if it is not deceptive and does not suggest a government office. Avoid phrasing like "City of" or "County" that could imply an official connection.

### Do I Need to Register My Law Firm Name?

Usually you register the business entity with your state, and some states require a fictitious name filing. Check both your secretary of state and your state bar.
