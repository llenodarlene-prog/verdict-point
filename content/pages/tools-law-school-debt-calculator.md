# Law School Debt Calculator

Law school loans usually start building interest before you graduate. This calculator estimates your balance when repayment starts, your monthly payment, and the total interest, using the rate you enter.

```tool
law-school-debt
```

## How the Calculator Works

1. **Borrowing:** Your yearly borrowing times the years of school.
2. **In-school interest:** Simple interest on each year's loan from the time it is paid out until six months after graduation. Unpaid interest is added to the balance.
3. **Monthly payment:** A level payment over your chosen term on the balance when repayment starts.

## Worked Example

A student borrows $55,000 a year for three years at 8%. That is $165,000 borrowed. About $33,000 of interest builds before repayment, for a starting balance of about $198,000. On a 10-year plan, the payment is about $2,402 a month. Against an $85,000 salary, that is about 34% of gross monthly pay.

## Check Current Federal Loan Terms

Federal loan rates, fees, and borrowing limits change. Check [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) before you borrow, and enter your actual rate.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **Income-driven repayment and forgiveness** can lower payments. This shows a standard level payment only.
- **Origination fees** are not included.
- **Private loans** may have variable rates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much Do Law Students Borrow?

It varies widely by school and scholarship. Check each school's cost of attendance and your own aid offer.

### Does Interest Build While I Am in School?

On unsubsidized loans, yes. This calculator adds that interest to your starting balance.

### What Repayment Term Should I Use?

Ten years is the standard plan. Longer terms lower the payment but raise total interest.
