# Wrongful Termination Settlement Calculator

Enter your pay, how long you have been out of work, and what you have earned since. The calculator adds up back pay, a front pay scenario, and any damages you want to test, and applies the federal limits on damages where they apply. It shows a scenario built from your numbers. It does not predict what a court or employer will pay.

```tool
wrongful-termination
```

## How the Wrongful Termination Settlement Calculator Works

The calculator builds the scenario in parts, so you can see where each dollar comes from.

1. **Back pay.** Your yearly pay plus the yearly value of your benefits, divided by 12, times the months since your job ended. Earnings from other work since then are subtracted. Back pay cannot fall below zero.
2. **Front pay scenario.** If you want to test future lost pay, enter a number of months and what you expect to earn each month from other work. The calculator multiplies the monthly gap by the months.
3. **Damages for emotional harm and punishment.** Enter an amount to test. For federal Title VII and ADA claims, the calculator limits it to the cap for the employer's size.
4. **Liquidated damages.** For intentional age discrimination or Equal Pay Act violations, the calculator adds an amount equal to back pay when you tick that box.
5. **Attorney fee.** An optional percentage is taken from the total to show what is left before taxes and costs.

## Federal Damages Caps by Employer Size

Under [42 U.S.C. 1981a(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1981a), compensatory damages for future money losses, emotional pain, suffering, inconvenience, and other non-money losses, plus punitive damages, cannot exceed these totals for each person bringing a claim:

| Employer size | Combined limit |
| --- | --- |
| 15 to 100 employees | $50,000 |
| 101 to 200 employees | $100,000 |
| 201 to 500 employees | $200,000 |
| More than 500 employees | $300,000 |

Employee counts are for each of 20 or more calendar weeks in the current or previous calendar year. The statute also says these damages do not include back pay or interest on back pay, so back pay sits outside the cap. That is why the calculator keeps back pay as a separate line.

## Age and Equal Pay Act Claims Work Differently

The [EEOC's summary of remedies](https://www.eeoc.gov/remedies-employment-discrimination) says people with age discrimination or Equal Pay Act claims cannot recover compensatory or punitive damages. They may be entitled to liquidated damages when the violation was intentional, in an amount equal to the back pay awarded. When you choose that claim type, the calculator leaves out any emotional distress amount you entered and adds liquidated damages only if you tick the intentional box.

## Worked Example

A worker earned $62,000 a year with $9,000 in yearly benefits and has been out of work for 8 months, earning $12,000 at a temporary job. Monthly pay and benefits are $5,916.67, so lost pay to date is $47,333 and back pay after the offset is $35,333.

Testing 6 months of front pay at an expected $1,500 a month from other work adds $26,500. A $150,000 emotional distress figure against an employer with 101 to 200 employees is limited to $100,000. The scenario total is $161,833, and a 33% attorney fee leaves about $108,428 before taxes and costs. Press "Use example" in the calculator to load these numbers.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **It is not a prediction.** Real outcomes depend on the facts, evidence, defenses, and negotiation. Many claims settle for less than a full scenario, and some for nothing.
- **State law can differ.** Many states have their own employment laws, some without these federal caps. Choose "State law, contract, or other" to skip federal limits, and check your state's rules.
- **Taxes and costs are not included.** Back pay is usually taxed as wages. Litigation costs and liens are separate.
- **Deadlines matter.** Federal discrimination claims usually start with a charge to the EEOC within strict time limits. Use the [legal deadline calculator](/tools/legal-deadline-calculator/) to count days, and check the limit that applies to you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is a Wrongful Termination Settlement Calculated?

Most scenarios start with back pay: the pay and benefits you lost, minus what you earned elsewhere. Other parts, such as front pay and damages for emotional harm, depend on the claim and the law that applies.

### What Is the Cap on Damages in an Employment Discrimination Case?

For federal Title VII and ADA claims, compensatory and punitive damages combined are capped at $50,000 to $300,000 depending on employer size. Back pay is not part of that cap.

### Does Back Pay Count Toward the Damages Cap?

No. The statute says compensatory damages under the cap do not include back pay or interest on back pay.

### Can I Get Emotional Distress Damages for Age Discrimination?

The EEOC says compensatory and punitive damages are not available for age discrimination or Equal Pay Act claims. Liquidated damages equal to back pay may be available for intentional violations.

### Does the Calculator Store My Figures?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to Verdict Point.
