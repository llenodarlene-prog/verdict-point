# Impairment Rating Payout Calculator

When a workplace injury leaves a lasting impairment, workers' compensation pays a benefit based on the impairment rating. Florida calls it impairment income benefits. Many people know it as permanent partial disability. This calculator applies Florida's statute to your average weekly wage and rating, and shows each step.

```tool
ppd
```

## How Florida Calculates Impairment Income Benefits

[Section 440.15 of the 2026 Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/440.15) sets the benefit in three steps.

1. **Temporary total disability rate.** Section 440.15(2)(a) sets this at 66 2/3, or 66.67, percent of the average weekly wage. It cannot exceed the yearly maximum under section 440.12.
2. **Weekly impairment benefit.** Section 440.15(3)(c) pays 75 percent of that temporary total disability rate. Benefits are paid every two weeks.
3. **Number of weeks.** For accidents on or after October 1, 2003, section 440.15(3)(g) sets the weeks by rating.

| Impairment rating | Weeks per percentage point |
| --- | --- |
| 1% to 10% | 2 weeks |
| 11% to 15% | 3 weeks |
| 16% to 20% | 4 weeks |
| 21% and higher | 6 weeks |

The weeks add up by band. A 12% rating earns 20 weeks for the first 10 points and 6 weeks for the next 2, for 26 weeks in total.

## Maximum Weekly Rate

Section 440.12(2) caps weekly compensation at 100 percent of the statewide average weekly wage for the year of injury. The [Florida Division of Workers' Compensation rate table](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/insurer/awwrate/maximum-compensation-rate-table) lists a maximum of $1,358 for 2026 injuries and $1,295 for 2025 injuries. The minimum is $20 a week.

## The 50 Percent Reduction

Section 440.15(3)(c) cuts the benefit in half for each week in which the worker earns income equal to or above the average weekly wage. Enter those weeks in the optional field to see the effect.

## Worked Example

A worker injured in 2026 earned an average weekly wage of $1,200 and received a 12% impairment rating. The temporary total disability rate is 66.67% of $1,200, or $800.04. The weekly impairment benefit is 75% of that, or $600.03. A 12% rating earns 26 weeks. The estimated total is 26 × $600.03 = $15,600.78.

If the worker earned their full wage for 10 of those weeks, those weeks pay $300.02 instead, and the estimated total falls to $12,600.63.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **Florida only, for now.** We add a state only after reading its statute and official rate table directly.
- **Accidents on or after October 1, 2003.** Older accidents use a different schedule.
- **Rating comes from a doctor.** Florida requires an authorized physician to rate the impairment using the state's uniform schedule. This tool does not convert body-part ratings.
- **Not a settlement value.** The estimate leaves out lump-sum settlements, attorney fees, offsets, and permanent total disability.
- **Not legal advice.** Confirm your figures with the carrier or a workers' compensation attorney.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much Is a 10% Impairment Rating Worth in Florida?

A 10% rating earns 20 weeks of benefits. At an average weekly wage of $1,200 for a 2026 injury, that is 20 × $600.03, or about $12,000.60.

### When Do Florida Impairment Benefits Start?

Section 440.15(3)(c) says they begin the day after the worker reaches maximum medical improvement or temporary benefits expire, whichever is earlier.

### Who Assigns the Impairment Rating?

An authorized physician, using the uniform permanent impairment rating schedule required by section 440.15(3)(b).

### Is Permanent Partial Disability the Same as Impairment Income Benefits?

In Florida, impairment income benefits are the benefit paid for a permanent partial impairment. Other states use the term permanent partial disability and different formulas.
