# EEOC Settlement Calculator

Before you can sue under most federal job discrimination laws, you have to file a charge with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission. This calculator does two jobs. It works out your EEOC charge filing deadline. It also builds a settlement scenario from your own figures, with the federal damages caps applied.

```tool
eeoc
```

## How the EEOC Filing Deadline Works

The EEOC page on [time limits for filing a charge](https://www.eeoc.gov/time-limits-filing-charge) sets these rules:

- **180 calendar days:** In general, you must file within 180 calendar days of the discrimination.
- **300 days with an agency:** The deadline extends to 300 calendar days if a state or local agency enforces a law against discrimination on the same basis.
- **Age claims:** For age discrimination, the deadline extends to 300 days only if a state law and a state agency cover it. A local law alone does not extend it.
- **Weekends and holidays:** These days count. If the deadline falls on one, you have until the next business day.
- **Harassment:** The clock runs from the last incident.

Equal Pay Act claims do not need a charge. You can sue within two years of the last discriminatory paycheck, or three years if the violation was willful.

## After You File

The EEOC page on [filing a lawsuit](https://www.eeoc.gov/filing-lawsuit) says that once you receive a Notice of Right to Sue, you must file your lawsuit within 90 days. Age claims follow a different path. You can sue 60 days after filing the charge, without a notice.

## Federal Damages Caps

For intentional discrimination under Title VII or the ADA, [42 U.S.C. 1981a(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1981a) caps compensatory and punitive damages together for each person. The [EEOC remedies page](https://www.eeoc.gov/remedies-employment-discrimination) lists the same limits.

| Employer size | Combined limit |
| --- | --- |
| 15 to 100 employees | $50,000 |
| 101 to 200 employees | $100,000 |
| 201 to 500 employees | $200,000 |
| More than 500 employees | $300,000 |

Back pay is not part of these caps. Age and Equal Pay Act claims cannot recover compensatory or punitive damages, but an intentional violation can add liquidated damages equal to back pay.

## Worked Example

A worker was fired on June 15, 2026, in a state with a fair employment agency. The 300-day deadline lands on Sunday, April 11, 2027, so it moves to Monday, April 12, 2027.

The worker earned $62,000 a year plus $9,000 in benefits, was out of work for 5 months, and earned $6,000 elsewhere since. Back pay is $71,000 ÷ 12 × 5, or $29,583, minus $6,000, for $23,583. The employer has 150 workers, so the $150,000 emotional distress figure the worker entered is capped at $100,000. The scenario total is $123,583.

## Limits of the Calculator

- **Not a prediction.** Every amount except the caps comes from your own figures. The calculator does not estimate what an employer or court would pay.
- **Federal rules only.** State laws may allow more time or higher damages.
- **Federal employees** follow a different process and generally must contact an agency EEO counselor within 45 days.
- **Not legal advice.** Deadlines are strict. Confirm yours with the EEOC or an employment lawyer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Long Do I Have to File an EEOC Charge?

Generally 180 calendar days from the discrimination, or 300 days if a state or local agency enforces a similar law. If the deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.

### What Is the Maximum EEOC Settlement?

For Title VII and ADA claims, compensatory and punitive damages together are capped at $50,000 to $300,000 depending on employer size. Back pay and attorney fees are not part of the cap.

### Does an EEOC Settlement Include Back Pay?

It can. The EEOC says remedies may include back pay and benefits you would have received, plus attorney fees, expert witness fees, and court costs.

### How Long After a Right to Sue Letter Can I File a Lawsuit?

Within 90 days of receiving the Notice of Right to Sue.
