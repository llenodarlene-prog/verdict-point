# LSAT Score Calculator and Percentile Lookup

Enter an LSAT score to see its percentile, or a target percentile to see the score that reaches it. You can also convert a raw score from a practice test, using the conversion table that comes with that test.

```tool
lsat-score
```

## How the LSAT Score Calculator Works

The LSAT is scored on a scale from 120 to 180. LSAC counts the questions you answer correctly to get a raw score, with no deduction for wrong answers, and then converts that raw score to the 120 to 180 scale so scores compare across test dates.

This LSAT score calculator does two things with published data:

- **Percentile for a score.** It looks up the share of test scores that were lower than your scaled score in LSAC's percentile table for the 2023-2024 through 2025-2026 testing years.
- **Score for a percentile.** It finds the lowest scaled score whose percentile reaches your target.

## Why Raw Scores Need Your Own Conversion Table

LSAC does not publish one formula that turns a raw score into a scaled score. Each test form is converted on its own, so the same raw score can lead to different scaled scores on different tests. A calculator that applies one table to every test would give you a number that looks precise but is not.

So this calculator only converts a raw score with a table you supply. Official practice tests on LSAC's LawHub include their own conversion. Copy the rows you need, one per line, as raw score then scaled score, and the calculator looks up your result and its percentile.

## What a Percentile Tells You

A percentile here is the percentage of scores in the three testing years that were lower than a given score. LSAC uses the previous three testing years for the percentiles on score reports and updates them each year by the end of July. A testing year runs from July 1 to June 30.

Your score report also shows a score band. LSAC says the band is a range slightly above and below your score, based on the standard error of measurement, because any single test gives an estimate of your skill.

## Worked Examples

- **A score of 170.** The table shows 94.48% of scores below 170.
- **A target of the 90th percentile.** The lowest score with at least 90% of scores below it is 168, at 91.36%.
- **A raw score of 56 on a practice test.** If that test's table lists 56 as 170, enter "56, 170". The calculator returns 170 and its percentile.

## Limits of the Calculator

- Percentiles describe past test takers. They change each year when LSAC updates the table.
- The calculator does not predict a scaled score from a raw score without your conversion table.
- LSAC says the LSAT will look different starting with the August 2027 administration. Check LSAC for any change to scoring before relying on older practice tables.
- Law schools weigh scores alongside other factors. Our [law school acceptance rates](/legal/law-school-acceptance-rates/) page shows how selective schools are.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Good LSAT Score?

It depends on the schools you are applying to. A score's percentile shows how it compares with recent test takers. Compare it with the scores reported by your target schools.

### How Is the LSAT Scored?

Your raw score is the number of questions you answer correctly in the scored sections, with no penalty for wrong answers. LSAC converts it to the 120 to 180 scale.

### Can I Convert a Raw Score Without a Conversion Table?

Not accurately. Each test form has its own conversion, so use the table that comes with your practice test.

### How Often Do LSAT Percentiles Change?

LSAC updates the percentiles each year by the end of July, using the previous three testing years.

### Does the Calculator Store My Scores?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to Verdict Point.
