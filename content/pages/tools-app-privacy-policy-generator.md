# App Privacy Policy Generator

If your app or website collects personal information from people in California, state law requires a posted privacy policy that covers specific points. This generator drafts a plain-English policy from your answers, with one section for each requirement. Copy it, edit it, and have a lawyer review it before you publish.

```tool

```

## What California Requires

California Business and Professions Code section 22575 applies to an operator of a commercial website or online service that collects personally identifiable information from California residents. The operator must "conspicuously post" a privacy policy that does all of the following:

1. **Collection and sharing:** Identifies the categories of personally identifiable information collected and the categories of third parties it may be shared with.
2. **Review process:** Describes any process users have to review and request changes to their information.
3. **Change notice:** Describes how users are told about material changes to the policy.
4. **Effective date:** States the policy's effective date.
5. **Do Not Track:** Discloses how the operator responds to browser "Do Not Track" signals, if it collects information about users' activities over time and across other sites.
6. **Other trackers:** Discloses whether other parties may collect that kind of information when someone uses the service.

An operator violates the posting requirement only if it fails to post a policy within 30 days of being notified that it is out of compliance.

## What Counts as Personal Information

Section 22577(a) defines personally identifiable information to include:

- a first and last name
- a physical address
- an email address
- a telephone number
- a Social Security number
- any other identifier that allows someone to be contacted
- other information kept together with one of those identifiers

The generator lists the categories you tick.

## How to Post It

Under section 22577(b), "conspicuously post" includes posting the policy on the homepage itself, or linking to it from the homepage with a link or icon that includes the word "privacy." For an app or other online service, the policy can be made available by any other reasonably accessible means.

## Worked Example

A fitness app collects names, email addresses, usage data, and payment details. It shares data with service providers, analytics providers, and authorities when the law requires it. Users can change their data in account settings. The app does not respond to Do Not Track, and its analytics tools track across sites. The draft covers each point and says changes will be announced by email.

## Limits of the Generator

- **California's posting law only.** Other laws may require much more, including the California Consumer Privacy Act, the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, and Europe's GDPR.
- **Your answers must be accurate.** A policy that does not match what your service really does can create legal risk of its own.
- **Not legal advice.** Have a lawyer review the draft before you publish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does My App Need a Privacy Policy?

If it is commercial and collects personally identifiable information from California residents, section 22575 requires one. App stores and other laws often require one too.

### What Is a Do Not Track Disclosure?

Section 22575(b)(5) requires you to say how you respond to browser "Do Not Track" signals if you collect information about users' activities over time and across other sites. A clear link to a page describing your approach can also satisfy it, under (b)(7).

### Where Should I Post the Policy?

On a website, put it on the homepage or link to it from the homepage with a link that includes the word "privacy." In an app, make it reasonably accessible, for example from the settings menu.
